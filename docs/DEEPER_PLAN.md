# Deeper: design and implementation plan

The goal of the game becomes getting deeper. The world is a grid of generated levels addressed by **(seed, depth)**. Every seed is a column you can dig down. Moving sideways steps to the neighbouring seed, as if you had started there. Because every level is a pure function of its address, players exploring the same seed see the same places, so sharing coordinates is how people explore together.

No level is guaranteed to be fully reachable. Places you can't reach yet are the point: you come back with new abilities.

## Decisions

| Topic | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Lateral movement | Infinite. Left and right exits go to `seed − 1` / `seed + 1` at the same depth. Each is locked with a key colour dealt by the level seed. The key is kept, the door stays open, and the side door you arrive through opens behind you. |
| Exit distance | Grows with depth, physically. Deeper levels place their exits further from the arrival point (and may be larger). |
| Going back | Always free. |
| Deeper | Costs stars, paid once per deeper door; the door then stays open. |
| Reachability | Not guaranteed. Exploration of the generated space is the game. |
| Respawn | At the last lantern you lit. It absorbs one death, then burns out. The run's starting lantern is lit. Lanterns are scarce: one at each level's way back and about one more. |
| Death | A lit lantern brings you back once at one heart; you drop all your stars into a ghost. The used lantern becomes spent for the rest of the run. |
| Restoring protection | Find and light another unspent lantern. Recovering the ghost restores your stars and full health; Mend can heal before recovery. |
| End of a run | Dying without a lit lantern ends the run. |
| Keys | Not used up. You carry one (more with the keyring); grabbing another leaves your oldest key where the new one was. Keys work in any level. A rare skeleton key opens any one door, then crumbles. |
| Abilities | Bought at shrines (no shop), and every ability has upgrade tiers. Cheaper deeper. A shrine offers its ability tier or, instead, healing to full (dearer deeper). |
| Ways to play | Enter a seed and dig its column, or start at a random seed. |

## 1. The world: a grid of (seed, depth)

- `level_seed = hash(seed, depth)` drives everything: WFC terrain, object placement, key and door colours, the shrine offer, and the depth band (WFC sample region and riso realm).
- Four exits per level:
  - **Deeper** leads to `(seed, depth + 1)`. It is placed low in the level, costs `P(d)` stars once, then stays open.
  - **Back** leads to `(seed, depth − 1)`. It is placed high in the level and is free.
  - **Left and right** lead to `(seed ± 1, depth)`. They are placed at the side edges.
  - Each side door is locked with a key colour, `lateral_lock(level, side)`, dealt by the level seed. Carrying that colour opens it and you go through; the key is kept. It then stays open in the level record. The side door you arrive through is marked open, so the way back never needs a key.
  - **A way out of the first level:** at depth 0 one side door (left or right, whichever side of the start it falls on) is placed on the nearest floor at least 5 cells from the start that the wizard can hop to from it, instead of at its side edge. A key in that door's lock colour is laid on a floor hopped to from the start, as near the way to the door as can be (the start key, on top of the level's usual keys). The way from the start to both is kept clear of doors and switch gates (`World.keep_clear`). "Hopped to" is the rough reach shared with hyperspace (`scripts/worlds/Reach.gd`), with hops 3 cells across rather than 4, so it is easy. `tests/start_escape_test.gd` checks 40 first levels.
- You arrive at the matching exit of the next level: going deeper puts you at its Back exit, and so on.
- Exits sit at least `D(d)` cells from the arrival point, with `D` growing with depth. Deeper levels can also grow beyond 64×64.
- **Lanterns are scarce.** Every level has an unlit lantern beside its way back (where a dive arrives; at depth 0 the lit start lantern stands there instead), and `LANTERNS_PER_K` (0.4) more per 1000 cells, at least one: two in most levels, where there were six to eight (one beside every exit and 1.5 per 1000 cells). The other exits have none.
- **Connected caves:** after the terrain is generated, open pockets under 6 cells are filled with rock. Every other open region is joined to the largest by carving the shortest tunnel through rock, nearest first, until the level is one cave. So every exit, the shrine, the ink well, keys, lanterns and moons are connected through open space. They are not necessarily reachable: gates (doors, cracked walls, locked or unpaid exits) and abilities (height, gaps) still apply. `tests/connect_test.gd` checks 20 levels across worlds and depths.
- **Size grows with depth:** `level_size(d)` = (36 + 3d) × (30 + 2.5d) cells, capped at 60 × 48 (from depth 8). That is 36 × 30 at depth 0 and 54 × 45 at depth 6. (It was 6d × 5d to 80 × 72, which grew too fast and ran slowly.) Each level is enclosed in solid rock three cells thick, flush against its edges, and the camera stops one cell into that rock.
- **Level record:** each visited level stores only what changed. That is stars taken, doors opened, keys dropped or taken, the deeper door paid, lanterns lit, and the ghost. On a revisit the level regenerates identically and the record is applied on top.
- **Determinism:** a failed WFC collapse currently retries with `randi()`. It must retry with `hash(level_seed, attempt)` instead. Neighbouring levels pre-generate on the background thread so transitions are instant.

## 2. Death, the ghost and the end of a run

- **Die with a lit lantern:** all carried stars go into a ghost at the death spot, recorded in that level. Any previous ghost and its stars are lost. The lit lantern absorbs the death, burns out, and brings you back to its location at one heart, even if it is in another world. Recovering the corpse restores full health and the dropped stars; Mend can heal a heart before recovery. Travelling, saving and lighting another lantern preserve current health.
- **Grace on arriving:** coming out of a portal or rift, arriving in a level through any door (or a tier III rift), and coming back at a lantern after dying each give a second of invulnerability (`Player.GRACE_TIME`, its own timer `graced`, so it never shortens or blocks a hit's 2 s), with the wizard flickering as after a hit. `tests/death_test.gd` and `tests/rift_test.gd` check it.
- **Spent lanterns:** each lantern can absorb only one death per run. Its cell is recorded as spent in its world's record; travelling away, revisiting, or continuing a saved run cannot relight it. Switching to another lantern before dying does not consume the former one.
- **Restore protection:** interact with another unspent lantern. It becomes your protected respawn point. Ghost recovery and star collection never restore protection; you can light another lantern without recovering the ghost first.
- **Giving up:** stuck (down a pit with no way out), choose Give up on the pause menu. It is only offered while a lantern protects you (`MapInfo.can_give_up`): it is a death like any other, so the stars drop into a ghost, the lantern burns out and you come back at it with one heart (`MapInfo.give_up`). Without a lit lantern it would end the run, so it is not offered.
- **Burning a lantern:** holding the mend spell short of draughts, interact with the lantern you lit to burn it into the spell (`MapInfo.burn_lantern`): the draughts fill, but the lantern is spent and you are unprotected until you light another. A drop of pink light rises over a lantern that can be burned. With lanterns scarce, that is the choice: a life kept in the lantern, or hearts now.
- **Die without a lit lantern:** the run ends. The seed's levels are unchanged, since they are generated. Your character and level records reset, and the new run starts with a fresh lit lantern. The ended run's save is deleted immediately.
- **Arming:** a ghost can only be recovered once the player has stepped off it, so dying on the respawn lantern doesn't hand the stars straight back.
- **Presentation:** the HUD's flame (beside the health beads) burns yellow while protected and becomes a thin, curling pink smoke thread otherwise, including after recovering a ghost. Spent lanterns have empty, dark glass, a charred wick, the same fading smoke thread and no light pool. Unspent lanterns keep their low ember. Insects no longer circle lanterns. The ghost uses the printed astral-silhouette style, and the HUD points toward it when it is in another level.
- **Saves:** existing version-1 saves remain readable. An old unprotected save marks its last respawn lantern spent when loaded. `tests/death_test.gd` covers consumption, relighting rejection, cross-world deaths, star and ghost recovery, revisits, save migration, and run reset.

## 3. Keys

- A held key opens every door of its colour in any level. Opened doors stay open.
- You carry one key at a time, or more with the **keyring** perk (`KeyRing.gd`): each tier carries one more (I two, II three, III four, one of each colour). Grabbing a new one with no room leaves your oldest key where the new one was, and the level record keeps it there. A key of a colour you already carry is left lying. Every key carried trails the wizard in a little chain, the newest at the shoulder and skeleton keys last (`RisoWizard._trail_keys`); the HUD shows no keys. The newest is the player's `carried_key` meta and the older ones `spare_keys`; saves keep them all (`keys`), and older saves load with their one key.
- **Skeleton keys** (the rare key): in `SKELETON_CHANCE` (30 %) of levels from depth 1, dealt by the level seed (`MapInfo.skeleton_at`), one takes the place of a star in the level's last secret room (on an open floor if the level has no secret room). They print bone white (bare paper, `RisoPrint.ink_key`): a key whose bow is a bone's two-knobbed end, with the shaft and bit of the other keys (three teeth, as a moss key's) (`RisoProp.key_shape`), with a pale halo and a paper twinkle; on paper (the prompt disc, a shrine's medallion) they print in night, and the map marks them, and bone gates, in grey (night screened to half). They go in a pocket apart from the ring (`skeleton_keys`, shown on the HUD after the ring). Interacting with a locked door (a corridor door or a side door) that no carried key opens uses one up and opens the door for good. Walking into a door never spends one. The map marks them in eye yellow.
- **Bone gates:** doors of pale bone-grey ironwork with a bone padlock (bare white, a bone on its face) that only a skeleton key opens: interact carrying one, and it is used up; walking into one never spends it, and no coloured key opens it. A level's doors take a bone gate's colour, `KeyRing.SKELETON`, so the map, prompts and records treat them as doors. Three kinds (`MapInfo.BONE_VAULT_CHANCE`, `RELIC_GATE_CHANCE`, `SKELETON_DOOR_DEPTH`, `SKELETON_DOOR_CHANCE`):
  - **Bone vaults:** in 25 % of levels from depth 1, dealt by the level seed (`MapInfo.bone_vault_at`), a vault behind a bone gate holding a hoard worth three star clusters (`World.BONE_LOOT`), a step above a plum vault. Placed after the keyed vaults (`World.place_bone_vault`): carved two high in the level's own rock (3 or 2 across), or where there is none (a cemetery's terraces, the sky's islands) built as a 2 × 2 strongbox. Strongboxes may now take the place of stars and plain ledges, not only empty air.
  - **Relics behind bone:** in half of relic levels (`MapInfo.relic_gated_at`) the relic waits in the bone vault instead of a secret room; with no room for one, it goes back to the first secret room (or, with none, a floor in the open).
  - **On the way:** from depth 6, 15 % of corridor doors are bone gates (dealt with the colours, `World.deal_colors`).
  - **Bought at a shrine:** at full health, the mending station sells a skeleton key instead of a relic's whereabouts at 30 % of shrines (`Shrine.SKELETON_SALE`), 70 % while a relic it already marked waits to be found (`SKELETON_SALE_HINTED`), and always when there is no relic left to point to. It costs twice the level's deeper price. Bought, the key is laid on a floor somewhere in the level, at least 10 cells from the shrine where there is one (`MapInfo.lay_sold_skeleton`), kept in the record as a dropped key, so it shows on the map until taken. The station shows a bone on a paper medallion.
  - `tests/bones_test.gd` covers them; `tests/capture_bones.gd` takes stills.
- Door colours are dealt per level. A matching key may be far away, or in another column.
- **Rarity:** the four colours are not equally common. By `MapInfo.KEY_RARITY` (18 : 7 : 3 : 1) sun is the commonest, then ember, moss and plum (about 62 %, 24 %, 10 % and one in 30). Keys, corridor doors, side-door locks (`lateral_lock`) and bell and vane padlocks are all dealt by these weights (`MapInfo.rarity_color`), so a common key opens a lot and a rare key seldom does, and which keys to carry is a choice. Key and door colours are dealt once the level is laid out (`World.deal_colors`), from the level seed and each thing's place in the order things were laid, never the world RNG; the colour is the cell's `extra_info`. A level's first key is always sun, so every level holds a key to its commonest doors. Keys themselves are scarce: a level keeps 1 per 1000 cells, at least 2 (`KEYS_PER_K`, `KEYS_MIN`, `World.key_count`; 2 at the surface, 3 in the largest levels), besides the start key and skeleton keys. Spots are still drawn for 2 per 1000 cells, at least 4 (`KEY_SPOTS_PER_K`), so levels are laid out as they were; only the first `key_count` keep their key. The rarer a key, the longer its shaft and the more teeth on its bit (sun one, ember two, moss three, plum four; `RisoProp.key_shape`), on the key, the wizard's chain and door prompts alike.
- **Vaults** (`World.place_vaults`, placed last of all): small rooms sealed in rock behind a locked portcullis, entered at floor height from a floor beside them, their loot in plain sight through the bars. 0.5 per 1000 cells (`VAULTS_PER_K`, at least one), as many as fit: found like a secret room (4, 3 or 2 cells across, 2 high, or 3 or 2 across and 1 high), but wholly walled in the level's own rock, which `make_room` leaves whole. A level with no rock thick enough (a sky level's thin islands) builds one instead, a strongbox: a 2 × 1 room walled, roofed and floored in rock out of free air beside a floor (hanging off its edge where there is no floor under it), with two rows of open air over it and one under any floor it builds, never near a chasm or gap (`World._strongbox_spots`). A vault's lock is dealt evenly among the four colours, unlike every other lock, and the rarer the colour the better the loot (`World.VAULT_LOOT`, `World.vault_loot`): sun a half-sized star cluster, ember a star cluster, and 40 % of the time a moss key too (`VAULT_KEY_CHANCE`, dealt by the level seed and the vault's door), moss a cluster and a half, plum a star cluster and a skeleton key. A cluster's worth is its share of `cluster_value(depth)` (at least 2). So rare keys open little, but what they open is worth it. Opening one is an ordinary door opening (the record keeps it). `tests/vaults_test.gd` covers rarity and vaults across 20 levels (18 have one); `tests/capture_vaults.gd` takes stills.
- A dropped key arms only once the player has stepped off it, so the swap does not immediately grab it back.

## 4. Shrines and tiered abilities

- One shrine per level, standing on two floor cells (its three stations packed side by side) 3–14 cells from the deeper exit.
- It offers three things: two different abilities and mending. Taking any one spends the shrine, and the level record keeps it spent: At least one of the two is always a strict upgrade, a gain with nothing given up: a new perk or the next tier of something already known, never a spell that would replace the one in the slot (if both picks would be swaps, the second is traded for the first strict upgrade along).
  - **The boon:** the next tier of an ability. The ability is picked by `level_seed`, or is the next one along that still has a tier to learn.
  - **Mending:** healing to full. It is the alternative to the boon and does nothing at full health.
- **Pay to learn.** There is no menu: interact with the side you want. The shrine prints no text from afar: stepping up to a niche (or the mending bowl) pops up a larger plaque over it with the ability's name, tier and price (or mending's price).
- **Prices:**
  - Learning costs `S(d, tier) = max(3, round(10 × 1.6^(tier−1) × 0.8^d))`: cheaper deeper, dearer per tier.
  - Mending works the other way: `H(d) = round(3 × 1.35^d)`, which is 3 at depth 0 and 13 at depth 5.
- **Only the dash is known at the start.** Everything else is found at shrines. A shrine offers an ability not yet known before any upgrade: it starts from the seed's pick and goes round. Until learned, parry and astral projection do nothing, and there is no hex, so no killing enemies or breaking cracked walls.
- **One spell slot.** Hex, astral projection, parry, levitate, awareness and rift are spells, and all use the Spell button (Q, or the pad's X; the old Parry binding renamed). You carry one at a time: learning a different spell at a shrine replaces it, and the pop-up over that niche is topped by a "swap" tag. Perks (dash, double jump, wall climb, blink, vigor) stack. The spell given up is not lost: it stays in that shrine's niche at its tier (the level record keeps it), and interacting there takes it back free, leaving the spell you hold in its place, even after the shrine is spent. `tests/capture_shrine_left.gd` shows it.
- **Levitate:** press Spell in the air to stop falling and float. You drift slowly up and down with the stick and sideways at walking pace, until it runs out or you press Spell again. You get one float per landing, and a moon brings it back.
- **Awareness:** press Spell to sense the level. While it lasts, pointers at the edge of the view show where things are, like the ghost's arrow. A short cooldown follows.
- **Astral projection** uses the Spell button. Tap it to leave your body and go out as an untouchable projection. Tap again, or get hurt, to snap back to the body. Let it run out and you stay where the projection is. There are no astral orbs any more.
- **Rift:** learn it at a shrine, then press Spell to place an end at your position. A second cast places its partner; interact to travel between them. Casting again deletes both old ends in the current world and starts a new pair. Each world (seed, depth) keeps one spell-created pair in its saved level record: pairs in other worlds remain untouched and return when you revisit. Swapping spells preserves placed pairs; starting a new run clears them. Travelling always needs the interact key. Tier I requires standing on a surface; tier II allows placement in midair. At tier III, casting builds the run's one cross-world link instead (`MapInfo.rift_link`, saved with the run): place one end, travel to another level, place the other, and interacting with either end travels to the other's level and comes out of it. A third cast closes both old ends wherever they are; per-world pairs made at lower tiers stay.
- **Switch gates:** every level has at least one (0.6 per 1000 cells): a gate across a corridor, like a door but with no lock, and its switch on a floor elsewhere, at least 8 cells away and reachable from the way in with that gate shut. Interact with the switch, or hit it with a hex bolt, and the gate lifts for good (the level record keeps the switch thrown and the gate open). The map marks gates and switches.
- **Kinds of world** (`scripts/worlds/`): every place a run can be in has a definition, `NextWorldDef` (from `MapInfo.def_for(at)`; `MapInfo.here` for the place being played), and the rest of the game asks it rather than knowing which kind of place it is in. The definition says how the terrain is collapsed (`region`, `size`, `fallback`) and dressed (`populate`), where each exit leads (`lead`: the place, the way the transition sweeps, the exit arrived at; `neighbours` for prefetch), what each exit costs (`price`, `pay`), and how the place prints (`realm`, `grows`, `title`, `exit_dir`, `exit_grand`, `grid_at` on the worlds map). The base class is the ordinary cave level. Every other kind is a **side world** (`SideWorld`), reached through a door that some levels deal (`deals`, `entry_price`). You arrive at its way back (`Exit.BACK`, which returns you out of that door); its way on (`Exit.DEEPER`) leads to its `destination`, and from then on that level's way back leads in again (`arriving`). A dead end, whose destination is the level it was entered from, just returns there. Side worlds are listed in `Worlds.KINDS`. Kind k entered from level (seed, depth) sits at `(seed, -(k * 100000 + depth) - 1)` (`Worlds.side_at`, `origin_of`, `kind_at`), so each has its own record, save entry, prefetch and tile on the worlds map. A door into kind k is exit number `Worlds.door(k)` (16 + k), kept in the level's `exits`. Its price is paid once (`doors_paid` in the record). The exits taken are kept as `ways_taken`. **To add a world:** extend `SideWorld`, set its name, crossing direction (`way`), realm, size, sample and price in `_init`, override `deals` and `populate` (and `destination_for` with `arriving`, `depth_for` and `fallback` as needed), then list it in `Worlds.KINDS`. Its door, exits, prices, travel, prefetch, save entry, map ribbon and legend rows come for free.
- **The hyperspace door:** a rare second way down, dealt by the level seed in 18 % of levels from depth 1 (`Hyperspace.CHANCE`), on the floor furthest from the way in. It costs 5 times the deeper exit's price (`Hyperspace.PRICE`), paid once, and leads into **hyperspace** rather than straight down. It prints as a double arch (accent round pink) under a falling cascade of chevrons. `tests/switches_test.gd` covers switches, the hyperspace door and the parry; `tests/capture_switches.gd` takes stills.
- **Hyperspace** (`scripts/worlds/Hyperspace.gd`, a side world): a short, linear, hazard-packed crossing, 64 × 16 cells, between the level the door is paid in and the one `Hyperspace.DROP` (4) below it. It has no ink well, shrine, keys or side doors. You come in at its way back on the left; its way back returns to the level, out of the hyperspace door, free. Its gate on the right (printed like the hyperspace door, and free: it was paid on the way in) drops you at the way back of the level 4 below, and by a draw of the entry level's seed (`Hyperspace.drift`) a world to the left, straight down, or a world to the right. The levels in between are skipped, not visited. On screen it is "world N · hyperspace".
  - **Landing level:** a level hyperspace drops into (`Hyperspace.arriving`; where two would, the one from straight above wins, then the one from the left) has its way back lead into that hyperspace, arriving at its gate, and also an ordinary way up (`Exit.RETURN`, placed a few cells from the way back) to the level above it, arriving at that level's deeper exit. Using it joins the two on the worlds map.
  - **Address:** `(seed, -depth - 1)` (kind 0), the depth being the level the jump was paid in. Its `depth` is the middle of the drop, for enemy health.
  - **Terrain:** collapsed by the same WFC as any level, but from its own, more dangerous 32 × 32 sample, `wfc_images/chasm_gauntlet.png` (thorn-capped floors, thorn-bottomed pits, toothed ceilings, thorned islands; about 10 % thorns; `tests/make_hyperspace_sample.gd` draws it), as a 64 × 16 strip. The collapse alone is often not passable; the dressing makes it so (below).
  - **Dressing** (`Hyperspace.populate`): both ends (8 cells) are laid flat and safe, with the way back and a lantern at the left, the gate and a lantern at the right, and a rest lantern halfway along. Then the gaps are filled (`Hyperspace._lifts`): a gliding ledge every 4 cells across every wide open stretch, a stack of rising and falling ledges every 3 cells up every tall wall, with moons beside them (at least 4 cells apart). Last, a crossing is made sure of (`Hyperspace._bridge`): the cheapest way through the open air from the way back to the gate is found (keeping to floors, crossing air only where it must, and through thorns only where nothing else gets through: those thorns are cleared), and a rough reach (2 cells up and 4 across a hop, a cell further across per two fallen; a moon caught on the way gives another hop) is followed along it from the way back. Wherever the reach stops, the lift (or, failing that, a floating ledge or a moon) that carries a rider furthest on along the way is laid, until the gate is reached (at most 96). `Hyperspace.crossable` checks the result. Seven watching eyes and five wisps are spread along the floor (one every few cells), with four more moons and 14 stars. Five lasers (`Laser.gd`, `Hyperspace._lasers`) are set in its rock, spread along the way and at least 5 cells apart, each in a ceiling, wall or floor with at least 3 cells of open air in front: on a cadence (0.9 s warming up behind a faint flickering sight line, 0.7 s firing, 1.6 s resting, each out of step with the others) it fires a beam straight out until the first rock (or ledge or lift), which hurts only while it fires. Lasers are only in hyperspace for now.
  - **Theme:** hyperspace prints in a realm of its own (its `print_realm`, `RisoPrint.REALMS.hyperspace`: violet rock, aqua stars, a cold paper), switched to on the way in and back to the realm you came from on the way out. Its sky has no skyline and no still stars: a glow lies ahead and streaks of light rush back past the view, the near ones bare paper (`RisoBackground._warp`). Nothing grows there (no decor or fireflies).
  - **Tests:** `tests/hyperspace_test.gd` covers the addresses, generation (determinism, ends, lanterns, thorns, every one crossable), travel in and out, the landing level's two ways up, and the worlds map. `tests/capture_hyperspace.gd` takes stills.
  - **Worlds map:** hyperspace, like every side world, is a narrow accent ribbon on a smooth curve out of the middle of the bottom of the level it is entered from and into the middle of the top of the one it drops to. It leaves and arrives straight down and widens into a mouth where it meets each level (`MOUTH`), so it reads as poured from one into the other; in between it bows through a point in the gutter between columns, so it runs past the levels it skips rather than over them (away from the landing's column when it drifts a world sideways). The cursor's frame round it is open at the ends. It stands in for the link bar between them, is cut off at the page's edge rather than left out when it runs past it, and the cursor follows its curve; legend rows are named after the kind ("hyperspace", "hyperspace door"). `tests/capture_hyperspace.gd` takes stills.
- **Room for big things:** each kind of thing that prints taller or wider than its cell declares its box in cells (`World.SIZES`: exits, portals, ink wells, lanterns and relics 1 × 2, the shrine 2 × 2, counting up from its own cell). Once any place is laid out (after its definition's `populate`, so hyperspace and future worlds alike), the rock and thorns in every such box are carved out (`World.make_room`), so nothing prints into the rock over it. Left alone: a secret room's rock and the rock sealing it, a vault's walls, rock framing a door or switch gate above or below it, and rock a laser is set in. A new big thing only needs its size in `SIZES`. `tests/room_test.gd` checks 44 places.
- **Secret rooms and relics:** every level has secret rooms (0.6 per 1000 cells, at least one; `World.place_secrets`, placed last): a pocket of rock, 4, 3 or 2 cells across and 2 high, with rock under it (wholly in rock where there is room), entered at floor height from a floor beside it through a false wall. The room and its false wall are cracked cells holding the room's number, drawn and mapped as plain rock, even on an inked map. The false wall can be walked and shot straight through; stepping into it, a hex bolt striking the rock behind it, an astral projection drifting into it or a warp landing in it opens the whole room for good (`MapInfo.open_secret`; the record keeps it open), and its rewards appear: up to 3 stars, and the level's relic if it has one. **Relics** (`Relics.gd`, `Relic.gd`): tier I of the five relic moves (double jump, wall climb, blink, levitate, astral projection) only comes from a relic; shrines offer their higher tiers once the move is known. From depth 3 (`Relics.MIN_DEPTH`), 5 % of levels hold one, rising a point every two levels to 8 % (`Relics.chance`, `CHANCE`, `CHANCE_MAX`; so finding one means going out of your way; every level in a debug run), a move dealt by the seed, in a secret room (on an open floor if no room fits). Interact and pay to take it, a lot: 4 times the deeper exit's price in its level (`Relics.price`; 88 stars at depth 3). It teaches the move's next tier; a relic bringing a spell leaves the spell it replaces there to take back free, as at a shrine. It prints as a medallion (an accent ring round a paper disc with the move's mark) over a small plinth. A relic for a move already known teaches its next tier, and its pop-up says "upgrade" ("already mastered" at the top tier). At full health, when there is nothing to mend, a **shrine's** third station sells either a skeleton key (§3, "Bone gates") or the whereabouts of the nearest relic neither found nor already marked (searching 12 levels across and down, `Relics.SEARCH`; 1.5 times the deeper price, `Relics.hint_price`; buying it spends the shrine, like mending). It shows that relic's move on a small medallion, and the relic is marked on the worlds map: on an unvisited level, a faint tile you can pick and open, whose page shows where the relic waits; one off the page is pinned to its edge with a chevron pointing the way. `tests/secrets_test.gd` and `tests/phasing_test.gd` cover them; `tests/capture_secrets.gd` takes stills.
- **Crossings left to relics:** deeper down, levels are likely to need a relic move (double jump, blink, levitate, wall climb) to cross fully. From depth 6 (`MapInfo.RELIC_NEED_FROM`), `relic_need(depth)` % of a level's crossings are left to them: 10 % at depth 6, 10 more each level, up to 60 % (`RELIC_NEED_STEP`, `RELIC_NEED_MAX`).
  - **Chasms and gaps:** that share of a cemetery's chasms and the sky's gaps, dealt by the level seed per chasm, lose their bells or vanes, the switches that free them, and their bridge's planks or their crosswind (`World.relax_crossings`, kept in `relic_chasms`). The level is laid out as always, then these are taken away, so nothing else moves. Too wide to jump with the dash, they are crossed with a relic move. The way back is always on the arrival side, so nothing is shut in.
  - **Hyperspace** that drops deep (`relic_need` of the depth it lands at, so from a door at depth 2): one stretch 6 cells wide (`Hyperspace.RELIC_GAP`) loses the lifts, ledges and moons laid across it, but only where the rough reach then cannot cross and a relic's longer reach (7 cells a hop, `RELIC_ACROSS`) can (`Hyperspace._relic_gap`, kept in `w.relic_gaps`). Where no stretch qualifies, it stays bridged.
  - `tests/relics_needed_test.gd` covers them.
- **Standing room:** the things you stand at to use them (exits, the shrine, lanterns, the ink well, relics, bells, switches, teleporters; `MapInfo.STANDERS`) always keep something under them. Where the rock under one is broken (a cracked wall struck, a secret room opened), a ledge (a one-way platform) appears in its place at once, and again whenever the level loads (`MapInfo.prop_up`). `tests/floor_test.gd` covers it.
- **Moons** are dash resets, with a target of 3 per 1000 level cells, placed only in open air: clear on every side and below, and at least 6 cells apart. They reject platforms in the fall below, including the full width and travel of moving platforms, until intervening rock or spikes block the fall. Suitable spots above spikes have triple the selection weight. Touching one spends it at once (it shows as a sliver), whether or not you have used your dash. While you stay inside it gives your dash back, even mid-dash, as often as you use it. Once you leave it waxes back over 2.5 s. Moons are never used up.
- Tiers live on the player (`Abilities.gd`) and reset when a run ends. The upgrade menu and the old `Upgrade`, `UpgradeManager`, double-jump and wall-climb nodes are gone.

The spell orb goes dim while you can't afford it, and a shrine's pop-up for them shows the price of a cast under the price to learn. The other spells are free: their cost is a cooldown, charges or mend's draughts.

Spawn budgets follow level area: keys 1 (at least 2, §3), corridor gates 3, extra lanterns 0.4 (at least one), moons 3, and cracked walls 2.5 per 1000 cells (and teleporter pairs 0.75, the two ends of a pair at least a quarter of the level's width and height together apart, at least 12 cells, `World.portal_apart`; a pair with no floor that far is not placed), and from depth 1 hoppers 2 per 1000 cells (placed last, on floors, so the rest of a level is laid out as before). A hopper (`Hopper.gd`) squats until it sees the wizard within 700 × 400 px with a clear line, crouches for 0.45 s, then leaps (about two cells high) at where the wizard is, resting 1.1 s after each landing; the hex wounds and stuns it like the other nightmares, and the parry catches it. `tests/hazards_test.gd` covers hoppers and lasers. Key colours are dealt by rarity (§3); exits, their lanterns, the shrine and ink well are reserved first. Natural teleporters use 0.75 pairs per 1000 cells, rounded with a minimum of one pair and no fixed cap: one pair at depth 0, two at depth 5 and in the largest levels. Coins, platforms and enemies use a fraction of available space. Terrain and spacing constraints can leave budgets unfilled.

Watching eyes detect within 1400 pixels (previously 1150, and 520 before that); only rock and the wizard block their line of sight, not stars or other areas. `tests/spawn_balance_test.gd` checks 20 levels across five seeds and both terrain families, plus platform rejection and spike weighting. `tests/rift_test.gd` checks placed-pair lifecycle, spell tiers and actual watcher sight physics.

## 4b. Archetypes: the garden, the cemetery and the sky

Every ordinary level has an archetype (`NextWorldDef.archetype`), dealt by depth: bands of `BAND` (6) levels take `ARCHETYPES` in turn, the garden (the caves a run starts in) at depths 0–5, the cemetery at 6–11, the sky at 12–17 (§4c), the garden again at 18–23, and so on (`NextWorldDef.first_depth` gives a band's first depth), until later archetypes join the turn. Every seed's column passes through each. Side worlds have none. A garden level is printed in the garden's own realm (`RisoPrint.REALMS.garden`: green rock and sun-gold stars under a deep green night, on a warm paper), switched to on arrival as the cemetery's is (so the F8 realms only preview until the next level); it is collapsed from the tunnels sample (`MapInfo.region_for`; the floating islands it used to take every other band are the sky's now).

**Hedges** (the garden, `RisoDecor.shrub_runs`): about half the stretches of floor at least 2 cells long have a hedge along them (dealt apart from the fences, so one often stands behind a fence), in lengths of 2–5 cells with a gap between: a plain band of moss leafage with a row of rounded tops along it, shaded at its foot, as long as its run, printed behind everything else in the decor (`HEDGE_SLOTS`, on a canvas of their own). They replace the garden's single bushes and shrubs.

**Background fences** (both archetypes, `RisoDecor.fence_runs`): about half the stretches of floor at least 3 cells long have a fence along them, in lengths of up to 6 cells with a gap between, printed in front of the garden's hedges and behind the rest of the decor in a lighter ink (`BACK_SLOTS`, on a canvas of their own): white wooden pickets over two rails in the garden (bare paper; now and then one missing or leaning), iron railings between stone posts in a cemetery.

**The cemetery** is a hillside graveyard:

- **Size and edges:** a sky level is 1.8 times as wide and 1.6 times as tall as a cave level of its depth (`NextWorldDef.SKY_SCALE`; 108 × 77 cells at depth 12), with no border rock anywhere (`MapInfo.enclose_map`, `open`): open sky to the sides and above, the camera going 6 cells past the edges (`SKY_MARGIN`) so the sky beyond shows, and a drop below.
- **Terrain:** collapsed from its own sample, `wfc_images/graveyard.png` (drawn by `tests/make_graveyard_sample.gd`): stacked terraces of rock that run across the level, stepping up and down a cell or two at a time, dotted with grave mounds and two- or three-wide mausoleums, with open graves (a pit with thorns at the bottom) and one drop per terrace to the one below. It is collapsed with no symmetry (`NextWorldDef.symmetry` 1; the levels' samples use 5, which turns patterns, and the extension's first flip is upside down), so up stays up. Crypts (tight tunnels and burial chambers) are left for a world of their own, maybe a side world.
- **Look:** its own print realm (`RisoPrint.REALMS.cemetery`: slate rock, violet stars and light, bone paper), switched to on arrival like hyperspace's; a faint moon in the sky with clouds drifting past (flat underneath, billowing on top, `RisoBackground._cloud`); its own decor (`RisoDecor.plan_cemetery`), in pale stone (bare paper, shaded down one side): headstones in five shapes (rounded, gothic, cross-topped, broken, a flat ledger with a low stone at its head), crosses, obelisks, urns on plinths, the odd stone angel, grave flowers, bare trees and dry grass on floors; cobwebs and roots under ceilings; ivy on walls; and iron railings behind (background fences, above). Its title reads "world 28 · depth 3 · cemetery".
- **Moths** (`MothSwarm.gd`, `moths.tscn`): a swarm a few cells from each lantern and `MOTHS_PER_K` (0.6) more in open air. They chase the wizard's spell orb within 560 px while a spell is equipped and the wizard is not drowsy, taking priority over a lit lantern. With no spell, a dimmed orb, or the wizard farther away, a lit lantern within 1100 px of their home draws them; otherwise they flutter back home. A moth touching the wizard stings like any hit. A dash or blink through the swarm, a hex bolt, or a parry scatters it for 6 s: the moths burst outward and sting no one, then gather again: while scattered they print faint, with a pink point pulsing where they will come back together. They are never killed. So the lantern that keeps you safe draws danger to it. `tests/moths_test.gd` covers the spell requirement, sleep fog, spell-orb priority, dash scattering, contact guards and regrouping.
- **Sleep fog** (`SleepFog.gd`, `sleep_fog.tscn`): `FOG_PER_K` (2.0) banks lying over floors at least 5 cells apart, each 600 × 240 px, drifting 220 px to and fro. Inside, the wizard is drowsy (`Player.is_drowsy`): the Spell button does nothing, a levitate float or an astral projection ends (back to the body), awareness stops sensing; the robe greys (`RisoPrint.DROWSY_ROBE`) and the spell orb dims. It wears off a moment after leaving. It prints as mist: five low bands each lifting the night toward the paper in soft steps, sliding to and fro at their own pace, with a faint pink wash low down and a few pink motes rising through it, so it reads as danger.
- **Wraiths** (`Wraith.gd`, `wraith_enemy.tscn`): `WRAITHS_PER_K` (0.9), in open air at least 10 cells from the way in. A pale shroud with a dark hood, its eyes dim hollows while it waits and lit bright pink, glowing, while it chases; it hangs still until it senses the wizard within 640 px (through rock: no line of sight), then drifting at them at 95 px/s, straight through rock (60 % as fast inside it, and printed fainter). Its touch hurts; it falls back for a moment after. Beyond 1400 px it drifts home. Nothing collides with it: only the hex wounds it (and a bolt still stops at rock), the hex or a parry stuns it. It has a `Wound` like the other enemies, so it drops stars and stays slain until you die.
- **Gates: chasms and their bells.** Doors and switch gates need corridors one cell high, which the open terraces hardly have, so a cemetery gates its way with chasms instead. `CHASMS_PER_K` (1.2, at least `CHASMS_MIN`, 2, as many as fit; so several bells a level) are cut across long stretches of floor right after the caves are joined (`World.carve_chasms`, before anything else is placed): 8–10 cells across, two deep, thorns at the bottom on rock, with 4 cells of floor kept whole either side where they fit, else 3 or 2; too wide to jump even with the dash (a run, jump and dash covers under 6). The graveyard's floors step up and down, so a stretch whose floor is everywhere within a cell of one row will do: cutting it levels the floor to that row first (`World._floor_near`, `_level_floor`). The pit and 4 rows of air over it are kept clear of everything (ledges, lifts, moons, pickups), so nothing but a bridge or a move found later (double jump, blink, levitate) gets you over. Across the top of each lie the planks of a bridge (`Bridge.gd`, `bridge.tscn`, a one-way ledge like a platform), printed as a faint dashed line and not there to stand on until the chasm's bell is rung. Each chasm has two bells (`Bell.gd`, `bell.tscn`), one on its post on the floor of each side a few cells from the edge, so it can be bridged from either side; either one lays the bridge, from its own side. Each is chained up on its own: either by a padlock in a key colour (interact carrying a key of it, kept as keys are, or a skeleton key, used up), or, about half the time, to a switch on its own side (`World._bell_switch`: on a floor reachable from the bell without crossing any chasm, at least 6 cells off; throwing it, by hand or hex, frees that bell). The chain is wound across the bell and pulled down to an iron ring in the floor beside the post, where the padlock (the same as the garden gates', `RisoProp.padlock`) or the switch's plate closes it. Chained, struck or rung, it only rattles; the record keeps each one freed (`bells_free`, by its cell). Free, it waits in a soft halo: interact with it, or strike it with a hex bolt, and it swings and rings out, and the bridge lays itself across plank by plank from the bell's side (solid as each starts to lay, so nobody is dropped), pale boards over a rope rail. The level record keeps it up (`bridges`, `MapInfo.ring_bell`). The map marks bells (with their padlock's colour, or a night dot for a switch, until freed) and bridges (dashed while unrung), on other levels' pages too. Doors and switch gates are still placed where corridors happen to allow, and side doors are still locked by key colour.
- `tests/cemetery_test.gd` covers the bands, generation (determinism, what is placed, decor, the shape of each chasm and its one bell), the realm, the bell and bridge in play (the wizard falls in before, a hex rings it, they stand on it after, and it stays up), the fog, moths and wraiths; `tests/capture_cemetery.gd` takes stills (and the garden's fences).

## 4c. The sky

The third band (depths 12–17, 30–35, ...) is clusters of floating islands of cloud, with wide gaps of open air between, over an open drop (`NextWorldDef.sky`, `MapInfo.World.populate_sky`).

- **Terrain:** collapsed from its own sample, `wfc_images/sky_islands.png` (`NextWorldDef.ISLANDS`, drawn by `tests/make_sky_sample.gd`; no symmetry, so up stays up): islands packed close, two or three cells apart, each a flat top 4–9 cells wide over a body tapering underneath. Then the collapse is kept only in clusters (`World.cluster_islands`): ellipses 6–9 cells across by 4–6 down (`ISLE_RX`, `ISLE_RY`), thrown at random at least 4 cells apart side by side or 3 stacked (`ISLE_GAP`), and the rock outside them is cleared to open air. A cluster with under 42 % rock (`ISLE_ROCK`) gets more islands of the sample's shape (`_stamp_islands`), fitting smaller 3–6-cell shelves with one cell of air beside and below and two rows above, as far as that headroom allows. Then the islands taper underneath (`taper_islands`): under every stretch of an island's underside, rows of rock narrowing toward a point, a cell off each side a row (now and then a side holds), stopping before they stray past the cluster, meet anything, or come within 2 rows of rock below (`KEEL_CLEAR`); this is real rock, laid before the clusters are linked, so the causeways go round it. The clusters are linked so all can be reached (`link_isles`): along a spanning tree of the shortest links, a causeway of cloud stepping stones (`Puff`, two cells wide) from the lower floor of the pair up or across to the higher, in hops of three cells across or two across and one up (so each can be taken either way), and an updraft beside the far floor where it is too high above to hop up to (`_stones`). The causeways are kept clear of everything built after (`lanes`). A cluster no causeway can reach is cleared away, so nothing is placed out of reach. Ledges are only laid in and about the clusters, and stars near them, so the gaps stay open. Falling out of the bottom (off an edge, too, in the end) (200 px under the last row) costs a heart and puts the wizard back on the last rock they stood on in this level (`Player._footing`, `fall_back`; at the way in if none yet). Ledges, lifts and clouds don't count as footing, since they move or go.
- **Look:** its own realm (`RisoPrint.REALMS.sky`: pale periwinkle cloud for rock, sun-gold stars under a deep indigo night, cool white paper); a thin gold crescent moon and banks of cloud drifting far off in the islands' ink (`RisoBackground`). Its decor (`RisoDecor.plan_sky`): grass, flowers and stones on the islands, standing stones (tall rounded slabs, leaning a little), stone piles and windsocks, shorter, scattered seeded root bundles and occasional cloud wisps; F7's Sky bottoms picks Tapered (default: each step of an island's keel smoothed on the diagonal and a point under its last cell, with a few roots), Roots, Clouds (rounded seeded ground contours without dangling props), or Clouds & roots (cloud contours with fewer roots). These are cosmetic and leave collision tiles unchanged; danglers stop before terrain and structures, vines down their sides, and behind (the sky's background fences) lines of peace flags strung between two poles standing in stone piles: triangles in turn of blue, paper, sun, moss and plum, printed so they lift the night and read in colour.
- **Clouds that give way** (`Puff.gd`, `puff.tscn`): `PUFF_SHARE` (half) of the ledge runs are clouds: one-way like a ledge, printed as a row of pale puffs. Once landed on they tremble and thin, and 0.35 s later (`Puff.STAND`) give way, whether the wizard stays or jumps off (keep moving across a causeway); they gather again 3 s later (`REFORM`) once the wizard is clear.
- **Jump pads** (`Pad.gd`, `pad.tscn`): `PADS_PER_K` (1.5) on floors with headroom and a ledge or shelf within 4 cells above (at least one a level: failing such a floor, any floor with headroom). A squat paper cushion with a sun band and two sun chevrons bobbing over it. Landing on it (or walking onto it) throws the wizard about 4 cells up (letting go of Jump doesn't cut it short) and gives the dash back; it squashes and springs as it throws.
- **Updrafts** (`Wind.gd`, `wind.tscn`, `{"up": cells}`): `UPDRAFTS_PER_K` (0.8) shafts of open air rising 4–9 cells from a floor to just over a floor beside the shaft, always blowing: inside, the wizard's rise eases toward 430 px/s (`Wind.LIFT_SPEED`), which lifts them onto that ledge. Nothing else is placed in the shaft. Printed as pale streaks rising with glints of sun.
- **Gates: gaps and their vanes.** A sky level is gated by chasms as a cemetery is (`CHASMS_PER_K`, at least `CHASMS_MIN`), but its islands are too small for long floors to cut into, so it takes the gaps between islands that are already about as wide (7–12 cells, open air from the row over the floor to the row under it, two cells of floor on each side, the far floor on the same row or one lower, raised to the gust's landing height with clear headroom; `World._span_gaps`), then builds the rest (`World._build_gaps`): 8 or 10 cells of open air between two 3-cell shores, rock laid under the shores and air cleared over the gap, at the spots that change the fewest cells: across the air between two clusters where it can (a shortcut from one to the next), else by a cluster, never across a causeway. Then the caves are joined again. A gap's air is kept clear like a chasm's. There are no planks: one crosswind (`Wind`, `{"chasm", "width"}`) spans each gap, from the last cell of floor on one side to the first on the other, the three rows over the floor and the one under it. It is still until one of the gap's two vanes is turned. The vanes (`Vane.gd`, `vane.tscn`, which extends `Bell.gd`) stand where a cemetery's bells would, one each side, chained the same way (a padlock in a key colour, or a switch on its side). Freed, turn one (interact, or a hex bolt) and the wind blows from its side across (`MapInfo.turn_vane`; the record keeps it in `winds`): the wizard is carried 360 px/s along (`Wind.CARRY`, a move of its own, so it never builds up in the velocity) and held level (`GLIDE` 0) while in the gust. Up/down steers vertically at `Wind.STEER_SPEED` (240 px/s); releasing holds the new height, and descending out through the bottom lets the wizard fall out early. Carry and steering are separate moves, so vertical steering against a floor or ceiling does not stop the sideways wind, and neither builds up momentum. Turning the vane on the far side sends the wind back. Still, a run, a jump and a dash all fall short. A vane prints as a blue post with a sun arrow and a cross of cups on top: the arrow points across while its wind blows and the cups spin, it idles on the breeze otherwise, and it waits in a soft halo once freed. A still crosswind shows only a few faint motes over the gap; blowing, pale streaks and glints stream across.
- **Shielded enemies** (`Shield.gd`): `SHIELD_SHARE` (40 %) of the wisps and hoppers carry a shield (`Cell.mods`), a faint veil of pink round them with a paper glint. It takes the hex bolts meant for its host, neither wounded nor stunned, cracking with each (a jagged split of paper running in from the rim, glowing pink round it in a slow pulse) until it breaks after `SHIELD_HP` (3) hits, bursting into pink shards. A parried shot (a reflected bolt, `HexBolt.reflected`) breaks a whole shield at once. Touching a shielded enemy still hurts.
- **Rebounding shots** (`bullet.gd`): every watcher in the sky (`BOUNCE_SHARE`), and `SKY_WATCHERS_PER_K` (1.2) more of them on its floors, fire shots that glance off `BOUNCES` (2) walls before bursting on the next, found by a sweep ahead of the shot. They print as round pellets of sun with a pink core that flash as they bounce, and those watchers have two sun pellets circling their eye. Parried, a shot turns back on its shooter as ever.
- **Swooping birds** (`Bird.gd`, `bird_enemy.tscn`): `BIRDS_PER_K` (0.9), each patrolling a stretch of open sky out of the clusters (a row of open air at least 10 cells long, `BIRD_SPAN`), back and forth at its own height at 170 px/s, at least 10 cells from the way in and apart from one another. When the wizard is close below (within 320 px across; the dive target 80–360 px down) and it has rested 1.5 s, it starts a swoop only when the bird is inside the camera view with a 48 px margin. It dives through the wizard's height and up the far side to its patrol height, taking 1 s plus 0.0012 s per pixel of descent, and only where that arc is clear of rock. Its touch hurts; the hex wounds and stuns it, a parry stuns it. A pink swallow with a forked tail and a paper eye, wings beating, swept back in the dive with a faint pink trail.
- `tests/sky_test.gd` covers the band and realm, generation (determinism, clusters with the rock in them and every one linked by a causeway, gaps with a crosswind and vanes, no bridges or bells, and pads, clouds, updrafts, shields and rebounding shots all dealt), the open drop (the fall puts the wizard back a heart down), a pad throwing the wizard at its speed, a cloud holding then giving way then gathering again, a vane chained then turned, a run, a jump and a dash falling short without wind and the wind carrying the wizard over, the far vane sending it back, an updraft lifting up its shaft, a shield taking 3 bolts then breaking (and a parried shot breaking one at once), a shot glancing off a wall, every watcher's shots rebounding, and a bird patrolling its height, swooping at the wizard below and climbing back. `tests/capture_sky.gd` takes stills.

## 5. Economy

- Going deeper costs `P(d) = round(8 × 1.4^d)`: 8, 11, 16, 22, 43 at depth 5, 118 at depth 8.
- **Star clusters:** every level has one (`World.place_cluster`, placed after the hoppers and before the secret rooms), worth `cluster_value(d) = round(10 × 1.25^d)` stars: 10 at depth 0, 31 at depth 5. It hangs in open air, as a moon does, at least half the exit distance from the way back, and is three times as likely over thorns; failing that, it stands on the floor furthest from the way back. It prints as a big star in a wide halo with five small stars wheeling round it (`star_cluster.tscn`, `RisoProp._cluster`), and the map marks it. Taking it saves the run.
- Warp and rift cost stars each cast (§4).
- Stars per level grow at about 1.3^d.
- Shrines follow `S(d, tier)` above.
- The tension: diving makes stars and abilities cheaper to get, but each death deep down risks more.

## 6. Interface

- **Start menu:**
  - **Continue** appears when a run is saved and names where it will resume.
  - **Begin** starts at the world typed in; a blank field picks a random world.
  - **Random world** rolls a new world and starts it.
  - **Roll** puts a random number in the field, and **Paste** takes a bare number or a copied location.
- **Pause menu:** Resume, Copy location (`world 28 · depth 3`, for sharing), Give up (only while a lantern protects you, §2), and Save and exit. Pausing also saves.
- **Printed HUD:**
  - stars, health, the carried key, and the level (`world · depth`)

  - a strip of the abilities you own, each drawn as its shrine mark with a pip per tier

  - The run starts with 9,999 stars, and again whenever the run restarts.

## 7. Map screen

`scripts/riso/RisoMap.gd` replaces MapInfo's pixel overlay. `ShowMap` (M, or the pad's Y) opens the map on the level page and closes it. A/D (or the arrow keys) turn between the level and worlds pages, shown as tabs. The map pauses the game while open, and Menu closes it. It is printed on the ink plates like the HUD, in the 320×180 UI space, so it needs the print on.

- **Level view:** the current level on paper, as far as it has been seen.
  - **Seen:** each level's record holds `seen`, a byte per cell (`MapInfo.reveal`, `is_seen`).
  - **Reveal:** cells within 5 of the wizard reveal as they move. Each level also has one ink well on a floor. Paying it `round(4 × 1.3^depth)` stars inks the whole level onto the map at once, and the well is then dry for good.
  - **Persistence:** seen cells are kept across revisits and in the save.
  - **Ink:** rock prints as solid blue ink and explored open ground as a light blue screen. They are two one-texel-per-cell textures, rebuilt only when something new is seen.
  - **Marks, where seen:** abstract and small, printed over whatever is under them (rock, ground, each other) without knocking it out, so they overlap and intersect as the plates do.
    - exits: a chevron, plus a key-colour dot while locked or a star while unpaid
    - the shrine: an arch, dim once spent
    - lanterns: yellow dots, with a halo on your respawn; a night dot once spent
    - teleporters: a screened accent disc with the pair's sigil in night ink (`RisoProp.pair_sigil`, the same at both ends), so pairs can be matched; your rifts are eye-yellow discs
    - doors: key-colour bars; switch gates: night bars with an accent dot
    - keys: key-colour dots (eye yellow for a skeleton key)
    - ink wells, switches, relics, star clusters, bells and bridges
  - **Always marked:** the ghost, and the wizard (a pink hat).
- **World view:** each visited level is a tile labelled `world · depth` on the grid, centred on the current one, which is highlighted.
  - **Links:** opened side doors and paid deeper doors are drawn as bars between tiles.
  - **Marks:** the respawn lantern's level, spent shrines, and the ghost.
- **Legend:** each page has a legend down its right edge, drawn with the same mark functions as the map, so it always matches.
  - **Level page:** you, then only the kinds the page shows (each mark notes its row as it is drawn, `RisoMap._note`): way out, deeper, locked, unpaid, shrine, lantern, respawn, spent lantern, teleporter, rift, door, gate, switch, bell, vane, relic, side world doors, key, skeleton key, star cluster, ink well, ghost. Another level's page (picked on the worlds page) shows the same kinds where seen, from its layout and record (`RisoMap._other_things`).
  - **Worlds page:** you are here, visited, way opened, respawn, shrine used, ghost.
- `tests/map_test.gd` covers the reveal radius, shard chunks, reveal persisting across a revisit, the open, cycle and close states with pausing, and the world tiles and links. `tests/capture_map.gd` takes stills of both views.

## 8. The hex bolt, enemy health and cracked walls

- **Hex** (`Hex.gd`, `HexBolt.gd`): `Cast` (F, or the pad's X; the action is added at runtime if the project doesn't define it).
  - It throws a comet of glow ink from the hat, aimed like the dash: the held direction, else facing.
  - It flies up to 8 cells. It passes through one-way ledges and moving platforms, and stops at rock and portcullises.
  - It wounds the first enemy near its path, or breaks a cracked wall.
  - Charges come back one per 1.5 s, and lighting a lantern refills them. They show as glow sparks after the HUD's health beads.
- **Tiers:** hex is learned at a shrine (not known at the start: a run starts with the spell slot empty). II adds a charge, III adds damage, IV pierces its first enemy.
- **The dash strikes** (`DashStrike.gd`), so a wizard with any spell, or none, can still fight:
  - While dashing the wizard passes through enemies (their bodies stop blocking: physics layer 2, "Enemy"), and each enemy the dash passes within 64 px of is struck once a dash: stunned 2.5 s (`DashStrike.STUN`).
  - On its own the dash only stuns. The **strike** perk (shrines, up to III) wounds first, 1 damage per tier; a stunned enemy takes double, as from the hex.
  - Touching an enemy never hurts mid-dash, nor for 0.3 s after from one it struck (`GUARD`). Shots, thorns and lasers still do; a dash scatters moths for 6 s, and moth contact is guarded like enemy contact.
  - A shield takes the dash whole (a hit off it, no wound or stun), ends the dash and throws the wizard back.
  - Dashing into a cracked wall breaks it. A secret room's hidden rock is left alone: secrets are found by walking in (or with the hex).
  - A blink happens while the dash runs, so it strikes everything along the way it jumps.
  - `tests/dash_strike_test.gd` covers it.
- **Enemies** get a `Wound`:
  - **Health:** 1 HP at depths 0–2, 2 at 3–5, 3 from 6. A stunned (parried) enemy takes double.
  - **Hits:** a hit bursts pink and knocks the enemy back.
  - **Death:** the enemy drops 1–2 stars. Stars buy upgrades and travel; lanterns restore death protection.
- **Slain until you die:** the level record keeps slain enemies. Dying clears them in every level and reloads the respawn level, so everything is back.
- **Cracked walls** (`CrackedWall.gd`): up to 10 per level, placed last, so they can block anything.
  - **Shape:** thin walls, one or two cells thick with open space on both sides. Half are picked within 3 cells of a key, lantern, exit, shrine or ink well.
  - **Solid:** a cracked cell is not a rock tile but a solid block on the same collision layer.
  - **Printing:** the terrain prints it as rock (body, shading, grass), and its prop adds a few fine night-ink cracks, quiet but visible up close.
  - **Breaking:** a bolt crumbles it in blue dust, and the rock reprints without it. The record keeps it broken, even across deaths.
- **Arming** (the ghost and dropped keys): they now arm once the wizard has been more than about a cell away. An overlap test armed them too early while a level reloaded, because the wizard's collision is off then.
- **Fixed:** `hit_box.gd`'s `stunned` setter never stored the value.
- **Fixed: stuck after a dash.** A dash ending inside an enemy turned enemy collision straight back on, and the push out of the enemy's body could wedge the wizard or fling them across the level. Enemies now block again only once the wizard is clear of every enemy body (`DashStrike._inside_enemy`).
- `tests/hex_test.gd` covers the hex, charges and lantern refill, wounding, double damage when stunned, star drops, slain until death, rock stopping the bolt, cracked walls (deterministic, not tiles, broken for good) and tiers. `tests/capture_hex.gd` takes stills.
