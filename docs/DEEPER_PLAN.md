# Deeper: design and implementation plan

The goal of the game becomes getting deeper. The world is a grid of generated levels addressed by **(seed, depth)**. Every seed is a column you can dig down. Moving sideways steps to the neighbouring seed, as if you had started there. Because every level is a pure function of its address, players exploring the same seed see the same places, so sharing coordinates is how people explore together.

No level is guaranteed to be fully reachable. Places you can't reach yet are the point: you come back with new abilities.

## Decisions

| Topic | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Lateral movement | Infinite. Left and right exits go to `seed − 1` / `seed + 1` at the same depth. Each is locked with a key colour dealt by the level seed. The key is kept, the door stays open, and the side door you arrive through opens behind you. |
| Exit distance | Grows with depth, physically. Deeper levels place their exits further from the arrival point (and may be larger). |
| Going back | Always free. |
| Deeper | Costs stars, paid once per deeper door; the door then stays open. |
| Reachability | Not guaranteed. Exploration of the generated space is the game. |
| Respawn | At the last lantern you lit. It absorbs one death, then burns out. The run's starting lantern is lit. Lanterns are scarce: one at each level's way back and about one more. |
| Death | A lit lantern brings you back once at one heart; you drop all your stars into a ghost. The used lantern becomes spent for the rest of the run. |
| Restoring protection | Find and light another unspent lantern. Recovering the ghost restores your stars and full health; Mend can heal before recovery. |
| End of a run | Dying without a lit lantern ends the run. |
| Keys | Not used up. You carry one (more with the keyring); grabbing another leaves your oldest key where the new one was. Keys work in any level. A rare skeleton key opens any one door, then crumbles. |
| Abilities | Bought at shrines (no shop), and every ability has upgrade tiers. Cheaper deeper. A shrine offers its ability tier or, instead, healing to full (dearer deeper). |
| Ways to play | Enter a seed and dig its column, or start at a random seed. |

## 1. The world: a grid of (seed, row)

- **Rows both ways** (`docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md`): a run starts at row 0; rows below it (positive) lead down, rows above it (negative) lead up. A level's `depth` is its distance from the start, `|row|`, and everything that grows "with depth" (prices, star values, enemy health, level size, relic chances, key rarity) grows with that distance, whichever way. Its title reads "world 28 · depth 5" below the start and "world 28 · height 5" above it, and its place marks turn the depth arrow up (`RisoMarks.place_marks`).
- `level_seed = hash(seed, row)` drives everything: WFC terrain, object placement, key and door colours, the shrine offer; the row picks the band (WFC sample region and riso realm).
- Four exits per level:
  - **The way on** (`Exit.DEEPER`) leads one row away from the start: down below it, up above it (`NextWorldDef.away`). It costs `P(d)` stars once, then stays open; its chevron points the way it leads. Below the start it is placed low in the level and the way back high; above the start the other way about, so the up branch is climbed, with a few ledges laid toward it (`Climb`, `docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §4; nothing promised).
  - **The way back** (`Exit.BACK`) leads one row toward the start, and is free. At the start itself it is **the way up**: a door framed pink like a way on (`NextWorldDef.leads_on`; pink on the map too), with the start lantern beside it, to row −1 that costs what the way down does (`P(0)`, paid once, `up_paid` in the record), arriving at row −1's way back; coming home from row −1 arrives at the start's way up (`NextWorldDef._homeward`).
  - **Left and right** lead to `(seed ± 1, row)`. They are placed at the side edges.
  - Each side door is locked with a key colour, `lateral_lock(level, side)`, dealt by the level seed. Carrying that colour opens it and you go through; the key is kept. It then stays open in the level record. The side door you arrive through is marked open, so the way back never needs a key.
  - **A way out of the first level:** at depth 0 one side door (left or right, whichever side of the start it falls on) is placed on the nearest floor at least 5 cells from the start that the wizard can hop to from it, instead of at its side edge. A key in that door's lock colour is laid on a floor hopped to from the start, as near the way to the door as can be (the start key, on top of the level's usual keys). The way from the start to both is kept clear of doors and switch gates (`LevelGen.keep_clear`). "Hopped to" is the rough reach shared with hyperspace (`scripts/worlds/Reach.gd`), with hops 3 cells across rather than 4, so it is easy. `tests/start_escape_test.gd` checks 40 first levels.
- You arrive at the matching exit of the next level: going deeper puts you at its Back exit, and so on.
- Exits sit at least `D(d)` cells from the arrival point, with `D` growing with depth. Deeper levels can also grow beyond 64×64.
- **Lanterns are scarce.** Every level has an unlit lantern beside its way back (where a dive arrives; at depth 0 the lit start lantern stands there instead), and `LANTERNS_PER_K` (0.4) more per 1000 cells, at least one: two in most levels, where there were six to eight (one beside every exit and 1.5 per 1000 cells). The other exits have none.
- **Connected caves:** after the terrain is generated, open pockets under 6 cells are filled with rock. Every other open region is joined to the largest by carving the shortest tunnel through rock, nearest first, until the level is one cave. So every exit, the shrine, the ink well, keys, lanterns and moons are connected through open space. They are not necessarily reachable: gates (doors, cracked walls, locked or unpaid exits) and abilities (height, gaps) still apply. `tests/connect_test.gd` checks 20 levels across worlds and depths.
- **Size grows with depth:** `level_size(d)` = (36 + 3d) × (30 + 2.5d) cells, capped at 60 × 48 (from depth 8). That is 36 × 30 at depth 0 and 54 × 45 at depth 6. (It was 6d × 5d to 80 × 72, which grew too fast and ran slowly.) An archetype may scale its levels (`Archetype.scale`): the crags' are narrower and taller, the sky's bigger, the cemetery's 35 % wider and 12 % taller (`CemeteryArchetype.SCALE`), for its wide crypts and the rock round them (the secret rooms need it). Each level is enclosed in solid rock three cells thick, flush against its edges, and the camera stops one cell into that rock.
- **Level record:** each visited level stores only what changed. That is stars taken, doors opened, keys dropped or taken, the deeper door paid, lanterns lit, and the ghost. On a revisit the level regenerates identically and the record is applied on top.
- **Determinism:** a failed WFC collapse currently retries with `randi()`. It must retry with `hash(level_seed, attempt)` instead. Neighbouring levels pre-generate on the background thread so transitions are instant.

## 2. Death, the ghost and the end of a run

- **Die with a lit lantern:** all carried stars go into a ghost at the death spot, recorded in that level. Any previous ghost and its stars are lost. The lit lantern absorbs the death, burns out, and brings you back to its location at full health, even if it is in another world. The ghost keeps that lantern (`RunState.ghost_lantern`). Recovering the ghost returns the dropped stars and relights the lantern its death burned out (`MapInfo.recover_ghost`): unspent again and, if no other lantern has been lit since, your protected respawn again. It does not heal. Travelling, saving and lighting another lantern preserve current health.
- **Grace on arriving:** coming out of a portal or rift, arriving in a level through any door (or a tier III rift), and coming back at a lantern after dying each give a second of invulnerability (`Player.GRACE_TIME`, its own timer `graced`, so it never shortens or blocks a hit's 2 s), with the wizard flickering as after a hit. `tests/death_test.gd` and `tests/rift_test.gd` check it.
- **Spent lanterns:** each lantern can absorb only one death per run. Its cell is recorded as spent in its world's record; travelling away, revisiting, or continuing a saved run cannot relight it. Switching to another lantern before dying does not consume the former one. Recovering the ghost is the one way a spent lantern burns again.
- **Restore protection:** interact with another unspent lantern. It becomes your protected respawn point. Or recover the ghost, which relights the lantern its death burned out. Star collection never restores protection; you can light another lantern without recovering the ghost first.
- **Giving up:** stuck (down a pit with no way out), choose Give up on the pause menu. It is only offered while a lantern protects you (`MapInfo.can_give_up`): it is a death like any other, so the stars drop into a ghost, the lantern burns out and you come back at it at full health (`MapInfo.give_up`). Without a lit lantern it would end the run, so it is not offered.
- **Refilling at a lantern:** lighting a lantern, or interacting with the one already lit, refills the hex's charges and mend's draughts (`MapInfo.light_lantern`). (Burning the lit lantern into mend, spending it for the draughts, is retired.)
- **Die without a lit lantern:** the run ends. The seed's levels are unchanged, since they are generated. Your character and level records reset, and the new run starts with a fresh lit lantern. The ended run's save is deleted immediately.
- **Arming:** a ghost can only be recovered once the player has stepped off it, so dying on the respawn lantern doesn't hand the stars straight back.
- **Presentation:** the traveler's carried lantern burns yellow while protected; otherwise it glows a dim, pulsing pink and trails pink smoke. Spent lanterns have empty, dark glass, a charred wick, the same fading smoke thread and no light pool. Unspent lanterns keep their low ember. Insects no longer circle lanterns. The ghost uses the printed astral-silhouette style, and a pointer at the edge of the view leads toward it, naming its place when it is in another level.
- **Saves:** existing version-1 saves remain readable. An old unprotected save marks its last respawn lantern spent when loaded. `tests/death_test.gd` covers consumption, full-health respawns, relighting rejection, cross-world deaths, star and ghost recovery (the lantern relit, no healing), revisits, save migration, and run reset.

## 3. Keys

- A held key opens every door of its colour in any level. Opened doors stay open.
- You carry one key at a time, or more with the **keyring** perk (`KeyRing.gd`): each tier carries one more (I two, II three, III four, one of each colour). Grabbing a new one with no room leaves your oldest key where the new one was, and the level record keeps it there. A key of a colour you already carry is left lying. Every key carried trails the wizard in a little chain, the newest behind the shoulder beside the bag and skeleton keys last (`RisoWizard._trail_keys`), an exam paper written out in full in front of the figure; the HUD shows no keys. The keys are on the player's `KeyRing` node (`player.keyring`, oldest first); saves keep them all (`keys`), and older saves load with their one key.
- **Skeleton keys** (the rare key): in `SKELETON_CHANCE` (30 %) of levels from depth 1, dealt by the level seed (`Rules.skeleton_at`), one takes the place of a star in the level's last secret room (on an open floor if the level has no secret room). They print bone white (bare paper, `RisoPrint.ink_key`): a key whose bow is a bone's two-knobbed end, with the shaft and bit of the other keys (three teeth, as a moss key's) (`RisoMarks.key_shape`), with a pale halo and a paper twinkle; on paper (the prompt disc, a shrine's medallion) they print in night, and the map marks them, and bone gates, in grey (night screened to half). They go in a pocket apart from the ring (`skeleton_keys`, trailing after the ring's keys). Interacting with a locked door (a corridor door or a side door) that no carried key opens uses one up and opens the door for good. Walking into a door never spends one. The map marks them in eye yellow.
- **Bone gates:** doors of pale bone-grey ironwork with a bone padlock (bare white, a bone on its face) that only a skeleton key opens: interact carrying one, and it is used up; walking into one never spends it, and no coloured key opens it. A level's doors take a bone gate's colour, `KeyRing.SKELETON`, so the map, prompts and records treat them as doors. Three kinds (`Rules.BONE_VAULT_CHANCE`, `RELIC_GATE_CHANCE`, `SKELETON_DOOR_DEPTH`, `SKELETON_DOOR_CHANCE`):
  - **Bone vaults:** in 25 % of levels from depth 1, dealt by the level seed (`Rules.bone_vault_at`), a vault behind a bone gate holding a hoard worth three star clusters (`LevelGen.BONE_LOOT`), a step above a plum vault. Placed after the keyed vaults (`LevelGen.place_bone_vault`): carved two high in the level's own rock (3 or 2 across), or where there is none (a cemetery's terraces, the sky's islands) built as a 2 × 2 strongbox. Strongboxes may now take the place of stars and plain ledges, not only empty air.
  - **Relics behind bone:** in half of relic levels (`Rules.relic_gated_at`) the relic waits in the bone vault instead of a secret room; with no room for one, it goes back to the first secret room (or, with none, a floor in the open).
  - **On the way:** from depth 6, 15 % of corridor doors are bone gates (dealt with the colours, `LevelGen.deal_colors`).
  - **Bought at a shrine:** at full health, the mending station sells a skeleton key instead of a relic's whereabouts at 30 % of shrines (`Shrine.SKELETON_SALE`), 70 % while a relic it already marked waits to be found (`SKELETON_SALE_HINTED`), and always when there is no relic left to point to. It costs twice the level's deeper price. Bought, the key is laid on a floor somewhere in the level, at least 10 cells from the shrine where there is one (`MapInfo.lay_sold_skeleton`), kept in the record as a dropped key, so it shows on the map until taken. The station shows a bone on a paper medallion.
  - `tests/bones_test.gd` covers them; `tests/capture_bones.gd` takes stills.
- Door colours are dealt per level. A matching key may be far away, or in another column.
- **Rarity:** the four colours are not equally common. By `Rules.KEY_RARITY` (18 : 7 : 3 : 1) sun is the commonest, then ember, moss and plum (about 62 %, 24 %, 10 % and one in 30). Keys, corridor doors, side-door locks (`lateral_lock`) and bell and vane padlocks are all dealt by these weights (`Rules.rarity_color`), so a common key opens a lot and a rare key seldom does, and which keys to carry is a choice. Key and door colours are dealt once the level is laid out (`LevelGen.deal_colors`), from the level seed and each thing's place in the order things were laid, never the world RNG; the colour is the cell's `extra_info`. A level's first key is always sun, so every level holds a key to its commonest doors. Keys themselves are scarce: a level keeps 1 per 1000 cells, at least 2 (`KEYS_PER_K`, `KEYS_MIN`, `LevelGen.key_count`; 2 at the surface, 3 in the largest levels), besides the start key and skeleton keys. Spots are still drawn for 2 per 1000 cells, at least 4 (`KEY_SPOTS_PER_K`), so levels are laid out as they were; only the first `key_count` keep their key. The rarer a key, the longer its shaft and the more teeth on its bit (sun one, ember two, moss three, plum four; `RisoMarks.key_shape`), on the key, the wizard's chain and door prompts alike.
- **Vaults** (`LevelGen.place_vaults`, placed last of all): small rooms sealed in rock behind a locked portcullis, entered at floor height from a floor beside them, their loot in plain sight through the bars. 0.5 per 1000 cells (`VAULTS_PER_K`, at least one), as many as fit: found like a secret room (4, 3 or 2 cells across, 2 high, or 3 or 2 across and 1 high), but wholly walled in the level's own rock, which `make_room` leaves whole. A level with no rock thick enough (a sky level's thin islands) builds one instead, a strongbox: a 2 × 1 room walled, roofed and floored in rock out of free air beside a floor (hanging off its edge where there is no floor under it), with two rows of open air over it and one under any floor it builds, never near a chasm or gap (`LevelGen._strongbox_spots`), and never where it would shut open air in or cut a switch off from the way in with its gate shut (`LevelGen._shuts_in`: another spot is drawn). A vault's lock is dealt evenly among the four colours, unlike every other lock, and the rarer the colour the better the loot (`LevelGen.VAULT_LOOT`, `LevelGen.vault_loot`): sun a half-sized star cluster, ember a star cluster, and 40 % of the time a moss key too (`VAULT_KEY_CHANCE`, dealt by the level seed and the vault's door), moss a cluster and a half, plum a star cluster and a skeleton key. A cluster's worth is its share of `cluster_value(depth)` (at least 2). So rare keys open little, but what they open is worth it. Opening one is an ordinary door opening (the record keeps it). `tests/vaults_test.gd` covers rarity and vaults across 20 levels (18 have one); `tests/capture_vaults.gd` takes stills.
- A dropped key arms only once the player has stepped off it, so the swap does not immediately grab it back.

## 4. Shrines and tiered abilities

- One shrine per level, standing on two floor cells (its three stations packed side by side) 3–14 cells from the deeper exit.
- It offers three things: two different abilities and mending. Taking any one spends the shrine, and the level record keeps it spent: At least one of the two is always a strict upgrade, a gain with nothing given up: a new perk or the next tier of something already known, never a spell that would replace the one in the slot (if both picks would be swaps, the second is traded for the first strict upgrade along).
  - **The boon:** the next tier of an ability. The ability is picked by `level_seed`, or is the next one along that still has a tier to learn.
  - **Mending:** healing to full. It is the alternative to the boon and does nothing at full health.
- **Pay to learn.** There is no menu: interact with the side you want. The shrine prints no text from afar: stepping up to a niche (or the mending bowl) pops up a larger plaque over it with the ability's name, tier and price (or mending's price).
- **Prices:**
  - Learning costs `S(d, tier) = max(6, round(20 × 1.6^(tier−1) × 0.8^d))`: cheaper deeper, dearer per tier.
  - Mending works the other way: `H(d) = round(6 × 1.35^d)`, which is 6 at depth 0 and 27 at depth 5.
- **The dash, slash and mend are known at the start**, one in each slot: the dash for movement, slash for damage, mend I (one draught) for utility (`"base": 1` in `Abilities.ABILITIES`); every damage spell parries as it is cast (see Parry below). Everything else is found at shrines. A shrine offers an ability not yet known before any upgrade: it starts from the seed's pick and goes round. Learning another ability for a slot replaces what is in it.
- **Three slots** (`Abilities.SLOTS`), each holding one ability at a time on a button of its own: the **damage** slot on the Spell button (Q, or the pad's X or left trigger): hex, slash, nova, push, seeker and lunge, the wizard's only ways to hurt, each raising the parry guard as it is cast; the **movement** slot on the Dash button (Shift, or the right trigger): the dash, blink, astral projection, levitate, rift, warp and ferry (the dash and blink are the wizard's own moves, `Player.dashes`; the rest are cast); and the **utility** slot on the Utility button (R, or the right shoulder button): awareness, mend and resurrect (§4b, a crypt's rite). A utility spell never moves the wizard or hurts anything itself (that is what the other two slots are for), though it may enhance what they do. Learning something for a slot at a shrine replaces what is in it, and the pop-up over that niche is topped by a "swap" tag; something for an empty slot is a strict upgrade. Perks (double jump, wall climb, vigor, speed, keyring, ward) stack. What is given up is not lost: it comes back as an **exam paper** at its tier (`MapInfo.put_back`; a shrine, a lesson and a lantern's study all hand one back), carried on the key ring as keys are and learned again at any lantern (see §4e, the institute). `tests/shrine_test.gd` covers the swap, `tests/spells_test.gd` the slots.
- **Levitate:** press Dash in the air to stop falling and float. You drift slowly up and down with the stick and sideways at walking pace, until it runs out or you press Dash again. You get one float per landing, and a moon brings it back.
- **Awareness:** press Utility to sense the level. While it lasts, pointers at the edge of the view show where things are, like the ghost's arrow. A short cooldown follows.
- **Astral projection** uses the Dash button. Tap it to leave your body and go out as an untouchable projection. Tap again, or get hurt, to snap back to the body. Let it run out and you stay where the projection is. There are no astral orbs any more.
- **Rift:** learn it at a shrine, then press Dash to place an end at your position. A second cast places its partner; interact to travel between them. Casting again deletes both old ends in the current world and starts a new pair. Each world (seed, depth) keeps one spell-created pair in its saved level record: pairs in other worlds remain untouched and return when you revisit. Swapping spells preserves placed pairs; starting a new run clears them. Travelling always needs the interact key. Tier I requires standing on a surface; tier II allows placement in midair. At tier III, casting builds the run's one cross-world link instead (`RunState.rift_link`, saved with the run): place one end, travel to another level, place the other, and interacting with either end travels to the other's level and comes out of it. A third cast closes both old ends wherever they are; per-world pairs made at lower tiers stay.
- **Switch gates:** every level has at least one (0.6 per 1000 cells): a gate across a corridor, like a door but with no lock, and its switch on a floor well away from it (`LevelGen.switch_floor`: 8 to 16 cells off, `SWITCH_MIN` to `SWITCH_MAX`, where there is such a floor, else the nearest further, else the furthest nearer, never right beside it; every switch is placed this way: a gate's, a chasm bell's or vane's, a gondola station's, a run of thorns') and reachable from the way in with that gate shut. A switch is on or off: interact with it to turn it over. On, its gate is up; off, it drops again, but only once the wizard is clear of it. A hex bolt turns a switch on, never off, so a gate can never be shot shut from its far side with the wizard beyond it. About a third of switches start on (`Switch.START_ON_SHARE`, dealt by the level seed and the switch's cell, no RNG draw; never a gondola station's, `Gondola`). The level record keeps each switch as it was left (`LevelRecord.switched`: cell -> on), and the gate follows its switch (`MapInfo.gate_open`); the portcullis winches up and down. The map marks gates and switches.
- **Thorns that come and go** (`Spikes`, `LevelGen.place_thorn_modes`, the last pass, `tests/thorns_test.gd`): past the start, runs of thorns (thorn cells side by side) are dealt by hashing the level seed and the run's first cell. 30 % go on a timer (`THORN_TIMED_SHARE`): out for 1.6 s of every 3 s (`Spikes.UP`, `CYCLE`), from a phase of their own, the whole run together, their tips peeking and flickering pink 0.45 s before they rise (`WARN`). 25 % go on a switch (`THORN_SWITCH_SHARE`, at most 2 a level), placed as any switch is (`switch_floor`, one draw each) on a floor reached from the way in: on, the switch drops them; off, they are out. Down, thorns do not hurt and can be walked through; rising under the wizard they hurt at once. A timed thorn cell may say how long it is out (`extra_info` `"up"`: a crypt's alternating thorns), and `Reach` counts timed thorns as passable (`Reach.timed`). A chasm's thorns and the cells kept for a big thing are never dealt, so a chasm still bars the way. A switched run's first thorn bears the sigil its switch shares. Across the test levels about a quarter of runs are timed and one in twelve switched.
- **Thorns send the wizard back** (`Spikes._touched`, `Player.thorn_back`, `tests/thorns_test.gd`): a hit from thorns that lands (a heart, or the ward's charge) puts the wizard back on the last safe footing they stood on in this level (`Player._back_spot`). That is the last safe rock (`Player.footing`, kept in every level: rock, not a block, with no thorns in the cells under either side of them, `_safe_here`), unless a floating ledge was stood on since (`Player.perch`, `_floats`: a platform, a lift or a rolling ladder's step, or a ferry raft; not a cloud that gives way nor a bridge of the dead). The ledge is remembered with where on it they stood (`perch_off`), so they are put back at that spot wherever it has moved to; if it is gone (a raft replaced or fading) or thorns lie there now, the rock instead. Struck, they are gone for `THORN_PAUSE` (0.8 s): hidden where the thorns struck them, nothing moving them, the buttons doing nothing, the timers waiting and the view held still (`CameraControl`); then they appear back on that footing (worked out as they appear, so a lift that moved on meanwhile is followed), in a puff of their glow, the view cut to them (`Player._appear`), never already moving, and the hit's untouchable time all comes after it. A blow that slays them, or one met on the way somewhere else, sends them nowhere; arriving somewhere new ends the pause (`Player.grace`). Where they have stood on nothing safe yet in this level, the hit only throws them back. A sky level's fall out of the bottom uses the same spot (`fall_back`), without the pause.
- **Sigils: which goes with which.** Every link in a level has a sigil (`Sigils.gd`, `LevelGen.sigil_at`): both ends of a pair of teleporters share one, and each switch shares one with what it works (its gate, the bell or vane it frees, the lift it starts). There are 12 (`RisoMarks.SIGILS`: a disc, a triangle, a square, a diamond, a triangle pointing down, a crescent, a cross, a saltire, a five-pointed star, an hourglass, a dome, three dots), none of them a mark that means something else. Teleporter pairs are dealt first, then switches, every link its own sigil while any are left; a level with more links than that (only big sky levels, up to about 17) reuses the sigil whose other links lie furthest from it. Where the shapes start and how they step through are hashed from the level seed (`Sigils.DEAL`), with no world RNG draws, once the level is laid out, so layouts are unchanged and a level deals the same on every visit and on the map. A teleporter prints its sigil on the paper disc over its rim; a switch on a paper plate on the front of its base; what it works on a paper plate with the switch emblem before the sigil (on a gate's cross-rail, where a padlock would hang on a bell's or vane's chain, on a parked lift's bar), and a bell's prompt shows the same pair. The map prints them small by teleporters, switches, gates and switch-chained bells and vanes. `tests/sigils_test.gd` covers the dealing (pairs and switches matching their partners, no two links sharing while there are sigils to go round, reuse by distance, the same on every build) and placed things reading them; `tests/capture_sigils.gd` takes stills.
- **Switch lifts:** (the institute has none: its lifts are rolling ladders, §4e) half of an ordinary level's lifts (`LevelGen.LIFT_SWITCH_SHARE`, picked by hashing the level seed and the lift's cell) wait for a switch on the free floor nearest them (within `LIFT_SWITCH_NEAR`, 6 cells) that is reachable from the way in. While its switch is off the lift stands still, its rune dark and the switch's emblem and sigil (see "Sigils" above) on a plate on its bar: parked at the start of its track as the level is entered, or wherever it was when the switch was turned off. On (by hand, or a hex bolt), it runs on from where it stands (the record keeps the switch on or off). Placed last, by `place_lift_switches`, with no world RNG draws, so nothing else in a level moves; ordinary levels never need a lift to get through, so a parked one blocks nothing. Side worlds' lifts (hyperspace's crossings) always run. `tests/ferry_and_lifts_test.gd` covers it.
- **Kinds of world** (`scripts/worlds/`): every place a run can be in has a definition, `NextWorldDef` (from `Rules.def_for(at)`; `MapInfo.here` for the place being played), and the rest of the game asks it rather than knowing which kind of place it is in. The definition says how the terrain is collapsed (`region`, `size`, `fallback`) and dressed (`populate`), where each exit leads (`lead`: the place, the way the transition sweeps, the exit arrived at; `neighbours` for prefetch), what each exit costs (`price`, `pay`), and how the place prints (`realm`, `grows`, `title`, `exit_dir`, `exit_grand`, `grid_at` on the worlds map). The base class is the ordinary cave level. Every other kind is a **side world** (`SideWorld`), reached through a door that some levels deal (`deals`, `entry_price`). You arrive at its way back (`Exit.BACK`, which returns you out of that door); its way on (`Exit.DEEPER`) leads to its `destination`, and from then on that level's way back leads in again (`arriving`). A dead end, whose destination is the level it was entered from, just returns there. Side worlds are listed in `Worlds.KINDS`. Kind k entered from level (seed, row) sits at `(seed, -(SIDE_BASE + k * STRIDE + row + STRIDE / 2))` (`Worlds.side_at`, `origin_of`, `kind_at`; `SIDE_BASE` is 1,000,000, so the rows above the start stay levels), so each has its own record, save entry, prefetch and tile on the worlds map. Saves from before rows went both ways (version 2) are read with their side worlds moved to this (`RunState.from_v2`, `Worlds.from_v2`). A door into kind k is exit number `Worlds.door(k)` (16 + k), kept in the level's `exits`. Its price is paid once (`doors_paid` in the record). The exits taken are kept as `ways_taken`. **To add a world:** extend `SideWorld`, set its name, crossing direction (`way`), realm, size, sample and price in `_init`, override `deals` and `populate` (and `destination_for` with `arriving`, `depth_for` and `fallback` as needed), then list it in `Worlds.KINDS`. Its door, exits, prices, travel, prefetch, save entry, map ribbon and legend rows come for free.
- **Gates and bosses** (`scripts/bosses/`, `docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §5): the last level of each band, toward the next, is a gate level (`Bosses.gate_at`: rows ±3 for the garden, then every 6 rows out). Its way on is sealed (`NextWorldDef.seal`; pink bars and the boss's eye on the door, no key or stars open it) while the band's boss lives, in every world column, and so is a side door whose world leads out past a living boss's gate (`Bosses.crossed`: a hyperspace from the garden waits on a garden boss). A boss is beaten once per run (`RunState.bosses`): then every gate level of its band opens, it is never placed again (`LevelLoader.place_cell`), and where it fell a relic waits, free, holding a move not yet known (`Bosses.relic_for`; `RunState.boss_relics` keeps it until taken; with every move known, a skeleton key and a star cluster). Every boss fights in its gate level itself (`LevelGen.place_boss`: the worm nearest the way on, the bramble in its knot, the golem in its hall's lair, the stand-in nearest the way on); there are no arenas. A save naming a place in an arena, from before, is moved out to the level its door was in (`RunState.gone_kinds`). The worm is built (`Worm`, its own prefab in `Bosses.SCENES`; `docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §6): ten cell-sized segments that bend like a pipe, every one but the head with thorns along one flank, the one facing the wizard as it comes out of the rock, kept until it goes in again (they hurt, and turn strikes from that side), crawling the walls of the whole level once the wizard comes near, digging through walls and rising from and sinking into burrows all through it (pausing, the ground bulging pink and the screen rumbling, before it comes out of the rock), its head biting, cut short or split in two by cuts, slain when no worm is left (`tests/worm_test.gd`). The bramble is built too (`Bramble`, `docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §6): its gate level has a shaft cut near the top (`BrambleShaft`, before anything else is placed, so only its gate levels change), four cells wide and half the level tall, walled in, with ledges a hop apart from side to side and a passage out through both walls at its foot; the way on stands on a landing at its head, and the knot the bramble is rooted in fills the head beside it, so the way on is reached only once it is slain. Its thorn vines lash out of the walls on a cadence (hurting only while out; a parried lash recoils for the stun), and its 3 to 5 bulbs, set in the walls, spit seeds a parry turns back into them and take 3 hits each from any bolt or dash (`Wound.least`); a burst bulb withers the vines it feeds, and the last tears the knot open, leaving the relic on the landing (`tests/bramble_test.gd`). Every boss now has its own prefab (`Bosses.SCENES`: the worm, the bramble, the golem, the necromancer, the whale and the beast); the stand-in (`Boss`: a wisp twice the size, 10 hits, shown as pips over it) is left for a boss without one. A lantern death heals it, as the level reloads. Gate levels hold no relic of their own. `tests/gate_test.gd` covers it.
- **The hyperspace door:** a rare second way on, dealt by the level seed in 18 % of levels a row or more from the start (`Hyperspace.CHANCE`), on the floor furthest from the way in. It costs 5 times the deeper exit's price (`Hyperspace.PRICE`), paid once, and leads into **hyperspace** rather than straight down. It prints as a double arch (accent round pink) under a falling cascade of chevrons. `tests/switches_test.gd` covers switches, the hyperspace door and the parry; `tests/capture_switches.gd` takes stills.
- **Hyperspace** (`scripts/worlds/Hyperspace.gd`, a side world): a short, linear, hazard-packed crossing, 64 × 16 cells, between the level the door is paid in and one `Hyperspace.DROP` (4) rows further from the start: in the same branch, or, in `Hyperspace.CROSS` (35 %) of them, across the start in the other (up from a level below it, down from one above; `Hyperspace.crosses`). It has no ink well, shrine, keys or side doors. You come in at its way back on the left; its way back returns to the level, out of the hyperspace door, free. Its gate on the right (printed like the hyperspace door, and free: it was paid on the way in) drops you at the way back of that level, and by a draw of the entry level's seed (`Hyperspace.drift`) a world to the left, straight across, or a world to the right. The levels in between are skipped, not visited. On screen it is "world N · hyperspace".
  - **Landing level:** a level hyperspace drops into (`Hyperspace.arriving`; where two would, the one from the same branch wins, then the one straight across, then the one from the left) has its way back lead into that hyperspace, arriving at its gate, and also an ordinary way up (`Exit.RETURN`, placed a few cells from the way back) to the level above it, arriving at that level's deeper exit. Using it joins the two on the worlds map.
  - **Address:** `(seed, -depth - 1)` (kind 0), the depth being the level the jump was paid in. Its `depth` is the middle of the drop, for enemy health.
  - **Terrain:** collapsed by the same WFC as any level, but from its own, more dangerous 32 × 32 sample, `wfc_images/chasm_gauntlet.png` (thorn-capped floors, thorn-bottomed pits, toothed ceilings, thorned islands; about 10 % thorns; `tests/make_hyperspace_sample.gd` draws it), as a 64 × 16 strip. The collapse alone is often not passable; the dressing makes it so (below).
  - **Dressing** (`Hyperspace.populate`): both ends (8 cells) are laid flat and safe, with the way back and a lantern at the left, the gate and a lantern at the right, and a rest lantern halfway along. Then the gaps are filled (`Hyperspace._lifts`): a gliding ledge every 4 cells across every wide open stretch, a stack of rising and falling ledges every 3 cells up every tall wall, with moons beside them (at least 4 cells apart). Last, a crossing is made sure of (`Hyperspace._bridge`): the cheapest way through the open air from the way back to the gate is found (keeping to floors, crossing air only where it must, and through thorns only where nothing else gets through: those thorns are cleared), and a rough reach (2 cells up and 4 across a hop, a cell further across per two fallen; a moon caught on the way gives another hop) is followed along it from the way back. Wherever the reach stops, the lift (or, failing that, a floating ledge or a moon) that carries a rider furthest on along the way is laid, until the gate is reached (at most 96). `Hyperspace.crossable` checks the result. Seven watching eyes and five wisps are spread along the floor (one every few cells), with four more moons and 14 stars. Five lasers (`Laser.gd`, `Hyperspace._lasers`) are set in its rock, spread along the way and at least 5 cells apart, each in a ceiling, wall or floor with at least 3 cells of open air in front: on a cadence (0.9 s warming up behind a faint flickering sight line, 0.7 s firing, 1.6 s resting, each out of step with the others) it fires a beam straight out until the first rock (or ledge or lift), which hurts only while it fires. Lasers are only in hyperspace for now.
  - **Theme:** hyperspace prints in a realm of its own (its `print_realm`, `RisoPrint.REALMS.hyperspace`: violet rock, aqua stars, a cold paper), switched to on the way in and back to the realm you came from on the way out. Its sky has no skyline and no still stars: a glow lies ahead and streaks of light rush back past the view, the near ones bare paper (`RisoBackground._warp`). Nothing grows there (no decor or fireflies).
  - **Tests:** `tests/hyperspace_test.gd` covers the addresses, generation (determinism, ends, lanterns, thorns, every one crossable), travel in and out, the landing level's two ways up, and the worlds map. `tests/capture_hyperspace.gd` takes stills.
  - **Worlds map:** hyperspace, like every side world, is a plain pink spline (`RisoMap.SPLINE_W`, a little bolder while you are in it) on a smooth curve out of the middle of the bottom of the level it is entered from and into the middle of the top of the one it drops to. It leaves and arrives straight down, so it reads as poured from one into the other; in between it bows through a point in the gutter between columns, so it runs past the levels it skips rather than over them (away from the landing's column when it drifts a world sideways). The cursor's frame round it is open at the ends. It stands in for the link bar between them, is cut off at the page's edge rather than left out when it runs past it, and the cursor follows its curve; legend rows are named after the kind ("hyperspace", "hyperspace door"). `tests/capture_hyperspace.gd` takes stills.
- **Room for big things:** each kind of thing that prints taller or wider than its cell declares its box in cells (`"size"` in `Placeables.TABLE`: exits, portals, ink wells, lanterns and relics 1 × 2, the shrine 2 × 2, counting up from its own cell). Once any place is laid out (after its definition's `populate`, so hyperspace and future worlds alike), the rock and thorns in every such box are carved out (`LevelGen.make_room`), so nothing prints into the rock over it. Left alone: a secret room's rock and the rock sealing it, a vault's walls, rock framing a door or switch gate above or below it, and rock a laser is set in. A new big thing only needs its size in its `Placeables` entry. `tests/room_test.gd` checks 44 places.
- **Relics won from masters** (the institute only): an institute level holding a relic has it carried by the master out in the level furthest from the way in (`Institute.populate`, `LevelGen.relic_carrier`, `Scholar.relic`; no draw), not laid in a secret room, a bone vault or the open. The carrier wears it as a small medallion over its pips, and the map marks it as the level's relic. Beaten by the wizard's own blow, it leaves the relic where it fell instead of its lesson, free (the fight was its price), kept in the level's record until taken (`MapInfo.relic_left`, `LevelRecord.relics_left`); taken, it is the level's relic found (`left_relic_taken`), and the master carries it no more (`relic_carried`). An instructor teaching levitate or astral to a wizard who has not found it leaves that move's relic instead of a paper, once a room (`instructor_fell`); that one is not the level's relic. Every other band keeps its relics in secret rooms and bone vaults. `tests/master_relic_test.gd` covers it.
- **Secret rooms and relics:** every level has secret rooms (0.6 per 1000 cells, at least one; `LevelGen.place_secrets`, placed last): a pocket of rock, 4, 3 or 2 cells across and 2 high, with rock under it (wholly in rock where there is room), entered at floor height from a floor beside it through a false wall. That floor and the rock under it are kept as they are by what is placed after the rooms (a vault or strongbox is not built on them, nor are they cut away to make room for big things; `LevelGen.secret_steps`), so every room can be walked into. The room and its false wall are cracked cells holding the room's number, drawn and mapped as plain rock, even on an inked map. The false wall can be walked and shot straight through by the wizard, but enemies take it for rock (it is on a physics layer only `enemy.tscn`'s mask holds, `LevelLoader.ENEMY_WALL`), so none wanders in and stands in the "rock", giving the room away (`secrets_test`); stepping into it, a hex bolt striking the rock behind it, an astral projection drifting into it or a warp landing in it opens the whole room for good (`MapInfo.open_secret`; the record keeps it open), and its rewards appear: up to 3 stars, and the level's relic if it has one. **Relics** (`Relics.gd`, `Relic.gd`): tier I of the five relic moves (double jump, wall climb, blink, levitate, astral projection) only comes from a relic; shrines offer their higher tiers once the move is known. From depth 3 (`Relics.MIN_DEPTH`), 5 % of levels hold one, rising a point every two levels to 8 % (`Relics.chance`, `CHANCE`, `CHANCE_MAX`; so finding one means going out of your way; every level in a debug run), a move dealt by the seed, in a secret room (on an open floor if no room fits). Interact and pay to take it, a lot: 4 times the deeper exit's price in its level (`Relics.price`; 88 stars at depth 3). It teaches the move's next tier; a relic whose move takes a slot hands back what it replaces as an exam paper (`MapInfo.put_back`), as a shrine does, and is gone once taken. It prints as a medallion (an accent ring round a paper disc with the move's mark) over a small plinth. A relic for a move already known teaches its next tier, and its pop-up says "upgrade" ("already mastered" at the top tier). At full health, when there is nothing to mend, a **shrine's** third station sells either a skeleton key (§3, "Bone gates") or the whereabouts of the nearest relic neither found nor already marked (searching 12 levels across and down, `Relics.SEARCH`; 1.5 times the deeper price, `Relics.hint_price`; buying it spends the shrine, like mending). It shows that relic's move on a small medallion, and the relic is marked on the worlds map: on an unvisited level, a faint tile you can pick and open, whose page shows where the relic waits; one off the page is pinned to its edge with a chevron pointing the way. `tests/secrets_test.gd` and `tests/phasing_test.gd` cover them; `tests/capture_secrets.gd` takes stills.
- **Crossings left to relics:** deeper down, levels are likely to need a relic move (double jump, blink, levitate, wall climb) to cross fully. The level is laid out as always, then these are taken away, so nothing else moves. Too wide to jump with the dash, they are crossed with a relic move. The way back is always on the arrival side, so nothing is shut in.
  - **Hyperspace** that drops deep (`relic_need` of the depth it lands at, so from a door at depth 2): one stretch 6 cells wide (`Hyperspace.RELIC_GAP`) loses the lifts, ledges and moons laid across it, but only where the rough reach then cannot cross and a relic's longer reach (7 cells a hop, `RELIC_ACROSS`) can (`Hyperspace._relic_gap`, kept in `w.relic_gaps`). Where no stretch qualifies, it stays bridged.
  - `tests/relics_needed_test.gd` covers them.
- **Standing room:** the things you stand at to use them (exits, the shrine, lanterns, the ink well, relics, bells, switches, teleporters; the "stander" flag in `Placeables.TABLE`) always keep something under them. Where the rock under one is broken (a cracked wall struck, a secret room opened), a ledge (a one-way platform) appears in its place at once, and again whenever the level loads (`MapInfo.prop_up`). `tests/floor_test.gd` covers it.
- **Moons** are dash resets, with a target of 3 per 1000 level cells, placed only in open air: clear on every side and below, and at least 6 cells apart. They reject platforms in the fall below, including the full width and travel of moving platforms, until intervening rock or spikes block the fall. Suitable spots above spikes have triple the selection weight. Touching one spends it at once (it shows as a sliver), whether or not you have used your dash. While you stay inside it gives your dash back, even mid-dash, as often as you use it. Once you leave it waxes back over 2.5 s. Moons are never used up.

**Cast costs:** warp and rift cost stars each cast (`Abilities.ABILITIES` "cost", `cast_price`): `round(base × 1.25^d)`, at least 1, with base 4 for warp and 2 for rift (warp 4 at depth 0, 12 at depth 5). A cast is only made when the stars are there and is paid only if it works (a warp still recharging costs nothing). The spell orb goes dim while you can't afford it, and a shrine's pop-up for them shows the price of a cast under the price to learn. The other spells are free: their cost is a cooldown, charges or mend's draughts.

Spawn budgets follow level area: keys 1 (at least 2, §3), corridor gates 3, extra lanterns 0.4 (at least one), moons 3, and cracked walls 2.5 per 1000 cells (and teleporter pairs 0.75, the two ends of a pair at least a quarter of the level's width and height together apart, at least 12 cells, `LevelGen.portal_apart`; a pair with no floor that far is not placed), and from depth 1 hoppers 2 per 1000 cells (placed last, on floors, so the rest of a level is laid out as before). A hopper (`Hopper.gd`) squats until it sees the wizard within 700 × 400 px with a clear line, crouches for 0.45 s, then leaps (about two cells high) at where the wizard is, resting 1.1 s after each landing; the hex wounds it like the other nightmares, and the parry catches and stuns it. `tests/hazards_test.gd` covers hoppers and lasers. Key colours are dealt by rarity (§3); exits, their lanterns, the shrine and ink well are reserved first. Natural teleporters use 0.75 pairs per 1000 cells, rounded with a minimum of one pair and no fixed cap: one pair at depth 0, two at depth 5 and in the largest levels. Coins, platforms and enemies use a fraction of available space. Terrain and spacing constraints can leave budgets unfilled.

**Difficulty presets** (`Difficulty`, picked in the F7 panel's Difficulty section; a game starts on **Hard**). Each preset scales how many foes a level is dressed with against Normal (`Difficulty.foes`: Normal 1, Hard 1.4, Brutal 1.8) how fast the bosses act (`Difficulty.haste`: 1, 1.15, 1.3; `REGIONS_PLAN.md` §6), how often the other enemies attack (`Difficulty.attack`: 1, 1.2, 1.4: a watcher's shots, a hopper's crouch and its rest between leaps, a bird's tell and its rest between swoops, a wisp's wind-up and its rests around a lurch, a skeleton's wind-up and rests around a swipe, a wraith's swell and rests around a dart, a nest's hatching all come that much sooner) and, a little, how fast they move (`Difficulty.speed`: 1, 1.08, 1.15: a wisp's walk, a wraith's drift, a bird's patrol, a moth swarm, a rock-bug's crawl; leaps, dives and shots keep their speeds). The foes scaled are the wisps and watchers placed by fraction of the free space, the hoppers, the cemetery's moths, sleep fog and wraiths, the crags' rock-bugs, stalactites and nests, the most crossbows a tower holds (`CROSSBOWS_MOST` × the preset, rounded down) and the keeps' crossbows' share, and the sky's extra watchers and birds (`LevelGen.foes_per_area` for the per-area counts). Normal lays every level out exactly as before the presets; a harder preset changes what levels hold, and since the extra foes draw from the world RNG, the rest of the dressing after them moves too (the terrain does not). A level is still the same on every visit under one preset; the loader keeps layouts per preset (`LevelLoader._world_key`). Changing the preset lays the current place out again, arriving at its way back (`MapInfo.lay_out_again`); what the run noted of the place was noted of the old layout, so it may not all line up (a debug convenience). The preset lasts while the game runs and is not saved. `tests/difficulty_test.gd` covers it.

Watching eyes detect within 1400 pixels (previously 1150, and 520 before that); only rock and the wizard block their line of sight, not stars or other areas. `tests/spawn_balance_test.gd` checks 20 levels across five seeds and both terrain families, plus platform rejection and spike weighting. `tests/rift_test.gd` checks placed-pair lifecycle, spell tiers and actual watcher sight physics.

## 4b. Archetypes: the garden, the cemetery and the sky

Every ordinary level has an archetype (`NextWorldDef.archetype`), dealt by its row (`docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §2): the garden (the caves a run starts in) is the hub, rows −3 to +3 (`GARDEN_ROWS`); down from it the cemetery (rows 4–9) then the institute (10–15, and on below); up from it the crags (−4 to −9) then the sky (−10 to −15, and on above), `BAND` (6) rows each (`NextWorldDef.DOWN`, `UP`). The crags (§4d) and the institute (§4e) have first passes of their own. `NextWorldDef.first_depth` gives a band's row nearest the start and `band_row(kind, k)` its k-th row away from it. Every seed's column passes through each. Side worlds have none. A garden level is printed in the garden's own realm (`RisoPrint.REALMS.garden`: green rock and sun-gold stars under a deep green night, on a warm paper), switched to on arrival as the cemetery's is (so the F8 realms only preview until the next level); it is collapsed from the tunnels sample (`GardenArchetype.SAMPLE`; the floating islands it used to take every other band are the sky's now).

**Darkness** (every band, `Archetype.shade` and `gloom`, printed by `RisoLight`): rock stops light and the wizard's sight. What they cannot see past the rock lies under night ink (`shade`: 0.3). A band may also be deep dark all over (`gloom`: everything no light falls on, thinner in each light's outer pool and gone in its inner one); none is now (the catacombs were, until the institute took their band), and the deep dark lies inside the cemetery's crypts instead. Side worlds are shaded as the garden is. See `docs/RISO_PRINT.md` (line of sight); `tests/sight_test.gd` covers it.

**Hedges** (the garden, `RisoDecor.shrub_runs`): about half the stretches of floor at least 2 cells long have a hedge along them (dealt apart from the fences, so one often stands behind a fence), in lengths of 2–5 cells with a gap between: a plain band of moss leafage with a row of rounded tops along it, shaded at its foot, as long as its run, printed behind everything else in the decor (`HEDGE_SLOTS`, on a canvas of their own). They replace the garden's single bushes and shrubs.

**Background fences** (both archetypes, `RisoDecor.fence_runs`): about half the stretches of floor at least 3 cells long have a fence along them, in lengths of up to 6 cells with a gap between, printed in front of the garden's hedges and behind the rest of the decor in a lighter ink (`BACK_SLOTS`, on a canvas of their own): white wooden pickets over two rails in the garden (bare paper; now and then one missing or leaning), iron railings between stone posts in a cemetery.

**The cemetery** is a hillside graveyard:

- **Size and edges:** a sky level is 1.8 times as wide and 1.6 times as tall as a cave level of its depth (`SkyArchetype.SCALE`; 108 × 77 cells at depth 12), with no border rock anywhere (`LevelLoader.lay_terrain`, `Archetype.open`): open sky to the sides and above, the camera going 6 cells past the edges (`SKY_MARGIN`) so the sky beyond shows, and a drop below.
- **Terrain:** collapsed from its own sample, `wfc_images/graveyard.png` (drawn by `tests/make_graveyard_sample.gd`): stacked terraces of rock that run across the level, stepping up and down a cell or two at a time, dotted with grave mounds and two- or three-wide mausoleums, with open graves (a pit with thorns at the bottom) and one drop per terrace to the one below. It is collapsed with no symmetry (`NextWorldDef.symmetry` 1; the levels' samples use 5, which turns patterns, and the extension's first flip is upside down), so up stays up.
- **Crypts** (`Crypts.gd`, `docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §7): one a level, or two (`TWO_SHARE`, 60 %, dealt by the seed) in a level of at least `TWO_AREA` (2400) cells. Each is dug into the hillside as the crags' towers stand on the cliff, but bigger: its inside is collapsed by WFC from a sample of its own (`wfc_images/crypt.png`, drawn by `tests/make_crypt_sample.gd`: four levels of ossuary rooms five rows high joined by burial passages, low squeezes with niches over their mouths, and stairs between; wide rooms hold ledges a hop over the floor and another over that, and now and then a room is a hall two levels high, open through to the level over it and climbed by ledges a hop apart, with a perch near its top), 42 × 14 cells for the first and 30 × 11 for the second (`INSIDES`), collapsed with the level's terrain (`Archetype.patches`; `WaveFunctionCollapse.generate_level` hands them to the layout after the terrain's columns, `LevelGen.patches`), and with them each crypt's **trials** (below). Before it is set in, the bits of its inside the collapse left apart from the rest (of at least `JOIN_MIN`, 4, open cells) are joined to the biggest by stairs carved through the rock, a step up or down for each cell across, with `STAIR_AIR` (3) rows of air over each step, two wide where they go straight up (`Crypts._joined`), never through a trial; and every shaft a cell wide and `SHAFT` (3) or more high is made two wide, so steps fit in it (`_widened`). Where the whole inside fits nowhere it is cut back, from the right to just past each trial but the last and to 30 and 22 cells across (`CROPS`), and from the top to 11 and 9 rows (`CROP_HEIGHTS`), the biggest first, keeping the trials still whole in it (in 48 test levels, all but one have a crypt). Right after the chasms are cut (`CemeteryArchetype.cut_gates`) each is set into the level, framed in a wall of masonry a cell thick, where it walls over the least open air and needs the shortest passages, kept `APART` (3) from the chasms' air, other crypts and anything kept whole; then the caves are joined again round it. It is entered by a doorway a cell high through a side wall at a floor inside, with a passage two rows high carved out through the rock to open air (at most `DOOR_REACH`, 7 cells): on the side with the shorter passage, and on the other side too where one fits; on each side, at the floor from which the most of the inside's footholds are both got to and got back from (`_reach_in`, a rough reach over the inside alone, so a doorway high over a drop no longer wins), then the shortest passage. What the collapse cut off from the doorways is filled with rock, and so is any pit inside that the wizard could drop into but not hop out of (`Reach`), until there are none (`_fill_pits`), but for a pit whose filling would cut air off from the doorways (it is left, and not counted a floor: so a crypt may hold a drop the wizard cannot climb back out of, as the owner's rule allows), and then whatever that filling cut off. A trial counts as crossed: a cell of it reached, all of it is (`_hops`). Where footholds are still out of reach high over a reached floor (a tall room with nothing to climb), steps are set in: a cell of rock a hop over the reached floor, a cell toward the foothold or else the other way if the foothold is a hop from its top, the nearest first, never in a trial, and taken out again if it would cut air off from the doorways, until none is needed or `STEPS` (24) are in (`_step`). Its stone, inside and passages are kept whole and out of the free cells, so nothing else is placed in it. The level's star cluster is brought in to the first crypt's deepest floor (in hops from a doorway; keeping its place among the level's objects, as a crag tower's key is), and a second crypt holds a hoard of `HOARD` (6) stars there instead; each holds skeletons asleep on its floors, niches first (one for each `SKELETONS_PER`, 9, of its floors, at least 2, more on a harder preset), and stars to find, one for each `STARS_PER` (5) of its floors, kept `STARS_APART` (3) cells apart, on its ledges first and then the floors furthest from a doorway (`Crypts._stars`); and, laid before the sleepers and the stars, alternating thorns along its floors (`Crypts._thorns`): along each stretch of free floor at least `THORN_STRETCH` (2) cells long, a cell clear at each end (at its start in a stretch of two) and none within `THORN_CLEAR` (2) cells of a doorway, the thorns take turns cell by cell (`THORN_BLOCK`, 1, counted on across the stretches), each out for `THORN_UP` (45 %) of the thorns' cycle and the next half a cycle on, so one is down while the next is out, their phase hashed from the seed. A snuffing font stands on a floor by each doorway outside (within `FONT_REACH`, 5 cells, of its step, or `FONT_FAR`, 10, where the doorway opens onto a drop), and another on the floor inside nearest each doorway (none in a level dealt no fonts, below). The steps set in to climb a tall room are kept (`"stairs"`), for the sun and shade blocks laid last. Inside it is completely dark (`Crypts.DARK`, 0.94: `RisoLight` lays deep dark over the inside alone, whatever the band), so nothing shows but what a light lights; its walls print as dressed stone and its insides hold only urns, rubble, broken columns and cobwebs (`RisoDecor.plan_cemetery`). Nothing here draws from the world RNG. The necromancer's gate level keeps its first crypt for the necromancer (`docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §6) and its graves, with no cluster, sleepers or trials. `tests/crypt_test.gd` covers it.
- **Trials** (`Crypts._set_trials`, `TRIALS`, samples drawn by `tests/make_crypt_trials.gd`): before a crypt's inside is joined up, trial chambers are set into it left to right (from `TRIAL_MARGIN`, 2 or 3 columns in, `TRIAL_GAP`, 2 to 4, apart), each collapsed by WFC from a sample of its own, so each is laid out afresh but keeps its sample's rules: the first crypt gets all three kinds, the second two (`TRIAL_COUNTS`), in an order dealt by the seed. They are collapsed **by slices** (`WaveFunctionCollapse`, a spec's `"slices"`): every column (a pit, a crawl) or row (a well) of the sample becomes one colour of a strip three cells thick, the strip is collapsed and each colour turned back into its column or row, so the other way keeps the sample's order and only the order of the slices is collapsed, three at a time (whole, these rigid samples settled about one time in 200; by slices, every time). An **ossuary pit** (14 × 7, on the inside's floor): a floor of thorns under a ceiling, crossed on pillars one or two across, their tops one to three rows over the thorns (the tallest right under the ceiling, so it is left by a dash, not a jump), now and then a stone standing on nothing, never more than two thorns side by side, and over some gaps between low pillars a tooth of rock hanging from the ceiling with thorns under it, crossed low by a dash. A **thorn crawl** (12 × 5, at a height dealt by the seed): a passage two rows high, thorns hanging from its ceiling in runs of one to three and dug into its floor one or two wide, now and then a step a row up. A **bone well** (6 across, as tall as the inside): a shaft four cells across climbed by ledges on alternate sides two rows apart, thorns on the walls between. Each is framed so it opens at its ends onto a floor (a pit's and a crawl's ends; a well's foot, open right across with a ledge a hop over it, and over its topmost ledge on each side), with a mouth carved out from each opening up to `MOUTH_REACH` (3) cells into the rest of the inside. Their thorns are always out. Nothing else is laid in them but a star where each is hardest to reach (`_lures`: on a free cell with thorns beside or under it, nearest its middle, or highest in a well) and, only where the floors outside are too few, a sleeper. Laid last (`Crypts.phase`, `_phase_trials`), each is worked by light with sun and shade blocks, the kinds taking turns from one dealt by the seed (`STONE_DEAL`): each of a pit's pillars keeps its right column as one stone at its top, a block standing on nothing over the thorns (so a pillar a cell across is a stone and a wider one keeps rock to rest on); a well's ledges are of one kind on the left wall and the other on the right (but its foot and the ledges its head exits open over), so every hop up switches the light; a crawl is shut by `CRAWL_GATES` (2) gates of blocks a passage high (one high under hanging thorns). The light is switched by a dash with darkness carried (`MapInfo.dash_switch`), so the trials spend the fonts' charges. Each is noted in the crypt's `"trials"`. `tests/crypt_test.gd` covers them (each kind met, thorny, worked with blocks); `tests/capture_crypt_trials.gd` takes stills of each trial and prize, and of whole crypts with their darkness lifted (`RisoLight.crypts_dark`).
- **The reliquary** (`Crypts._prize`, `prize_kind`): past the cluster or hoard, on the deepest free floor left, each furnished crypt holds a prize dealt by the seed (`PRIZE_DEAL`): the **resurrection rite** in `RITE_SHARE` (15 %), never twice in a level (an exam paper of resurrect at tier I, the only way that spell is first found; see Resurrect below), else a **skeleton key** in `SKELETON_KEY_SHARE` (half) of the rest, else a **large font** (a snuffing font holding `Snuffer.LARGE`, 8, charges, its cell's extra info `{"charges": 8}`; it stays in a level dealt no fonts). Over 2000 seeds the rite was the prize of 599 of 4000 crypts.
- **Resurrect** (`Resurrect.gd`, a utility spell, `"found": true` in `Abilities.ABILITIES`, so `Abilities.found_first`): no shrine offers it and the institute does not teach it until the wizard has it (`MapInfo.teaches`); its tier I comes only from a crypt's rite, learned at a lantern as any exam paper is, and shrines then teach tiers II and III. Press Utility standing on something to bind the soul there: a grave mark rises (`GraveMark`, `prefabs/grave_mark.tscn`, `GraveMarkArt`), and any earlier mark goes. A death while the mark stands in the level being played, with a rite left, is undone (`Player.die` asks `Resurrect.rise` first): the wizard rises at the mark at full health, in the arrival grace (`Player.grace`), stars, keys, darkness and lantern as they were, the rite spent and the mark gone; the mark is their footing, so thorns after send them back to it. One rite a tier (`Player.resurrect_rites`, saved with the run), refilled at a lantern as mend's draughts are (`MapInfo.light_lantern`) and on learning a tier. Leaving the level loses the mark. It moves the wizard only out of a death, and hurts nothing. Its mark is a headstone with a spark rising off it (`RisoGlyph`). `tests/resurrect_test.gd` covers it.
- **Light** (`Lamplight.gd`), as the game reads it, apart from how it is printed: light falls on a point within `LIT_SHARE` (0.85) of the printed reach of the wizard's carried flame (while it is lit: while a lantern protects them) or of a lantern not burnt out (the respawn lantern's, or the faint one of any other), and only where the flame sees it past the rock. The moon over the terraces and the small things that shine are no light here. The wraiths freeze in light, the skeletons wake in it, and the necromancer can be wounded only in it.
- **Light gates** (`LightGate.gd`, `light_gate.tscn`, `LightGateArt`): no longer laid; the gate's own code is kept. A crypt's doorway may be shut by a sun or shade block instead (below, `Crypts._door_blocks`), dealt by the seed for each doorway as the gates were (`DOOR_DEAL`): a shade block, passed lit, in 30 % (`SHADE_DOOR_SHARE`, where a sun gate stood), a sun block, passed dark, in 35 % (`SUN_DOOR_SHARE`, where a shade gate stood), the rest open; a second crypt's may be ossuary doors instead. So a crypt is entered lit, waking its sleepers, or in the dark. As it was: a portcullis worked by light in a crypt's doorway. A sun gate is up only while light falls on it (`Lamplight`, looked for just either side of it: the wizard's lit flame, a lantern's light), a shade gate only while none does, so it is passed with the flame snuffed and shuts on a lit one. It keeps no record, follows the light from moment to moment, and drops only once the wizard is clear of it. Its plate shows a sun or a crescent moon; the map marks it the same way. While down it stops light and sight (`MapInfo.light_gates`, `solid_at`). `tests/crypt_test.gd` covers it, `tests/capture_light_gates.gd` takes stills.
- **Snuffing fonts** (`Snuffer.gd`, `snuffer.tscn`): a stone basin of black water by each crypt doorway, and more about the terraces (`CemeteryArchetype.scatter_fonts`, laid last: `FONTS_PER_K`, 2.5, on free floors with room over them, each at least `FONT_APART`, 9 cells, from another font and `FONT_CLEAR`, 4, from the way in, their places hashed from the level seed, no RNG), marked on the map. Interact to douse the flame in it and take all the darkness it holds (`MapInfo.douse_font`): `Snuffer.CHARGES` (3) in a font never touched (`Snuffer.LARGE`, 8, in a crypt's large one, its prize; `Snuffer.capacity`), carried as charges (below) and their flame goes out (`MapInfo.snuff`) until a dash or a lantern lights it again; their lantern still protects them all the while (`MapInfo.flame_lit`: lit while protected and not snuffed). Taken, a font is dry (`LevelRecord.fonts`, the charges each holds by cell, 0 when dry), through a death and every visit after, its basin empty with a stain ring, and marked faint on the map. A dry font is also a store: interact at it while carrying darkness and the wizard pours every charge back in (`MapInfo.fill_font`), the flame left as it was; the record keeps them there, out of reach of the dash and of a death, and the next douse takes them all back (prompts `douse · n` and `pour · n`). A font still holding darkness takes none, so to gather it into one font, take it first. Some cemetery levels have no fonts at all (`Rules.fontless_at`: `FONTLESS_SHARE`, 30 %, dealt by the place; never the band's first row, the necromancer's gate level or a side world; `CemeteryArchetype.dry_up` takes away the fonts the crypts laid, but a large one, and none are scattered), so their blocks are worked only on darkness carried in. Snuffed, the sleeping skeletons stay asleep and the moths ignore the wizard, but the wraiths hunt them and in a crypt they see almost nothing. A snuffed lantern is drawn apart from an unprotected one (`MapInfo.flame_snuffed`): dark glass with a pale crescent and a cold blue glow rather than the pink ember (`docs/RISO_PRINT.md`); the font's water glows, shines and gives off rising motes while there is a flame to put out, and rings and smoke rise from it just after one is put out there (`Snuffer.dipped`); just after darkness is poured back in, the water wells up from the middle of the basin with rings spreading (`Snuffer.filled`). `tests/capture_snuffer.gd` takes stills.
- **Sneaking** (`Lamplight.notice`, `SNEAK` 0.6): while the wizard's flame is out (snuffed at a font, or no lantern protecting them: `MapInfo.flame_lit`), the enemies that notice them from afar notice them only 60 % as far off: a watcher's sight (1,400 px) and a crossbow's (9 cells), a hopper's (700 × 400 px), a bird's reach before it swoops (320 px across), a scholar's sight (its kind's, `Scholar.SIGHT`, for turning on the wizard and for being roused by seeing them where they do not belong), and an abomination's sense (760 px). Close tells (a wisp's lurch, a skeleton's swipe) and the spawners' waking (breaches, nests) are as ever; a wraith hunts only a wizard whose flame is out and a skeleton wakes only in light, so they are unchanged, as are the bosses. `tests/sneak_test.gd`.
- **Darkness charges** (`RunState.dark_charges`, saved; the owner's idea, worked through in the cross-world puzzle notes): the darkness the wizard carries, gathered at fonts and spent by dashing. In every world a dash switches the light by `Rules.dash_dark` (`MapInfo.dash_switch`, from `Player`'s dash): out of the dark it lights the flame and spends a charge; while lit it puts the flame out again if a charge is left; with none, it only lights the flame, as a dash always did. So from a font's 3 on top of 4: dark 7, lit 6, dark 6, lit 5, … lit 0, and lit after that. Darkness carried out of the cemetery is spent the same way, so it dwindles with every other dash; to keep it, pour it into a dry font first (above). There is no cap. Lighting a lantern lights the flame and leaves the charges alone. A death drops them into the ghost with the stars (`RunState.ghost_charges`), and taking the ghost back returns them. A charge spent flares (`Lamplight.FLARE_TIME`, 0.6 s): the flame reaches `FLARE_REACH` (1.5) times as far, rising over `FLARE_RISE` and easing back over `FLARE_FADE` (`Lamplight.carried_reach`, which `RisoLight` prints), so wraiths freeze and sleepers wake further out. The lantern carried shows them as beads under its base, up to 5 (`RisoCostume.CHARGE_BEADS`), and the sitting card counts them. `unit_test` checks the switch and the fontless deal; `tests/darkness_test.gd` the rest in play.
- **Sun and shade blocks** (`PhaseBlock.gd`, `phase_block.tscn`, `PhaseBlockArt`, `LevelGen.Type.PHASE_BLOCK`): a cell of dressed stone solid only to a wizard in the right light: a sun block while their flame burns, a shade block while it is out; the other kind is a ghost of itself they pass through. It turns solid only once the wizard is clear of it (as a light gate drops), stops only the wizard (its own physics layer, 11, `Phase`, which only the wizard looks at; not an astral projection), never light or sight, and keeps no record. Besides those dealt into a crypt's doorways as it is built (Light gates, above), laid last of all in a cemetery level, inside the crypts only, hashed from the level seed and cells with no world RNG draw (`Crypts.phase`): a crypt's steps, where it has at least `STAIRS_MIN` (2), each become a block, the kinds taking turns up the climb (a step with something on it stays rock); over each run of its alternating thorns, by a share of `SHADE_FLOOR_SHARE` (50 %), a floor of shade blocks a cell up, walked dark over the thorns or passed under lit, timing them; up to `PHASE_WALLS` (1) of its passages a cell or two high shut by a block of either kind; and by a share of `POCKET_SHARE` (50 %), one of its stars walled in sun blocks wherever it is open round it. The necromancer's crypt has none inside (its doorways are dealt as any). The map marks them, a blue square with an accent dot or a night one. Across 32 test levels 27 had blocks.
- **Ossuary doors** (`OssuaryDoor.gd`, `ossuary_door.tscn`, `OssuaryArt`): in `Rules.OSSUARY_SHARE` (40 %) of cemetery levels with two crypts (never the gate level, `Rules.ossuary_at`), the second crypt's doorways (the hoard's) are doors of stacked skulls. Interact to pour in the charges carried (`MapInfo.pour_ossuary`), as many as it still wants: the record keeps what was poured, by the crypt, shared by its doorways (`LevelRecord.poured`), and it opens for good once it has had its fill, `Rules.ossuary_fill`: 6 in the band's first row, then 8, 9, 11 and 12 in the row before the gate level (`OSSUARY_FILL`), more than one level's fonts usually give. Shut, it stops light and sight (`MapInfo.ossuary_open`, `solid_at`). Its skulls show the fill, one each, bright with darkness in their sockets once fed, the darkness standing behind them as high as the share fed; every door is built as a lintel on two jambs, flush with the stone over the doorway (`OssuaryDoor.STYLE`; a square-topped arch and a bare wall of skulls are kept in its art to compare). Marked on the map while shut.
- **Skeletons** (`Skeleton.gd`, `skeleton_enemy.tscn`): asleep in the crypts, lying in their niches, harmless, until light falls on one; it rises (`RISE`, 0.9 s) and walks at the wizard (70 px/s, a little faster on a harder preset) along its floor, hopping up a step, stopping at a ledge unless the wizard is below; its touch hurts. It cannot be killed (its `Wound` is `unhurt`): a blow or a parry's stun knocks it to pieces, a heap of bones on the floor, harmless, for `APART` (2.6 s, after its bones take `FALL`, 0.3 s, to drop), and then it pulls itself together again (`REFORM`, 0.8 s) and comes on, awake; struck again in pieces, it only lies there. It never drops stars and is never slain. The necromancer raises the same skeletons, awake.
- **Look:** its own print realm (`RisoPrint.REALMS.cemetery`: slate rock, violet stars and light, bone paper), switched to on arrival like hyperspace's; a faint moon in the sky with clouds drifting past (flat underneath, billowing on top, `RisoBackground._cloud`); its own decor (`RisoDecor.plan_cemetery`), in pale stone (bare paper, shaded down one side): headstones in five shapes (rounded, gothic, cross-topped, broken, a flat ledger with a low stone at its head), crosses, obelisks, urns on plinths, the odd stone angel, grave flowers, bare trees and dry grass on floors; cobwebs and roots under ceilings; ivy on walls; and iron railings behind (background fences, above). Its title reads "world 28 · depth 3 · cemetery".
- **Moths** (`MothSwarm.gd`, `moths.tscn`): a swarm a few cells from each lantern and `MOTHS_PER_K` (0.6) more in open air. While a lit lantern protects the wizard, the lantern they carry burns too, and it draws the moths within 560 px (`CARRIED_PULL`), taking priority over the lit lantern itself; the spell orb draws nothing. With the wizard farther away, the lit lantern draws them if it is within 1100 px of their home; with no lantern lit (the wizard unprotected), they flutter back home. A moth touching the wizard stings like any hit. A dash or blink through the swarm, a hex bolt, or a parry scatters it for 6 s: the moths burst outward and sting no one, then gather again: while scattered they print faint, with a pink point pulsing where they will come back together. They are never killed. So the lantern that keeps you safe draws danger to it. `tests/moths_test.gd` covers the spell requirement, sleep fog, spell-orb priority, dash scattering, contact guards and regrouping.
- **Sleep fog** (`SleepFog.gd`, `sleep_fog.tscn`): `FOG_PER_K` (2.0) banks lying over floors at least 5 cells apart, each 600 × 240 px, drifting 220 px to and fro. Inside, the wizard is drowsy (`Player.is_drowsy`): the Spell button does nothing, a levitate float or an astral projection ends (back to the body), awareness stops sensing; the robe greys (`RisoPrint.DROWSY_ROBE`) and the spell orb dims. It wears off a moment after leaving.   - **Silenced:** drowsy (sleep fog, a pit's mist or a librarian's hush), a spell's button (Spell with a damage spell, Utility with a utility spell, Dash with a spell in the movement slot) casts nothing but shows it: the spell goes out at the bag's knot, a few puffs of blue smoke rising and motes of glow dropping dead, with the fizzle's dull click (`Player._presses_spell`, the "silenced" visual event, `RisoFx` "silenced"). ## 4c. The sky

The third band (depths 12–17, 30–35, ...) is clusters of floating islands of cloud, with wide gaps of open air between, over an open drop (`NextWorldDef.sky`, `SkyArchetype.populate`).

- **Terrain:** collapsed from its own sample, `wfc_images/sky_islands.png` (`SkyArchetype.SAMPLE`, drawn by `tests/make_sky_sample.gd`; no symmetry, so up stays up): islands packed close, two or three cells apart, each a flat top 4–9 cells wide over a body tapering underneath. Then the collapse is kept only in clusters (`SkyArchetype.cluster_islands`): ellipses 6–9 cells across by 4–6 down (`ISLE_RX`, `ISLE_RY`), thrown at random at least 4 cells apart side by side or 3 stacked (`ISLE_GAP`), and the rock outside them is cleared to open air. A cluster with under 42 % rock (`ISLE_ROCK`) gets more islands of the sample's shape (`_stamp_islands`), fitting smaller 3–6-cell shelves with one cell of air beside and below and two rows above, as far as that headroom allows. Then the islands taper underneath (`taper_islands`): under every stretch of an island's underside, rows of rock narrowing toward a point, a cell off each side a row (now and then a side holds), stopping before they stray past the cluster, meet anything, or come within 2 rows of rock below (`KEEL_CLEAR`); this is real rock, laid before the clusters are linked, so the causeways go round it. The clusters are linked so all can be reached (`link_isles`): along a spanning tree of the shortest links, a causeway of cloud stepping stones (`Puff`, two cells wide) from the lower floor of the pair up or across to the higher, in hops of three cells across or two across and one up (so each can be taken either way), and an updraft beside the far floor where it is too high above to hop up to (`_stones`). The causeways are kept clear of everything built after (`lanes`). A cluster no causeway can reach is cleared away, so nothing is placed out of reach. Ledges are only laid in and about the clusters, and stars near them, so the gaps stay open. Falling out of the bottom (off an edge, too, in the end) (200 px under the last row) costs a heart and puts the wizard back on the last rock they stood on in this level (`Player._footing`, `fall_back`; at the way in if none yet). Ledges, lifts and clouds don't count as footing, since they move or go.
- **Look:** its own realm, white first (`RisoPrint.REALMS.sky`: white cloud for rock, sun-gold stars under a dark, stormy slate sky, bright white paper); behind, a storm (`RisoBackground._backdrop_sky`): heavy banks of cloud in the islands' pale ink rolling the way the level's wind is set, surging in its gusts, and now and then a distant flash of lightning, a jagged bolt of bare paper with the sheet lit pale a moment; no moon or stars. Its decor (`RisoDecor.plan_sky`): grass, flowers and stones on the islands, standing stones (tall rounded slabs, leaning a little), stone piles and windsocks, shorter, scattered seeded root bundles and occasional cloud wisps; F7's Sky bottoms picks Tapered (default: each step of an island's keel smoothed on the diagonal and a point under its last cell, with a few roots), Roots, Clouds (rounded seeded ground contours without dangling props), or Clouds & roots (cloud contours with fewer roots). These are cosmetic and leave collision tiles unchanged; danglers stop before terrain and structures, vines down their sides, and behind (the sky's background fences) lines of peace flags strung between two poles standing in stone piles: triangles in turn of blue, paper, sun, moss and plum, printed so they lift the night and read in colour.

- **Gates: gaps and their vanes.** A sky level is gated by chasms as a cemetery is (`CHASMS_PER_K`, at least `CHASMS_MIN`), but its islands are too small for long floors to cut into, so it takes the gaps between islands that are already about as wide (7–12 cells, open air from the row over the floor to the row under it, two cells of floor on each side, the far floor on the same row or one lower, raised to the gust's landing height with clear headroom; `SkyArchetype._span_gaps`), then builds the rest (`SkyArchetype._build_gaps`): 8 or 10 cells of open air between two 3-cell shores, rock laid under the shores and air cleared over the gap, at the spots that change the fewest cells: across the air between two clusters where it can (a shortcut from one to the next), else by a cluster, never across a causeway. - **The level's wind, set by its vanes** (`docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §7). Every sky level has one wind, left or right, and it starts still. The vanes (`Vane.gd`, `vane.tscn`, which extends `Bell.gd`) stand where a cemetery's bells would, one each side of each gap, chained the same way (a padlock in a key colour, or a switch on its side; a switch that is on frees it, as a bell's does). Freed, interact or strike it with a hex bolt to switch it on or off (`MapInfo.switch_vane`; the record keeps `vanes_on`, by cell, and `wind_way`). The wind blows the way of the vane last switched on, as strong as the vanes on that point that way (`Rules.wind_of`); vanes on that point the other way do nothing until the wind is turned their way (by switching one of them on); switching one of the wind's way off weakens it a vane; with none of its way left on, it is still (`unit_test`). **Gusts:** the wind blows in gusts (`MapInfo.gusting`, `gust_wind`): `Wind.STILL` (3.5 s) of stillness, then a strong gust `Wind.GUST` (1 s, for one vane) long, over again, counted from arriving (`MapInfo.gust_clock`), the gap's streaks starting to stir `Wind.WARN` (1 s) before. So a gap is crossed by jumping off its shore into a gust, with the wind, and dropping onto the far shore with Down; a crossing must be made within a gust, and a gap the wind is set the wrong way across cannot be crossed until it is turned. In a gust, streaks also rush across the whole storm sky behind (`RisoBackground`). Still, a run, a jump and a dash all fall short. A vane prints as a blue post with a sun arrow and a cross of cups on top: switched on, the arrow points the way it blows and the cups turn, spinning hard in a gust; off, it idles on the breeze; it waits in a soft halo once freed.

## 4e. The institute

The last band down (rows 10 to 15, and on below): a sunken institute of magic dug into the earth, in place of the catacombs (`InstituteArchetype`; `docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §7). A first pass: its terrain, halls, realm, backdrop and decor, its people, its abominations, its departments' portals, and its timetable (below), with exam papers fetched from the departments' field levels. The eldritch beast is built (`Beast`, `docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §6): in its gate levels a chamber of marble is raised first (`Institute.CHAMBER_INSIDE`, 18 × 6, low down where it fits), holding it alone; the first time it wakes it sends its souls out (`BeastSouls`: 8, one into a visited level for every 5 levels visited, in any band and column, and the rest flung far into levels not yet visited, 3 to 12 worlds off and a band each in turn, so exploring is rewarded, never a side world or gate level; kept in `RunState.souls`, saved), each a breach of its own (`BeastSoul`, 8 blows) with 2 abominations laid round it on every visit, marked on both map pages; one of its eyes shuts for each soul destroyed, and while any lives a strike glances off it; then its great eye opens and takes 9 blows. It strikes with tentacles, up out of the floor under the wizard and, every third strike, along the floor to be jumped, each after a pink warning; a parry stuns it. `tests/beast_test.gd` covers it; `tests/capture_beast.gd` takes stills.

- **Terrain:** collapsed with no symmetry from its own sample, `wfc_images/institute.png` (drawn by `tests/make_institute_sample.gd`, 40 × 32): storeys of rooms three rows high with floors two rows thick between, walls a cell or two thick parting the rooms (most with a doorway two rows high at the floor), tall halls through two storeys, stair holes with a step under them, stairs of steps a row high, and a corner of bare earth with a winding passage. Levels are a cave level's size (60 × 48 at these depths).
- **Halls** (`Institute.build`, right after the caves are joined, no world RNG: sites hashed from the level seed): buildings of pale marble (`LevelGen.masonry`) a cell thick all round, the air inside them printed over dark panelling (`LevelGen.interiors`), each at the site walling over the least open air and needing the shortest passages (as a crypt's), entered by a doorway a cell high through each side wall with a passage two rows high carved out to open air (at most `DOOR_REACH`, 8 cells; a side that finds none has no doorway), kept whole and out of the free cells so nothing else is laid in them, and noted in `LevelGen.halls`. Every level has one library (`LIBRARY_INSIDE`, 18 × 8: galleries of one-way ledges every 3 rows, `GALLERY`, all across but for a stair gap 2 cells wide at alternating ends with a ledge under it, so each storey is climbed in two hops; under its lowest gallery two book ladders, `BOOK_LADDERS`, rolling ladders (*Rolling ladders* below) hanging from the gallery's edge to the floor, their steps level with the gallery, side by side from a cell past its stair gap, each rolling over 6 cells, `BOOK_TRACK`, a cell apart, laid by `Institute._book_ladders` in every library) and up to five classrooms, the lecture halls (`LECTURE_INSIDE`, 10 × 5, `LECTURES`: a flat floor with the lectern at its front end, dealt by the seed, `front`, and a gallery of one-way ledges all across it two rows up, `GALLERY_UP`, where the students sit; a doorway through each end wall at the floor and at the gallery where a passage finds open air, at least 2, `MIN_DOORS`, each doorway past those counting 12 open cells for a site, `DOOR_WORTH`, so a classroom is a way through rather than a dead end). In 18 test levels each had all its halls: five classrooms a level, 3.6 doorways each. Last of all, in every level with a library or a classroom, the bell room (`BELFRY_INSIDE`, 3 × 4): a little marble room of its own for the great bell, the level's one (*The bells and their cords*), sited as the halls are, with a doorway at its floor through each side wall where a passage finds open air. It is no hall: it is kept in `LevelGen.belfry`, not `LevelGen.halls`, so the timetable reads it as the corridors (the wizard belongs there in passing time). `institute_test` covers the book ladders and the bell room.
- **The way on** stands in a classroom (`Institute.way_on`, `Timetable.way_on_hall`): the one the most classrooms in from the way in, counting the classrooms crossed to reach its doors through the corridors (`Institute.rooms_in`, `regions`: open air parted by rock and classroom doors only), the furthest from the way in of those, on its floor 7 cells from its lectern's wall (`WAY_ON_SEAT`). The spot drawn for it the usual way is passed over, so nothing else in the level moves; nothing is drawn. Not in the beast's level, whose way on stays where it was laid. That classroom opens on its class's own prerequisite like any other; the way on itself takes an exam paper (not a spell held) of any department but its classroom's at II or higher as the wizard goes through (`Timetable.WAY_ON_PAPER`), so getting through means sitting classes: the lowest-tier one it would take, oldest first, off the key ring, and it asks for none again in that level (`MapInfo.way_on_due`, `hand_in_way_on`, `LevelRecord.way_on_paid`; `LevelExit`); without one it lets no one through. While it would take one, the paper hangs beside its arch: a bare sheet with a pip for each tier (`ExitArt`). Nothing makes sure the paper can be had in the level: some ways through run through other levels. A classroom's doors (`ExamDoor.gd`, `exam_door.tscn`, `LevelGen.Type.EXAM_DOOR`, the name kept from the exam it was made for) are portcullises with no lock, kept by the timetable (below). Shut, they stop sight and light (`MapInfo.exam_doors`, `RisoLight`).
- **The timetable** (`Timetable.gd`, `SchoolBell.gd`, `Lectern.gd`, exam papers on `KeyRing.gd`; `school_bell.tscn`, `lectern.tscn`; `LevelGen.Type.SCHOOL_BELL`, `LECTERN`; `MapInfo` "the timetable"; `docs/REGIONS_PLAN.md` §9): the band's puzzle. There is no clock: nothing in it happens until the great bell is rung or the wizard goes somewhere.
  - *Rings and periods.* The bells and the classes go passing time, class time, passing time, class time. A level is first met before any ring, in passing time (`LevelRecord.passing`). Any bell rung in passing time begins class time (`MapInfo.pull_cord`, `_class_time`): a wizard then in a classroom by right (gone in enrolled, `MapInfo.class_hall`), its class in session, attends it (`LevelRecord.started`, `class_ready`); anywhere else they are truant. Going in starts nothing. Class time shuts every classroom both ways (*Hall doors*, below): a wizard in a class stays in it until a bell rings the period out. Any bell rung in class time does (`ring_period`): a wizard in a classroom rings its own cord to be let out, and the class begun there is attended (*Exam papers*, below); one truant in the halls (or in the library) rings any other, nothing attended. Either way the level moves on a period (`LevelRecord.rings`; a day is 3 periods, `Timetable.PERIODS`, marked on the great bell's yoke) and passing time begins, the doors opening. One who gets into a class in session in class time (a warp, a rift; its doors are shut to them) is late: no longer truant, but attending nothing, and shut in with the class. One who gets out of a class in class time the same ways forfeits it (`MapInfo._left_class`, `LevelRecord.started` cleared) and is truant until a bell rings; walking out of a classroom no longer ends anything. No cord can be rung twice running (`LevelRecord.last_bell`, `MapInfo.bell_ready`, `SchoolBell.spent`), as a crags tower flipped last cannot be flipped again, so the same classroom's class cannot be begun two periods running; the one exception is a classroom's own cord letting out the class it began (`MapInfo.dismissing`). None rings within 1 s of the last ring (`SchoolBell.RING_GAP`).
  - *Departments, their cycles and their tiers* (`Timetable.LADDERS`, `IN_SESSION`, `HOLDS`, `phase`, `in_cycle`, `meets`, `subject`, `climb`; `LevelRecord.dept_tiers`; `MapInfo.dept_tiers`, `_meeting`, `ring_period`). Each department has three subjects: astronomy hex, seeker, warp; geology slash, lunge, rift; botany push, levitate, ferry; necrology nova, astral, awareness. The first four classrooms take every department once, in an order dealt by the seed, a fifth one dealt by the seed (`Institute._deal_dept`). Each classroom keeps its own cycle: in session two periods of the day's three, then closed for one (`IN_SESSION`), from a place in the cycle dealt by the seed (`PHASE_DEAL`), shown over its doors (*Hall doors*). In session it has a class, its subjects one a session (geology's each held for two, `HOLDS`) from a place dealt by the seed (`ROTATION_DEAL`). A department meets in a period when any of its classrooms is in session. Each department has a tier in the level, I at first, up to IV (`TOP`), the tier its classes teach (up to each spell's top, `tier_taught`). When the bell rings a period out, each department that met in it climbs or falls back to I, reading the tiers as they stood: **botany** rises when its class was attended; **astronomy** when another class was (attending astronomy, or nothing, sends it back: its paper is taken at its peak); **geology** when the class attended was another department's standing at geology's own tier (laid down on its own stratum: attending nothing, its own class, or a department above or below it sends it back), so it climbs in step with another department, each rung of it costing a class at its level; **necrology** when another department crashed, fell back to I from II or higher (a fall from I is none). A department that did not meet neither moves nor counts; one at IV holds there. So no ring raises a tier by itself: every climb is the wizard's doing, and most cost another. The timetable model (`tools/timetable_model.py`, fed the game's own rules by `tests/dump_timetable.gd` and checked against every climb they give) plays these rules out by search and at random. On 40 real levels and 81 ways four rooms can start (each with its cycle dealt): botany III takes 5 to 12 rings and IV 9 to 17, necrology III 4 to 8 and IV 6 to 20, astronomy III 3 to 7 and IV 6 to 15, geology III 4 to 11 and IV 8 to 21 (geology III in 38 of the 40 real levels, IV in 31; astronomy IV, which asks for a geology III paper, in 34). By chance botany stands at II or more in 25 % of rings and geology in 28 %; one key slot shuts whole climbs (botany IV never, geology IV never), a third barely helps. Strata was chosen on 2026-10-18 from twelve rules modelled after the simpler cycles left the old rule (rising when strictly below every other department meeting) giving geology III in only 14 real levels and IV in none: strata gives the most geology and astronomy of the rules that are not easy to stumble into, and holds up best with a key ring of one (geology III in 33 levels). It found two faults, both fixed: an untaught botany class crashed botany, so a level with one botany room never gave a botany III paper (nor geology IV; fixed by counting such a class as attended, below), and a random wizard was stuck in 8 % of runs, every class asking for a paper they lacked and the bell silent in passing time. Its route model (`--route`) walks 40 real levels as the game lays them out (the corridors between classrooms, the cords, the locks, the way on's classroom, from `tests/dump_timetable.gd`) for a wizard coming in with nothing on their ring: getting through takes 2 to 6 rings, 3 at the median, in 36 of them; 4 are not got through on the level's own classes within 30 rings (3 not even with no paper asked at the way on), and with locked doors and gates shut 13 are not. With no paper asked at the way on most levels are got through with no ring at all, so the way on's paper is what makes the puzzle one to do. Asking a III paper at the way on would make it 5.5 rings at the median, the department before's II paper 5. Stolen papers are left out of it. A big move (levitate, astral: `Relics.MOVES`) is taught only to a wizard who already has it, in its slot or as a paper (`MapInfo.teaches`): relics stay how those are found. A class of one they lack still counts as attended (the department climbs by it), with no paper. The library is always open.
  - *Classes seated by the bell* (`MapInfo._seat_classes`): every ring, each lecture hall's class goes, whatever befell it (a fallen master or student is not slain any more), and where the hall has a class now it is placed again (`LevelLoader.place_cell`, which leaves out a class whose hall is empty, `MapInfo.class_away`), its master teaching the hall's subject (`Scholar.setup`, `MapInfo.subject_of`; for a move or the utility spell it casts its department's damage spell, the first rung of its ladder, `Scholar.spell_cast`, `Timetable.default_spell`).
  - *Hall doors.* Every doorway of a hall with a class has a door (`ExamDoor`, laid by `Institute._raise`; not marked on the map): the library's open always; a classroom's shut until the wizard walks up to it (within 2 cells, `ExamDoor.near`, `NEAR`), and open then only in passing time, and only if they may go through (`MapInfo.hall_door_up`): from inside always, so no one is shut in a room a bell has emptied; from outside to a wizard enrolled in its class in session (*Prerequisites*, below). In class time every classroom is shut both ways, to those in it and those outside, until a bell rings the period out: the classroom's own cord lets a class out (*Rings and periods*, above). On the wall over each, up or down, two signs. Its plate: a disc in the classroom's department's colour with the mark of what it teaches now and a pip for each tier taught (`ExamDoor.dept`, `subject`, `tier`), ringed by the classroom's day, three arcs going round clockwise from the day's first period at the top, an arc for a period in session and none for one closed, and a small notch outside the rim at the period it is now, as a clock's hand (`ExamDoor.cycle`, `period`). Beside it, while its class asks for a prerequisite, its tag: a sheet in the colour of the department it asks for, with that department's emblem (a sprout, a skull, crystals, a star) and a pip for each tier it needs (`ExamDoor.prereq`); the two stand side by side over the doorway. The rotation is learned by play. Every ring moves each classroom on, so the paper that opens a door changes ring by ring. Each department has a colour (`RisoPrint.DEPT_INKS`: astronomy blue, botany green, necrology violet, geology orange), on its papers, on the plates over its classrooms' doors, on the tags of the doors that ask for it, and on its portal's keystone. A door far off sleeps with its chunk and settles as the wizard comes near; it never drops on the wizard.
  - *The bells and their cords* (`SchoolBell`): one great bell, in the bell room (*Halls*), in the middle of its floor, hung from the ceiling, its cord hanging from it (`great`), the day's periods marked along its yoke (the one it is now lit, bobbing in passing time); out in the corridors 0.4 cords per 1000 cells, at least 2 (`Institute.CORRIDOR_BELLS_PER_K`, `CORRIDOR_BELLS_LEAST`, the bell room's counted among them), running up into the ceiling as a hall's does, each over a free floor with 3 open cells over it (`BELL_ROOM`; else any free floor) at least 3 cells from the way in (`BELL_CLEAR`): the first the nearest to the way in in the stretch of corridors it opens on (`Institute.regions`; anywhere out of the halls if it has no such floor), so a wizard just come in can ring it, at least 12 cells from the bell room's (none so, and the bell room's stands for it), and each other as far from those already hung as can be, at least 12 cells (`BELL_APART`; no draw). And a cord on each lecture hall's floor and the library's, reaching all the way up into the ceiling (`SchoolBell.reach`, `ceiling_over`). Pulling a cord (interact, or striking it with a hex bolt or any spell, `HexBolt.ANSWERS`) rings it if it can (*Rings and periods*): in passing time class time begins, in class time it ends; when a period ends every class is seated afresh and every scholar calms down. Every ring feeds the beast (`RunState.fed`) and the institute shakes while it rings out for 3 s (`MapInfo.BELL_TIME`, `BELL_SHAKE` 5 px, fading). The cord rung last hangs dim.
  - *Where the wizard belongs* (`Timetable.belongs`): in the corridors (anywhere out of a hall's open box; a doorway counts) in passing time, in the library, and in a classroom whose class is in session and they are enrolled in. Anywhere else (the corridors in a period, a classroom with no class or one they are not enrolled in, the beast's chamber) they are truant. A scholar is calm until it sees them truant (its sight, rock stopping it, `Scholar.sees`), then turns on them (`Scholar.rouse`), and every scholar within 640 px with it (`ROUSE_REACH`): a local fuss. Struck by the wizard (`SpellStrike.strike`, `HexBolt`, `Parry`), it turns on them anywhere; a spell cast at nothing (or at anything but a scholar) leaves it be, however near. The next bell calms it (`Scholar.calm`). A calm scholar's touch and spells leave the wizard be (`Scholar.hostile`); it still fights the abominations. Carrying a stolen paper (*Exam papers*, below), the wizard belongs nowhere: every scholar who sees them turns on them until they are rid of it. Where there is no timetable every scholar is turned on the wizard, as before. Calm, its pupils print night; turned, pink.
  - *Prerequisites* (`Timetable.needs`, `NEEDS`; `MapInfo.enrolled`): a class of tier II or higher asks for a paper of one other department, any of its subjects, a tier lower; a class of tier I asks for nothing. The four ask round in a ring: astronomy geology's, geology botany's, botany necrology's, necrology astronomy's. The wizard is enrolled who holds a spell of that department at that tier in a slot (`Abilities.tier`) or carries a paper of it at that tier or higher. Only an enrolled wizard finds a classroom's doors open, belongs in it, and starts its class by going in. One who gets in any other way (a warp, a rift, a projection ending there) is put back where they last were out of the classrooms (`MapInfo.keep_out`); coming back through a department's way back puts them in the classroom they left by, by right. So papers are keys to the classrooms, cycled through the key ring's few slots, and a high class takes a high paper from the department before it round the ring.
  - *Exam papers* (`KeyRing.paper`; `MapInfo._attend`, `field_of`, `_lay_papers`, `lectern_paper`, `lectern_slot`, `lectern_swap`; `RunState.papers_out`, `LevelRecord.lifted`, `pigeonholes`): attending a class is being in its classroom as a bell begins class time and still in it (back from a field trip or never gone) as a bell rings it out, its master there and not turned on the wizard; got out of it by a warp or a rift, it is forfeit. A class whose subject the institute will not teach them (a relic's move they lack) counts as attended all the same, with no paper. Resitting: a paper of its subject carried at a lower tier is graded up to the tier taught, there in hand (`KeyRing.replace`), and nothing is sent away. Its exam paper (the subject at the tier taught, a code on the key ring from `KeyRing.PAPER` up, holding both) is otherwise sent to the level its department's portal leads to (the portal stands in the department's first classroom, `Institute.first_of`; the others' papers go to the same place), and laid there on a free floor at least 10 cells from the way in (`PAPER_CLEAR`, where the level allows; the floor dealt by the run seed) when the wizard next arrives, as a dropped key is (`LevelRecord.dropped`): a field trip. The level map marks a paper lying there whether or not its floor has been seen, so the wizard arriving knows where to fetch it (`RisoMap._mark_level`; `tests/papers_test.gd`). Papers ride on the key ring as keys do, a slot each (taken, swapped off a full ring and left lying, saved), and are kept through a death as keys are; no door takes one. Each classroom's lectern is a pigeonhole holding one paper (`Lectern`, `MapInfo.lectern_slot`): using it puts the paper picked up last on it and gives back the one it held, either of which may be missing (`lectern_swap`), kept in the level's record, so papers are left about the rooms and swapped as the way through asks. Sneaking: in a classroom while it is empty (no class) and its lectern bare, the paper of the class to come lies on it, (`lectern_paper`; at its department's tier now, which may be one its prerequisites would not yet let the wizard attend: the reward for the risk), lifted without the field trip, once a period (`LevelRecord.lifted`). Lifted papers and instructors' prizes are stolen (`KeyRing.stolen`, `STOLEN`: the paper's code plus 5, holding the same spell and tier; its folded corner printed pink): while the wizard carries one, the scholars turn on them (*Where the wizard belongs*); learned at a lantern, put on a lectern or graded up by a resit, it is theirs. A class's master felled leaves no lesson, whoever fells it, and with it gone the class cannot be attended until the next ring seats it again.
  - *Instructors* (`Scholar.instructor`, a classroom's master): the institute at its strongest, 14 more blows than a master out in the level (`INSTRUCTOR_HP`), casting and moving half again as fast (`INSTRUCTOR_HASTE`), and turning on the wizard turns its whole class with it (`Scholar.rouse`: everyone in the room, besides those within 640 px). Turned, it moves as its subject does (`move`): warp, to another floor of its room every 3 s (`MOVE_EVERY`, sooner by its haste); rift, back and forth between its room's two furthest floors as often (floors with room either side, clear of walls, doors and seals: `Scholar.floors`); ferry, across its room on a raft without a stop (170 px/s, `FERRY_SPEED`), turning at its walls; astral, projecting itself for 1.4 s every 3 s (`ASTRAL_TIME`), unhurt meanwhile (`Wound.unhurt`); levitate, always hovering 150 px over the floor (`HOVER`); awareness, seeing the wizard through rock. Felled by the wizard's own blow it leaves a stolen paper of its subject at the spell's top tier where it fell (or, teaching levitate or astral to a wizard who has not found it, that move's relic: *Relics won from masters*), once a room (`MapInfo.instructor_fell`, `LevelRecord.prizes`), if the institute teaches that subject to the wizard: the taste of an exam, the hardest fight in the band for the best paper. Felled by abominations it leaves nothing.
  - *Studying* (`Checkpoint`, `StudyDesk`, `MapInfo.study`, `learn_paper`, `teaches_paper`, `put_back`): a paper is a spell known but not held. At a lantern already lit as where the wizard comes back to, interacting studies too: the oldest paper carried that teaches something is learned, its ability taking its slot at the paper's tier, and the one it puts out of the slot comes back as a paper at its tier. Studying burns the lantern out, as a death does (`MapInfo.burn_lantern`): it protects the wizard no more and is not lit again this run. In the institute the library's reading desk teaches the same way, free, in class time to a wizard in the library, once a period (`MapInfo.desk_paper`, `study_at_desk`, `LevelRecord.studied`): its price is the bell rung to begin class time. Over the desk floats the mark of the paper it would teach now. A paper of the spell held at no higher a tier teaches nothing; a higher one raises it, nothing back. Papers hold any ability that takes a slot (`KeyRing.subjects`: the dash and mend too), so a wizard's loadout is what they hold and what they carry, anywhere in the run. A shrine's boon and a lesson hand back what they put out the same way. There is no exam, and no run-long list of spells taught: papers kept through death are the record.
  - *The library* (`Institute`, `LIBRARY_INSIDE` 18 × 8, as tall as it was, wide enough for the board and bookcases either side of it): a place the wizard belongs at any time, so a safe place in class time, where a bell rung by them ends class time without truancy; its ground floor 4 rows high (`LIBRARY_GROUND`) for the board, one gallery over it, its cord at one end, its reading desk before the board's right end (`DESK_OUT`), bookcases floor to ceiling either side and along the gallery over the board (`RisoDecor._over_board`), and between them on the back wall its **timetable board** (`TimetableBoard`, `BoardArt`, `LevelGen.Type.TIMETABLE_BOARD`, decor kept off its wall): a band for each classroom in its department's colour with its emblem, a column for each period of the day with what it teaches then, now's column bright and the rest shaded, with the tier taught and the department a class asks for; and at each band's end a locator, a plan of where the halls stand, the library and that classroom marked and a line between them (a chevron under the way on's). The way through a level is planned here.
  - *Rolling ladders* (`RollingLadder.gd`, `rolling_ladder.tscn`, `LevelGen.Type.ROLLING_LADDER`, `Archetype.ladders`): where any other band lays a lift, the institute lays a library's rolling ladder (`LevelGen.make_moving`, `_make_ladder`: the same draws are made, but its track always runs right, so only the institute's layouts change), and each library has two of its own under its lowest gallery (*Halls*). Its step, a one-way ledge as wide as the ladder (48 px, `RollingLadder.WIDTH`), stands at the run's left end and rides a rail along its track, over the rest of the run and the 2-4 cells past it. A ladder hangs from the step down to the floor, and is laid only where its foot stands on solid ground all along its track: the same number of open cells down to rock in every column it passes over, 2 to 6 (`RollingLadder.stands`, `LADDER_LEAST`, `LADDER_MOST`; nothing it would run into, `SOLID`, in the way); elsewhere the run stays a ledge. The air it hangs through is kept out of the free cells, so nothing laid later stands in its way. A library's hangs from its gallery's row, the cell under the gallery its own (a rise of one cell, its extra's fourth entry). It stays where it is (starting at a place along its track hashed from its cell) and moves only while the wizard is on the ladder and presses left or right: then it rolls that way at 240 px/s (`ROLL_SPEED`, `RollingLadder.roll`, called by `Player._on_ladder`), to the end of its track and no further, the wizard with it. Up on the ladder takes hold of it (from anywhere along it under its step), and down takes hold from its step (climbing down under it; from a library's gallery too) or from the air beside it (`Player._take_ladder`); held, the wizard climbs it at 260 px/s (`LADDER_CLIMB`), climbing and rolling at once, to dodge a bolt, say; the jumps and the dash come back as on the ground. Climbed to the top they stand on its step, climbed down onto a floor or past its foot they let go, and a jump, a dash or a blow lets go (a quarter second before it can be caught again, `LADDER_REGRIP`). No switch works one. `ladder_test` covers the layout (no lifts left in the institute, every ladder's foot on rock all along its track, the garden's lifts as they were) and the climbing (the step's width, standing still until ridden, held with no way pressed staying put, pressing a way rolling it with the wizard).
  - *Abominations and proctors by the timetable*: during a class (a period) abominations keep still, neither hunting, growing nor launching, and breaches let none out (`MapInfo.abominations_quiet`); in passing time they act up, growing and launching at half their pace before any ring in the level and a quarter more for every period rung out there (`MapInfo.surge`, `SURGE_LEAST` 0.5, `SURGE_PER_RING` 0.25), and breaches letting one out every 16 s before any ring, 1.5 s sooner for every period rung out, down to 5 s (`Breach.every`). A proctor out in the corridors walks them during a class, 10 cells either way of its place (`Scholar.PATROL`, `patrolling`), and keeps its place in passing time.
  - *The beast is fed* (`RunState.fed`, every ring of any cord all run long), and it is the bells that make it strong: fed nothing its great eye takes 3 blows (`Beast.CORE_HP`), its strikes come at 1.6 times their time (`SLOWEST`) and it sends 1 soul (`BeastSouls.SOULS`); it takes a blow more for every 2 rings, up to 12 more (`Beast.core_hp`), its strikes come 5 % of their time sooner a ring, down to 55 % (`Beast.attack_every`), and it sends a soul more for every 3 rings, up to 11 more (`BeastSouls.count`; its eyes have room for 12).
- **The people** (`Scholar.gd`; `student.tscn`, `master.tscn`, `proctor.tscn`, `librarian.tscn`, the kind set in each prefab; `Institute.furnish`, last of all, and classes out in the level in `populate`): about 19 students, 6 masters, 3.8 proctors and a librarian a level. Each classroom a master on its floor before the lectern and three on its gallery (a proctor among them in every other one, `Institute.SEATS`); the library its librarian on the top gallery with 3 stars and a student on each gallery below; and out in the level `CLASSES_PER_K` (0.35) classes, a master with up to 3 students (more on a harder preset) on its floor within 4 cells, every other class with a proctor. Each walks its own floor (hopping a step, its figure rising and falling with its body through the hop rather than held on the floor it left and lifted a cell at once, `ScholarArt._feet`; stopping at a ledge, never more than 5 cells, `HOME`, from where it was laid), fights whatever is nearest of the wizard and the abominations it can see (`SIGHT`, 900 to 1100 px, rock stopping it), keeping its distance as its spells want (`KEEP`, `KEEP_MASTER`), and goes back to its place with nothing to fight. Its touch hurts; a stun stops it; health by kind (`HP`: a student 2, a master 6 and one more for each row past the band's first, a proctor and a librarian 3).
  - *Students* cast a weak bolt (a `Bullet` printed as theirs, 300 px/s) every 3.2 s (`STUDENT_EVERY`, sooner on a harder preset) after a 0.5 s wind-up, while they see their foe. ## 5. Economy

- Going deeper costs `P(d) = round(16 × 1.4^d)`: 16, 22, 31, 44, 86 at depth 5, 236 at depth 8. (Every price was doubled after a playtest: the deeper exit, and with it relics, their hints, skeleton keys and side doors, which are multiples of it; learning, mending, the ink well and cast costs.)
- **Star clusters:** every level has one (`LevelGen.place_cluster`, placed after the hoppers and before the secret rooms), worth `cluster_value(d) = round(10 × 1.25^d)` stars: 10 at depth 0, 31 at depth 5. It hangs in open air, as a moon does, at least half the exit distance from the way back, and is three times as likely over thorns; failing that, it stands on the floor furthest from the way back. It prints as a big star in a wide halo with five small stars wheeling round it (`star_cluster.tscn`, `_cluster`), and the map marks it. Taking it saves the run.
- Warp and rift cost stars each cast (§4).
- Stars per level grow at about 1.3^d.
- Shrines follow `S(d, tier)` above.
- The tension: diving makes stars and abilities cheaper to get, but each death deep down risks more.

## 7. Map screen

## 8. The hex bolt, enemy health and cracked walls

- **Enemies' bodies** (`enemy.tscn`, which the walking and hopping enemies share) collide with the rock and floors (layer 3) and not with one another, so one never stands on, hops onto or is pushed up over another; they pass through each other (wisps still turn back at another wisp ahead, `Mover._wisp_ahead`). The wizard still lands on them.

- **Hex** (`Hex.gd`, `HexBolt.gd`): `Cast` (F, or the pad's X; the action is added at runtime if the project doesn't define it).
  - It throws a comet of spell light from over the Fool, aimed like the dash: the held direction, else facing.
  - It flies up to 8 cells. It passes through one-way ledges and moving platforms, and stops at rock and portcullises.
  - It wounds the first enemy near its path. A cracked wall stops it as rock does (it only chips grit off one); cast empowered (§4d, lodestones) it bores on through up to 3 cells of cracked rock (`HexBolt.BORE`).
  - Charges come back one per 2 s (1.25 s from tier II), and lighting a lantern refills them. Their recharge shows on the spell's mark on the Fool's bag.
- **Tiers:** hex is learned at a shrine (not known at the start: a run starts with slash in the damage slot). II adds a charge, III adds damage, IV pierces its first enemy.
- **The dash is a move, not an attack** (`DashStrike.gd`): the damage spells are the wizard's
  ways to hurt (parry, hex, slash, nova, push; `SpellStrike` for the three that strike round the
  wizard, which wound at least as much as any blow, `Wound.least`, and then stun).
  - While dashing, and while projected, the wizard passes through enemies (their bodies stop blocking: physics layer 2, "Enemy", until the wizard is clear of them), and touching an enemy or a moth swarm never hurts mid-dash; nothing it passes is wounded, stunned or scattered. Shots, thorns and lasers still hurt.
  - The worm's body still blocks the dash (`WormSegment`).
  - Dashing into a cracked wall does nothing to it: only an empowered spell breaks one (§4d). Secrets are found by walking in (or with an empowered spell).
  - `tests/dash_strike_test.gd` covers it; `tests/damage_spells_test.gd` covers slash, nova and push.
- **Enemies** get a `Wound`:
  - **Health:** 2 HP at depths 0–5, 3 from 6 (`Wound.hp_for`), so a parry (1 damage) stuns rather than kills. A stunned (parried) enemy takes double, and even a blow of no damage (a push at tier I) wounds one a parry has left stunned (`Wound.dealt`: at least 1). No damage spell stuns: only a parry does.
  - **Touch:** an enemy hurts the wizard where it is printed: its hit box (`HitBox`, the touch its `Damager` hurts through) is sized and placed over its art in its prefab, while its body (what blocks the wizard and what it walks on) stays the 32 px box round where it stands. A wisp's touch (44 × 30 px, 10 to 40 px over where it stands) lies over the body floating above its path, not on the path itself: walking, its body's middle bobs 19 to 27 px up, well inside (rising to about 45 px only over the top of its turning drop). Put at its cell's centre, a wisp settles onto its floor on its first step (`Mover.SETTLE`; the level moves it to its cell only after adding it, so settling as it was added left it to fall into place when its chunk first woke), and whenever it drops (settling, falling) its art drops its whole body with it (`Mover.fallen`, as it does for `carried`), so a drop never hangs the body tail-up above its touch; a wraith's covers its whole shroud (72 × 130 px); a scholar's runs up its robe to its hat's brim (a student's 40 × 58 px, a master's 44 × 92 px); a skeleton's up to its skull; a bird's, a watcher's and an abomination's across their bodies; a hopper's and a rock-bug's are the plain 34 px box, which fits them. `tests/hitbox_test.gd` checks a wisp's, a student's and a master's touch covers the middle of its printed body, that touching a wisp where it is printed hurts, and that a wisp put in the level stands on its floor after its first step with its body level behind its head; `tests/capture_hitboxes.gd` outlines every kind's body and touch over its art.
  - **Where it is struck:** a spell strikes an enemy anywhere on its touch (its HitBox, over its body as printed; see Touch), as well as on the column through its middle it always tested (from 20 px under to 56 px over its origin). Spells that strike round the wizard feel for it at 3 by 3 points across that box (`SpellStrike.body_of`, `BODY_GRID`); a hex bolt strikes it when its step passes within 12 px of it (`HexBolt.BODY_REACH`, `crosses`). An abomination's touch is fitted round the blobs it has now, the way it faces (`Abomination._fit_touch`, `HitBox.fit`). Things with no HitBox (crossbows, nests, breaches) are struck on the column alone (`tests/damage_spells_test.gd`, `tests/institute_test.gd`).
  - **Hits:** a hit bursts pink (shards, streaks, a ring of motes and a few accent sparks flung the way it went) and knocks the enemy back, and is felt (`Wound.felt`): the action freezes for an instant (`Wound.HIT_STOP`, 0.05 s, or `KILL_STOP`, 0.09 s, for a blow that slays, running at `STOP_SCALE`, 0.05; freezes that overlap end with the last, `Wound.hit_stop`, which a parry's shares), the camera kicks, and the enemy's art is thrown back the way the blow went and squashes and wobbles back over 0.28 s (`RisoProp.struck`). The bosses with wounds of their own (the bramble, the golem, the necromancer, the beast) freeze the same. `tests/hex_test.gd` covers it.
  - **Death:** the enemy drops 1–2 stars. Stars buy upgrades and travel; lanterns restore death protection.
- **Slain until you die:** the level record keeps slain enemies. Dying clears them in every level and reloads the respawn level, so everything is back.
- **Cracked walls** (`CrackedWall.gd`): up to 10 per level, placed last, so they can block anything but a door.
  - **Shape:** thin walls, one or two cells thick with open space on both sides. Half are picked within 3 cells of a key, lantern, exit, shrine or ink well.
  - **Never at a door:** none stands within 3 cells (across plus down) of an exit (side doors among them) or a corridor door (`LevelGen.CRACK_DOOR_CLEAR`), in every band, so a run that has tempered no spell yet is never shut in. A key may well be behind one.
  - **Solid:** a cracked cell is not a rock tile but a solid block on the same collision layer.
  - **Printing:** the terrain prints it as rock (body, shading, grass), and its prop adds a few fine night-ink cracks, quiet but visible up close.
  - **Breaking:** only an empowered spell breaks it (`Empower`, `CrackedWall.crumble`; §4d, lodestones), in every band: it crumbles in blue dust, and the rock reprints without it. A plain bolt or blow only chips grit off it, and the dash does nothing. The record keeps it broken, even across deaths. So cracks met early in a run are locks to come back to once a lodestone has tempered the damage spell.
- **Arming** (the ghost and dropped keys): they now arm once the wizard has been more than about a cell away. An overlap test armed them too early while a level reloaded, because the wizard's collision is off then.
- **Fixed:** `hit_box.gd`'s `stunned` setter never stored the value.
- **Fixed: stuck after a dash.** A dash ending inside an enemy turned enemy collision straight back on, and the push out of the enemy's body could wedge the wizard or fling them across the level. Enemies now block again only once the wizard is clear of every enemy body (`DashStrike._inside_enemy`).
- `tests/hex_test.gd` covers the hex, charges and lantern refill, wounding, double damage when stunned, star drops, slain until death, rock stopping the bolt, cracked walls (deterministic, not tiles, a plain bolt only chipping one, an empowered one breaking it for good) and tiers. `tests/capture_hex.gd` takes stills.
