# Riso print presentation

The main game is presented as a risograph "tarot print": flat ink shapes drawn in code, printed by a shader with misregistration, wobbling edges, dot gain, a grainy halftone and uneven laydown. It is always on: the prefabs keep their old sprites, but they sit under the print, unseen.

## Controls

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| F7 | Print controls (scrolls; grouped in sections that fold open or shut from their headers, a click or A: Travel (debug runs) and Print start open, Look, Keys and Abilities folded, and which are open is remembered while the game runs; `tests/capture_panel.gd` takes stills): print detail, sheet rate, zoom, plate offset, UI detail, specks (missed-ink flecks of bare paper; 20% by default), registration (new sheet / locked / drift), reprint on clock or motion, cut or blend between sheets, realm, plates (independent / trapped), portals (TV static / ripples), light by sight (rock stops it / off), cemetery fog, sky bottoms (Roots, Clouds, Clouds & roots; live cosmetic changes), a Keys section to equip or remove each shaped key and adjust skeleton keys (respecting keyring capacity), and an Abilities section that sets any ability's tier outright (a spell above none takes the slot) |
| F8 | Cycle realm (deep night, twilight, aurora) |

Defaults: deep night realm, independent plates, fine detail, new sheet registration at 8 sheets/s, reprint on motion (landings, take-offs, dashes, hits and portals print a fresh sheet), hard cuts between sheets, and the camera zoomed out to 0.72× of the scene zoom.

## How it works

- `scripts/riso/RisoPrint.gd` is added as the first child of `Main`. It creates eight plate `SubViewport`s (night, blue, pink, accent, eye yellow, hat glow, robe, traveler cloth) plus an overlay viewport. All of them share the game `World2D`, and each one culls to its own canvas visibility layer (bits 12–19; 20 is the overlay). A full-screen `ColorRect` on canvas layer 1 runs `shaders/riso_print.gdshader` over the plates. The HUD and menus draw above it. Traveler cloth uses fixed federal blue (`CLOTH_INK`), independent of the realm's terrain ink and the carried spell's ink.
- Art nodes lay ink *coverage* on a plate: alpha is coverage, so a solid is 1 and a "25" tint is 0.25. By default each ink also lifts the same coverage off the night plate. `knock()` clears plates to bare paper (multiply blend), which is how the eyes, lantern and hat bead read as light. `InkCanvas` keeps these operations ordered; plate order across nodes comes from `z_index`.
- A viewport only renders an item whose ancestors all share its layer, so every `InkCanvas` ORs the ink bits into its ancestors (`RisoPrint.share_layers`). Ancestors draw nothing themselves, and legacy sprites keep only layer 1, so they never reach the plates. The main viewport's cull mask hides the ink bits, and the opaque print covers the legacy art.
- The print is pinned to the world. `RisoPrint` passes the camera's pixel offset (`pin`), and every imperfection is evaluated at screen pixel + `pin`. That covers the halftone screen, grain, edge wobble, laydown, specks and paper texture. So as the camera moves, they stay on the paper instead of sliding over it. Only a new sheet reprints them. The noise uses an exact integer hash (PCG) with its domains turned off the pixel axes. The earlier `fract()` hash lost float precision at screen-sized coordinates, which lined specks up in columns. `tests/riso_pin_test.gd` (windowed) checks that a camera move shifts the printed rock by exactly the camera's pixel shift.
- While printing, the window uses `canvas_items` stretch, so the world renders at full resolution while UI keeps its 320×180 layout. Plates are capped at 1080 lines; the shader samples them by UV.
- Every plate registers on its own, as in the prototype (pull 10): its own offset, rotation and edge wobble, with the prototype's offsets (about 0.3–1.4 px at 720p) and up to ±0.6 px more per new sheet. Where inks meet, a paper sliver opens on one side and the inks overlap dark on the other, shifting with each sheet. **Plates: Trapped** on the F7 panel instead registers night to blue and shares one wobble across all plates, so those edges close up. `tests/capture_plates.gd` prints the same frame both ways side by side.
- The print-detail stops (screen size, wobble, grain, dot gain, laydown) are the prototype's.
- **UI print**: the HUD, interaction prompts, in-world plaques (prices, shrine pop-ups) and the printed map (on top of them all) draw in a canvas of their own (`RisoPrint.ui_canvas`, `InkCanvas.ui`), with its own eight ink plates plus a paper plate. A second pass of the same print (`shaders/riso_ui.gdshader`, sharing `riso_core.gdshaderinc`) lays it over the scene: the same inks and sheets, but finer (the **UI detail** slider on the F7 panel, default 70%, runs from printing like the scene to screen cell ×0.35, wobble ×0.1, grain ×0.4, dot gain ×0.3 and misregistration ×0.1; `UI_*` in `RisoPrint`) and pinned to the screen, so small marks and text stay legible. Knocks there lay paper, so plaques and discs print as paper over the scene.
- **Parry** (`RisoWizard._draw_guard`, `Parry`): the guard prints over the figure and is the same all round, as it guards every side. It is a thin bright band of gleam, in a softer wider one, sweeping across the figure once over the window, as light runs over polished metal, with no bubble or ring round it. A catch bursts in spell light where the hit landed (`RisoFx` `&"parry"`: a flash, sparks thrown back the way the hit came, a ring of motes), and while the parry's invulnerability lasts the figure shimmers gold instead of the hurt pink (`RisoWizard.parry_glory`).
- A hit or death prints a 15% pink screen over the whole sheet for a moment (`RisoHud.flash`), on top of the wizard's own pink hit flash.

## Art

The worm's pink head has a Pac-Man wedge mouth cut out of its rounded front, centered on
the segment's solid circle, showing what is behind it. The front and bite use that same radius;
the mouth only removes flesh, so the head never extends past it. The wedge's root stays ahead
of the centre, clear of the health dots. It gapes near the wizard and
nearly closes during the 0.65 s rest after biting or while stunned. The front is clipped to the
open air at rock faces. `tests/worm_test.gd`
checks the front against the rock and collider; `tests/worm_combat_test.gd` checks the bite
shape, recovery and real dash contact; `tests/capture_worm.gd` takes stills.

**Character** in F7 → Look selects Wizard (the default), Arcane cosmonaut, Mask shaman or The Fool. The choice lasts through travel and new runs in this scene. `RisoCostume` draws the three travelers on `RisoWizard`'s body canvas, using its feet, bow, lean and cloth springs. The cosmonaut has a back cape, glass helmet, a corked backpack flask containing the spell, a pink dash antenna, chest health beads and a chest lantern. The shaman changes the whole mask for each spell; a staff carries the lantern and a dangling chain of health beads, and a pink charm marks dash readiness. The Fool wears a broad poncho and a beret with a curved feather, pink when dash is ready; a knotted cloth bindle changes its shape, folds, ink and arcane mark for every spell, and a separate lantern hangs from the other hand. All three have floating gloves without arms.

Their beads show current and maximum health, including vigor's six hearts. A warm lantern flame means spawn protection; a cold wick means the run is vulnerable, independently of health and dash. Each unlit carried lantern leaves a wispy pink trail at its past world positions, one point every 0.06 s, fading over 1.4 s; it clears on teleport or appearance change and uses no world RNG. Spell accessories show recharge fill, running time, the last-seconds pink warning and Mend's draughts, and stay legible during astral translucency. The Robe comparison setting affects the original wizard; traveler accessories always keep the spell's ink. Dash echoes, astral return marks, death ghosts and portal trips follow the selected figure. `tests/character_styles_test.gd` checks selections, state cues, all eight spells, smoke, unchanged collision and RNG, effects and travel. `tests/capture_characters.gd` takes close comparisons, moving smoke, gameplay-scale views and the F7 selector under `docs/mockups/wizard-replacements/game-captures/`, beside the concept sheets.

Cemetery fog can switch live between Original bands, Torn ribbons (the default), Billowing bank, Ragged ink, Spectral billows, Smoke plumes and Billows & ribbons, using the F7 **Fog shape** dropdown. These options change the cloud's outer silhouette and negative space: separated torn wisps, rounded lobes, jagged edges, tall spectral crests or curling plumes. The same translucent print treatment follows each contour, with no common oval envelope or interior eyes, stitches or particles. The drift, sleep boundary and spell suppression still come from `SleepFog`. The choice lasts for the current session and carries across level changes, like the portal style. `tests/fog_styles_test.gd` checks every menu option, distinct animated contours and the sleep effect; run with a renderer for comparison frames and the F7 selector in `art-captures/fog-shapes`.

Torn ribbons and the ribbons in the blend have a wider pink band along their upper edge. Both the pale body and pink edge fade to zero over roughly the outer quarter at each end, using graded ink coverage while retaining the torn contour and curling motion.

- **Motion: bend, never squash.** Nothing printed squashes or stretches; things bend, sway and trail like cloth on springs. The wizard's body bows above the waist (`RisoWizard.bow`, bending more the higher up, so the hat whips furthest): forward over its feet on landing, back on take-off, flicked out like cloth coming out of a portal, while the robe's hem, the hat's tip and the lean keep their own springs; against a wall the robe lies flat on it rather than bulging. The hopper curls forward to crouch, arches through the air and is thrown forward on landing, its thorns whipping after on a looser spring (`RisoMarks.bent`). A wraith's shroud streams back from the way it drifts. Moths' wings beat on a hinge at the body. Stars rock on their points instead of flipping like coins. A portal or warp trip whirls the silhouette into the core and unwinds it out (`RisoPortalWarp._silhouette`). Plants, lanterns and the bell already bent and swung. `tests/capture_motion.gd` takes a strip of a jump and a warp.
- **Wizard** (`RisoWizard.gd`, on the Player): drawn at 1.3x around the feet so the body is about a tile tall as in the prototype (level cells are 128 px, the collider ~62 px; collision is unchanged). It uses the spring rig from the HTML prototype, driven by the real velocity, floor, dash, climb and invulnerability state. It has planted feet in boots, a cloth hem with folds, a lagging head, a two-spring hat, eased turns, breathing and blinks. The face sits behind the brim and a raised collar, with yellow eyes that have paper-white centres.
- **Astral projection**: while phasing, the wizard prints at 40% coverage on every plate, including partial clearing beneath the robe and hat, so the background shows through. The spell orb prints at its normal coverage throughout astral, with its usual countdown and warning blink. It stays translucent without hurt flashes and returns to full coverage when the 1.5–3 s projection ends. Hits cannot hurt or interrupt it. `tests/astral_moon_test.gd` checks coverage and damage immunity; `tests/capture_astral.gd` shows the normal and projected wizard.
- **Robe ink** is the spell element: the robe, sleeve, collar and hat print on their own plate in the ink of the spell carried (`RisoPrint.ROBES`): hex burgundy, astral teal, parry flat gold, levitate kelly green, awareness orange, rift purple, and federal blue with no spell. Learning a spell re-dyes it over a moment. The F7 panel's **Robe** row switches back to plain blue for comparison. `tests/capture_robes.gd` prints each one.
- **Hat glow** is the movement element: it follows the movement ability used last, dash (fluorescent pink), blink (violet), wall climb (green) or double jump (orange), and flares when it fires. Its tip is the dash: lit with a halo while ready, dark from the moment the dash is used until it comes back (landing or a moon).
- **Spell orb**: spells are a second element, an orb of spell light (accent ink) floating at the wizard's far shoulder. Ready, it is a solid, glowing orb with a big paper-white star turning on it, and a ring pings outward the moment it becomes ready. Recharging (hex, parry, awareness's cooldown, a spent levitate) it is a faint hollow ring filling from the bottom like a vial with a light screen of spell light, no glow, so even nearly charged it reads as not ready: only a ready orb is solid ink. While a spell runs (astral projection, awareness sensing, a levitate float) it swells and a ring round it drains with the time left (a float lasts up to 6 s); in the last 1.5 s (`ORB_WARN`) the orb and ring turn pink and blink faster toward the end. It flares when a spell is cast. Spells do not recolour the hat, and the hex bolt prints in the same accent ink. `tests/capture_orb.gd` takes close-ups of each state.
- **Dash** leaves afterimages: silhouettes of the wizard (robe and hat) every few steps along the dash, in the hat's glow ink and fading over 0.3 s, newest strongest; the glow is knocked out under the wizard so the robe stays blue. Silhouettes read the same in every direction. The figure itself is not squashed. **Hits** flash the whole figure pink. **Astral projection** holds a glowing silhouette at the return point.
- **Terrain** (`RisoTerrain.gd`) is rebuilt from the TileMap's ground layer when `MapInfo` finishes a world. It is one contiguous mass with rounded outer corners, concave fillets and a thin cap strip per walkable run. Shading is two screened night bands inset 22 px and 58 px from every exposed edge, cleared around inside corners, so it follows the outline rather than the cell grid. Floating platforms print with the terrain as bars that join neighbouring platforms and rock and share the cap strip. It knocks the sky out beneath itself.
- **Background** (`RisoBackground.gd`) follows the camera: a night flood, parallax stars and a few abstract shapes per realm, drawn by that realm's backdrop (`RisoBackground._backdrops`, by realm as `RisoPrint.REALMS` holds its colours; a realm without one gets the night's orrery). Decor works the same way: an archetype names its plan (`Archetype.decor`, a `RisoDecor.PLANS` entry), and a band without one wears the garden's. Hyperspace prints in its own realm (violet rock, aqua stars), outside the F8 cycle: no skyline or still stars, just streaks of light rushing back past the view toward a glow ahead.
- **Keys and doors**: each key and door is dealt one of four key colours, printed as ink overprints (sun, ember, moss, plum). The rarer the colour (see `docs/DEEPER_PLAN.md` §3), the longer the key's shaft and the more teeth on its bit: sun one, ember two, moss three, plum four (`RisoMarks.key_shape`). A skeleton key is bone white, bare paper (`RisoPrint.ink_key`): a bone's two-knobbed end for a bow, and the shaft and bit of the other keys (three teeth, as a moss key's), in a pale halo with a paper twinkle (in night on paper, and a small grey bone on the map). A bone gate's ironwork is lifted to pale bone grey rather than blue (`RisoMarks._gate_iron`), its padlock bare white with a night bone on it, and the map marks it in grey (`tests/capture_bones.gd`). A vault is a door like any other, with its room open behind the bars so its loot shows (`tests/capture_vaults.gd`). A key waiting to be picked up floats in a soft halo of its own colour, like a star's. Every carried key trails the wizard in a chain (the HUD shows none). Doors are portcullises filling their corridor cell. Each has a header beam, four spiked iron bars, and one cross-rail hung with a padlock in the key colour it needs (`RisoMarks.padlock`: a pale body tinted with the colour, a dark shackle and keyhole; the cemetery's bells hang the same one), or, on a switch gate, the switch's emblem and the switch's sigil on a paper plate (`RisoMarks.sigil_plate`; the switch carries the same sigil on a plate on its base, and bells, vanes and parked lifts chained to a switch hang the same plate; see `docs/DEEPER_PLAN.md`, "Sigils"). Unlocking plays a short printed opening (`RisoDoorOpen.gd`): the grate winches up into the header with a shudder and a puff of dust, then fades.
- **Prompts** (`RisoPrompt.gd`): a printed paper disc pops up above the focused interactable with an interact symbol: a hand pointing down at it (a blue cuff, three curled fingers, the index finger with a paper nail) that taps about every 0.8 s, a ring of accent ink rippling from the fingertip as it lands. On doors it shows the key colour needed instead. Only one interactable is focused at a time, the nearest the player is touching (`Interactable.focused`): only it shows a prompt and only it answers the interact key, so the shrine's neighbouring stations never both act. `tests/capture_prompt.gd` takes a strip of the tap.
- **Props** are attached by a dresser by each prefab's art kind (`Placeables.TABLE`, `RisoPrint.DRESS` for the rest); `RisoProp.gd` is what they share (printing in view, plaques, pop-ups) and each kind's look is its own script in `scripts/riso/props/` (`RisoProp.KINDS`), with marks shared with the map and HUD in `RisoMarks.gd` and ability marks in `RisoGlyph.gd`: star motes, moons (an accent crescent in a halo that shrinks to a faint sliver while it wanes), the level's ink well (a big gold-rimmed pot under a beam of light and a pulsing halo, with a rolled map floating over it circled by motes, a drop of ink rising and falling, and a price plaque; dry once paid), crescent key, portals (an upright doorway about the wizard's height, shaped as a stadium: half circles top and bottom joined by straight sides. What fills it is chosen on the F7 panel (`RisoPrint.portal_style`): bands of TV static (the default: thin rows of paper-and-night snow flickering about 18 times a second, in bands of a few rows, bright or dim, rolling down) or ripples on a pool of water (deep blue, rings spreading from a drip of light in the middle and fading at the rim, a sheen across the top). A level's own are gates: a solid accent rim on a stone threshold with light pooled on the floor, and a sigil on a paper disc on top (one of the 12 in `RisoMarks.sigil`) shared by both ends of a pair and by no other link in the level while there are sigils to go round. The wizard's rifts are torn in the air: a rim of flickering eye-yellow dashes, no threshold, a spark sigil; one waiting for its partner is a dim dashed outline that breathes. With the wizard right at it (it takes standing close), the inside slows to about a third of its speed, as if it waits for them, and the rim brightens; a portal flares when someone comes through. A trip (`portal.gd`, `RisoPortalWarp.gd`) is three beats, the wizard held still: in, 0.3 s, the wizard's glow silhouette stretched tall and thin and pulled into the core along converging streaks, ending in a flash; a cut, the camera snapping to the far portal; out, the far core flashing, a sliver of light opening into the silhouette and dropping to the wizard's feet as they reappear (popped out tall, springing back), with a ring, streaks and motes bursting outward. `tests/capture_portals.gd` and `tests/capture_portal_warp.gd` take stills), portcullis doors, lantern checkpoint (an ember when unclaimed, fully lit when it is your respawn), level exits (a lit doorway under a chevron pointing where it leads; an unpaid deeper exit has a pink frame, stays dark, and prints its price on a paper plaque), the shrine (a plinth across two cells with three stations side by side: two niches where the offered ability's mark floats over its tier pips, and a bowl holding an ember bead for mending; stepping up to one pops up a larger plaque with its name, tier and price in thickened night ink; it dims once spent), floating ledge, straight thorns (rotated with the spikes), the death ghost (the wizard's astral silhouette in glow ink, with a dark hood and lit eyes, and the stars it holds circling it), cracked walls (printed as rock by the terrain, with a few fine night-ink cracks; the rock reprints when one breaks), the hex bolt (a crimson glow-ink comet with a tapering tail), Wisp nightmare (stunned eyes close), the watching eye shooter, the hopper (a squat pink toad of a nightmare with a ridge of thorns and paper eye slits that squashes to crouch, stretches as it leaps over its shadow and slumps as it lands), the laser (a dark housing set in the rock; warming up, a flickering pink sight line; firing, a broad pink beam with a bare-paper core and a splash where it strikes), and shard bullets. ## Interface

- **Surface decor** (`RisoDecor.gd`): small printed props that make the grotto feel lived in. They never affect gameplay.
  - **Floors:** grass tufts, moon-flowers, mushroom clusters, stones; in the garden, broad-leaved plants (two to four long stalks fanning from one root, each holding a big pointed leaf with a darker midrib, varied per cell, swaying), and hedges in runs of 2–5 cells along stretches of floor (a band of moss leafage with rounded tops, behind the fences).
  - **Ceilings:** hanging roots, stalactites, ink drips.
  - **Walls:** leafy vines.
  - **Placement:** every choice is a hash of the level seed and cell, so a level always wears the same decor. The world's RNG is untouched. Props stay inside the level and clear of structures (exits, shrine, doors, lanterns, thorns, portals, orbs, lifts, bells, switches, switch gates, keys, relics) and the cells either side of them, with a wider stretch kept clear by the tall ones. Decor is background: it prints at z -15 (fences at -16, the garden's hedges behind them at -17), under the lantern light (-12), every prop (2), the wizard's trail (9) and the wizard (10), so keeping it off structures means nothing of it shows over or through them.
  - **Drawing:** props are sketched and then grown ×2 from where they sprout. They are batched onto one ink canvas per 16×16 chunk and drawn once per level.
- **Sway:** plants (tufts, broad-leaved plants, moon-flowers, roots, vines) are drawn live on screen. They sway in a slow breeze and spring away from the wizard as they brush past, more so when moving fast. Lanterns rock on their hooks the same way. Static decor (stones, mushrooms) stays batched.
- **Awareness pointers** (`RisoHud`): arrows at the edge of the view with a mark for each sensed thing off screen. Exit arches are pink for deeper; the ink well, shrine and keys get their own marks. Overlapping pointers are dropped.
- **Lantern light** (`RisoLight.gd`): the lit respawn lantern lifts the night ink in a soft, flickering three-ring pool. It absorbs one death, then stays dark for the rest of the run with empty glass and a charred wick. Other unspent lanterns give a faint glow. The lantern the wizard carries lights the way too: while a lit lantern protects them, a smaller warm pool (`CARRIED_RINGS`) follows its flame wherever they go; unprotected, only a dim pool faintly tinted pink (`UNLIT_RINGS`). Open exits, portals (and rifts), an ink well not yet dry and hex bolts in flight give small pools of their own (`GLOW_RINGS`, `BOLT_RINGS`; they join the `RisoLight.GLOWS` group). Uses `InkCanvas.lift_ink`, a partial knock. `tests/capture_carried_light.gd` takes stills.
- **Line of sight** (`RisoSight.gd`, `RisoLight`): rock stops light and sight. `RisoSight` works over the level's cells (the TileMap's ground, plus cracked walls until they break, shut doors and shut switch gates, kept up to date each frame): rays to every corner of the rock in a box, and just either side of each, outline what a point sees as one polygon, reaching `FACE` (26 px) into the faces of the rock it meets so a lit wall's face reads as lit. Every pool is cut to what its flame sees (a still light's outline is kept until the rock changes), so walls cast hard-edged shadows. The wizard sees from their eyes to the edge of the view; what they cannot see (buried rock, caves behind a wall, and everything in them) lies under more night ink, the place's `shade` (`Archetype.shade`: 0.3, the catacombs 0.6), printed at z 8 over the props and under the wizard's trail, so it stays there to make out but dimmed. The catacombs are also deep dark (`Archetype.gloom`, 0.6): night ink over everything no light falls on, in sight or not, lifting in three steps ring by ring inside each pool (`GLOOM_STEPS`), so most of what is seen is what a lantern lights and an unprotected wizard's single dim ring is felt. The print fills only simple outlines, so the dark round a seen polygon is split into pieces with no holes (`RisoSight.outside`). **Light by sight** on the F7 panel (Look) turns the cut and the shade off to compare (the deep dark stays). It costs about 1.5 ms a frame. `tests/sight_test.gd` covers it; `tests/capture_sight.gd` takes stills (the garden with it on and off, the catacombs lit and unlit) and prints its cost.
- **Plated guards** (`RisoWard.gd`): an enemy's shield (`Shield`, pink) and the wizard's ward (`Ward`, eye yellow) print the same way: a ring of curved plates, one per hit left, turning slowly, with wide gaps between them (`PLATE_SHARE`) so the plates left can be counted, the night and rock lifted underneath so they read bright over both, each with a paper glint, over a faint veil; struck, the ring flashes, and a broken plate flies apart in shards. An enemy's prints solid, centred on the middle of what its art draws (`RisoProp.guard_center`: a wisp's body trails behind its origin); the wizard's prints faint (`Ward.COVER`, thinner plates) so it never competes with the figure, flaring fully only when struck. A ward's charge growing back prints its plates faintly. `tests/capture_ward.gd` takes stills.
- **The bramble** (`Bramble`, the garden's way-up boss) prints itself on one canvas at the props' z (2), in the boss's rules: danger pink, weak points accent, health countable. Its knot fills the head of the shaft: a dark green mass (darker than rock, lighter than the open shaft, so it reads as solid) under thick stems of bark twisted across it, with pink thorns lifted bright off the bark and longer thorns along its underside, and a pip of accent for each bulb on a dark knot-hole (dark once burst). Roots of bark creep down the walls' faces to the bulbs and vines. A bulb is an accent swelling cupped in bark at the rock face, a paper gleam on it, its hits left as dark dots; it swells as it readies a seed and flashes pink when struck, and bursts into a dark husk with accent petals flying. A vine out is a writhing pink stem tapering from its root, thorns on both edges and a hooked tip; warning, three pink shoots tremble out of the rock; withered, a short dry curl of bark. Its seeds are spinning pink seeds with a dark eye (`ShardArt`). As the knot tears open its two halves pull apart toward the walls and shrink, and everything fades, ink lifted in step (never knocked to bare paper mid-fade). `tests/capture_bramble.gd` takes stills.
- **Ambient life** (`RisoAmbient.gd`), only on screen:
  - paper-white fireflies fading in and out over flowers and grass
  - ink drops falling from ceiling drips and splashing where they land
- **Distant skyline** (`RisoBackground.gd`): ruined colonnades, towers and floating islands in a faint blue screen, sliding at a tenth of the camera's speed.
- **Enemy anchors:** wisps cast a night shadow on the floor, and watchers grow on a swaying stalk rooted in the floor.
- **A wisp turning round** (`WispArt`): its head runs round a teardrop cut in half by its path, point where it turns and round end ahead, dipping under first and coming back over the top, and the body follows the head's path all the way round (the drop is laid into the trail in 2 px steps, so the tail traces it at any frame rate); its shadow darkens as it dips and fades as it rises.
- **Visual language:**
  - **Colours:** pink is danger (thorns, wisps, watchers), yellow is reward (stars, keys, price plaques), glow ink is the wizard's own (hat, hex, ghost), and blue is the world. Decor uses only blue, night, moss (accent over blue) and bare paper, never pink or yellow.
  - **Shapes:** points hurt, rounds are collected, arches are ways through.
  - **Breakables:** thin night-ink lines in rock only ever mean a cracked wall; nothing else is drawn inside the rock.
- **Level transitions** (`RisoTransition.gd`): travelling between levels, or respawning in another level, sweeps a sheet of night ink with a ragged edge across the view like passing scenery: up when going deeper, down going back, sideways through the side doors. It holds for at least 0.35 s with the destination's marks and numbers on a paper plaque (see **Places without words**), then sweeps on and away. `tests/capture_transition.gd` takes stills.
- **Camera kicks:** hex hits shake the camera a little, kills a little more, and breaking a cracked wall most. A bolt ending against rock gives a faint tick.
- **Printed map** (`RisoMap.gd`): a paper sheet over the view, opened with M. The level view prints seen rock in blue ink and explored ground as a light blue screen, from one-texel-per-cell textures on the blue plate. It marks exits, the shrine, lanterns, teleporters, doors, keys, the ghost and the wizard with small abstract marks printed over what is under them (never knocking it out), and its legend lists only what the page shows (see `docs/DEEPER_PLAN.md` §7). The world view prints visited levels as labelled tiles joined by opened doors. See `docs/DEEPER_PLAN.md` §7. On the worlds page a cursor picks any visited level (W/A/S/D or the arrows; A past the leftmost tile turns back), and E, Space or Enter, or a click on its tile, opens that level's map as far as it was seen, rebuilt from its seed (`MapInfo.world_at`), with what is in it as its record leaves it: exits, shrine, lanterns, ink well, teleporters, keys (and keys dropped there) and doors in their colours (as the level dealt them, `RisoMap.dealt_colors`), gates, switches, relics, star clusters, bells, vanes and bridges. Debug runs see every level's whole map from the start. `tests/map_worlds_test.gd` covers picking and the F7 ability picker.
- **Printed HUD** (`RisoHud.gd`): no standing plaques. What they showed is in the game: health is the traveler's necklace, protection their lantern (lit, or a dim pulsing pink while unprotected), keys trail behind them, and the place shows when sitting and on the level transition. It lives on the ink plates and follows the camera, in the 320×180 UI space, and prints in its own, finer pass (see **UI print** below). What remains:
  - **Places without words** (`RisoMarks.place_marks`, `Rules.place_numbers`): the only writing in the game's UI is numbers. A place prints as a globe (a disc banded by two paper latitudes) before the world's number, a thick down arrow before the depth's, and, in a side world, an upright portal mark after them (its depth is that of the level it hangs off). The sitting card, the ghost's pointer, the level transition, the end card and the signpost all print places this way.
  - **Sitting** (hold Down while standing still, `Player.is_sitting`): a card over the traveler's head with the stars carried (a gold plaque with a rocking star) and, over it, the place. Near the top of the view the card hangs lower rather than off the edge. The UI print is pinned to the screen, so text gliding across it shimmers: sitting pans the camera down, and the card is put on whole pixels where the traveler will be once the view settles (`RisoHud._settling`) and held there (`CARD_SLACK`). The ghost pointer's plaque likewise stays still while its arrow bobs. The Fool sits down for it, sets its lantern beside its boots and flips a gold coin.
  - **The ghost:** while there is one, a pointer at the edge of the view toward it (unless it is in view), with its mark on a disc (pink while unprotected) and a small plaque with its stars and, when it is in another place, that place. The map marks it too.
  - **Awareness pointers**, as below, and the **end card** when a run ends: the ghost over the run's world and the deepest depth reached.
  - **Icon ink:** HUD icons are night ink, never glow ink, because the glow plate takes the hat's colour, which can be pink.
  - **The signpost** (`Signpost.gd`, `prefabs/signpost.tscn`, `props/SignArt.gd`), not placed in levels for now and kept for later uses: a blue post with a paper board bearing the globe and depth marks. Standing by it (`Signpost.READ_REACH`, nothing to press) pops up the place's marks and numbers and a plaque of the abilities known (each mark, a pip per tier past the first, the slotted spell underlined); it closes as the wizard walks on. It never opens off the top of the view.
  - `tests/hud_in_world_test.gd` covers sitting, the ghost's pointer and the signpost on its own.
- **Menus** (`RisoMenu.gd`, `RisoTheme.gd`): the start and pause menus print in the UI's riso, as finely as the HUD. Their Godot controls only lay them out and take focus and input (they draw nothing while the print is on); `RisoMenu` prints each visible one like the HUD's plaques, night text on paper: a button is a paper plaque tinted and rimmed in blue, solid accent while focused or hovered, solid blue while pressed; a field is paper with a blinking caret while focused and its placeholder in faint night; labels (the title, the saved run) print as paper over the scene. `RisoTheme` restyles the shared `main_menu_theme.tres` in the realm's inks. The printed sky shows behind the main menu. `tests/capture_menu.gd` takes stills.

## Performance

Measured on the largest level (60 × 48, depth 8+) at 1280 × 720 with vsync off: about 15 ms a frame (it was 89 ms on the old 80 × 70 cap); a level change stalls one frame for about 0.2 s (it was 0.66 s). `tests/bench_big_level.gd` times each part of the print; `tests/bench_chunks.gd` times level loads.

- **One draw call per ink operation** (`InkOp.gd`): every shape an op holds goes into one triangle array. The terrain alone holds thousands, which had been thousands of draw calls on each ink plate.
- **Chunks** (`LevelLoader.sleep_far_chunks`): the level is cut into 8 × 8 cell chunks; a few times a second each chunk wakes or sleeps by its distance from the view, or from the wizard (the camera lags a rift jump or respawn) (awake within 640 px, asleep past 896 px). Everything placed with the level in a sleeping chunk stops processing, leaves the physics world and is hidden. Bolts, ghosts and rifts always run. An awakened worm also stays active for its pursuit across the level, even when its root's original burrow sleeps (`tests/worm_combat_test.gd`). Its full 12-cell activation range wakes it independently of those chunks, so a sleeping burrow cannot prevent emergence (`tests/worm_wake_test.gd`).
- **Props print as they come into view** (`RisoProp._process`), not all in the frame a level loads; still kinds then stop processing. Stars are printed once and animated by moving their canvases.
- **Loading:** the layout (`LevelGen.new`) is built on the generator thread with the cells, and kept with them for revisits and the map. Rock is laid as plain tiles (the print draws its own rock; every rock tile has the same collision), and there is no background tile layer.
- **Wisps** place their bodies from a per-frame table of their trail (positions, directions and bends every 2 px) instead of walking the trail for each vertex: about 0.4 ms a wisp on screen.

## Limits

- Presentation only: no collision, physics or generation changes. The only gameplay-file edit is one notify call at the end of `MapInfo._arrived()`.
- Menus keep their Godot controls for layout and input; RisoMenu prints them.
- Prop art is sized to the current prefab scales (cells are 128 world px). Standing props measure their own ground each redraw, because checkpoints shift themselves after spawning; spikes stand on the surface they are rotated toward. A prefab added later needs an entry in `RisoPrint.DRESS` and a drawing in `RisoProp.gd`, or it stays invisible under the print.
