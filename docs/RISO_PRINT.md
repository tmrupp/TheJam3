# Riso print presentation

The main game is presented as a risograph "tarot print": flat ink shapes drawn in code, printed by a shader with misregistration, wobbling edges, dot gain, a grainy halftone and uneven laydown. It is always on: the prefabs keep their old sprites, but they sit under the print, unseen.

## Controls

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| F7 | Print controls (scrolls; grouped in sections that fold open or shut from their headers, a click or A: Travel (debug runs) and Print start open, Look, Keys and Abilities folded, and which are open is remembered while the game runs; `tests/capture_panel.gd` takes stills): print detail, sheet rate, zoom, plate offset, UI detail, specks (missed-ink flecks of bare paper; 20% by default), graphics (full / light, below), registration (new sheet / locked / drift), reprint on clock or motion, cut or blend between sheets, realm, plates (independent / trapped), portals (TV static / ripples), light by sight (rock stops it / off), cemetery fog, sky bottoms (Roots, Clouds, Clouds & roots; live cosmetic changes), a Keys section to equip or remove each shaped key and adjust skeleton keys (respecting keyring capacity), and an Abilities section that sets any ability's tier outright (a spell above none takes the slot) |
| F8 | Cycle realm (deep night, twilight, aurora) |

Defaults: deep night realm, independent plates, fine detail, new sheet registration at 8 sheets/s, reprint on motion (landings, take-offs, dashes, hits and portals print a fresh sheet), hard cuts between sheets, and the camera zoomed out to 0.72× of the scene zoom.

## How it works

- `scripts/riso/RisoPrint.gd` is added as the first child of `Main`. It creates eight plate `SubViewport`s (night, blue, pink, accent, eye yellow, hat glow, robe, traveler cloth) plus an overlay viewport. All of them share the game `World2D`, and each one culls to its own canvas visibility layer (bits 12–19; 20 is the overlay). A full-screen `ColorRect` on canvas layer 1 runs `shaders/riso_print.gdshader` over the plates. The HUD and menus draw above it. Traveler cloth uses fixed federal blue (`CLOTH_INK`), independent of the realm's terrain ink and the carried spell's ink.
- Art nodes lay ink *coverage* on a plate: alpha is coverage, so a solid is 1 and a "25" tint is 0.25. By default each ink also lifts the same coverage off the night plate. `knock()` clears plates to bare paper (multiply blend), which is how the eyes, lantern and hat bead read as light. `InkCanvas` keeps these operations ordered; plate order across nodes comes from `z_index`.
- A viewport only renders an item whose ancestors all share its layer, so every `InkCanvas` ORs the ink bits into its ancestors (`RisoPrint.share_layers`). Ancestors draw nothing themselves, and legacy sprites keep only layer 1, so they never reach the plates. The main viewport's cull mask hides the ink bits, and the opaque print covers the legacy art.
- The print is pinned to the world. `RisoPrint` passes the camera's pixel offset (`pin`), and every imperfection is evaluated at screen pixel + `pin`. That covers the halftone screen, grain, edge wobble, laydown, specks and paper texture. So as the camera moves, they stay on the paper instead of sliding over it. Only a new sheet reprints them. The noise comes from a table of an exact integer hash (PCG) with its domains turned off the pixel axes. The table (`shaders/riso_noise.gdshader`, 1024 × 1024 texels of three 8-bit values each) is drawn once, and each plate, noise stream and sheet reads it wrapped, from a corner of its own (`RisoPrint._set_noise`, from a hash of the sheet's seed and the plate); each plate's screen angle and rotation reach the shader as cosines and sines (`plate_screens`, `plate_turns`). Value noise is the table blended by the GPU's bilinear filter at the smoothstepped fraction, and the specks' threshold, near 1, reads two values as one 16-bit number. Hashing every pixel had been most of the print's cost on an integrated GPU, whose 32-bit integer multiplies are slow. The earlier `fract()` hash lost float precision at screen-sized coordinates, which lined specks up in columns. `tests/riso_pin_test.gd` (windowed) checks that a camera move shifts the printed rock by exactly the camera's pixel shift.
- While printing, the window uses `canvas_items` stretch, so the world renders at full resolution while UI keeps its 320×180 layout. Plates are capped at 1080 lines (`FULL_LINES`); the shader samples them by UV.
- **Graphics** (`RisoPrint.graphics`, chosen at the start by the GPU, and on the F7 panel): **full** antialiases the plates (4× MSAA) and prints at the window's size; **light**, for a weak GPU (integrated graphics are put on it), leaves the plates unantialiased and renders them and both print passes at most 720 lines tall (`LIGHT_LINES`) into a viewport of its own (`sheet_viewport`), scaled up to the window (`sheet_rect`). The print is a little softer; the GPU's work a frame is about a quarter of full's. `tests/capture_graphics.gd` prints the same view both ways, side by side and close up.
- **Skipping empty plates** (`riso_core.gdshaderinc`, `ink_near`): most of the sheet holds no ink of most plates, so for each plate the print first looks for any of its ink within reach of the pixel (as far as the wobble and dot gain can carry a sample, at nine points) and, finding none, skips the wobble, gain and grain. The stray flecks such paper prints (the grain pulling a dot's threshold under nothing, near a dot's middle) are kept: there they are worked out from the same grain, read without the wobble (which only shifts where it is read). The two print passes cost about half what they did.
- Every plate registers on its own, as in the prototype (pull 10): its own offset, rotation and edge wobble, with the prototype's offsets (about 0.3–1.4 px at 720p) and up to ±0.6 px more per new sheet. Where inks meet, a paper sliver opens on one side and the inks overlap dark on the other, shifting with each sheet. **Plates: Trapped** on the F7 panel instead registers night to blue and shares one wobble across all plates, so those edges close up. `tests/capture_plates.gd` prints the same frame both ways side by side.
- The print-detail stops (screen size, wobble, grain, dot gain, laydown) are the prototype's.
- **UI print**: the HUD, interaction prompts, in-world plaques (prices, shrine pop-ups) and the printed map (on top of them all) draw in a canvas of their own (`RisoPrint.ui_canvas`, `InkCanvas.ui`), with its own eight ink plates plus a paper plate. A second pass of the same print (`shaders/riso_ui.gdshader`, sharing `riso_core.gdshaderinc`) lays it over the scene: the same inks and sheets, but finer (the **UI detail** slider on the F7 panel, default 70%, runs from printing like the scene to screen cell ×0.35, wobble ×0.1, grain ×0.4, dot gain ×0.3 and misregistration ×0.1; `UI_*` in `RisoPrint`) and pinned to the screen, so small marks and text stay legible. Knocks there lay paper, so plaques and discs print as paper over the scene.
- **Parry** (`RisoWizard._draw_guard`, `Parry`): raised with every damage spell as it is cast, the guard prints over the figure and is the same all round, as it guards every side. It is a thin bright band of gleam, in a softer wider one, sweeping across the figure once over the window, as light runs over polished metal, with no bubble or ring round it. A catch bursts in spell light where the hit landed (`RisoFx` `&"parry"`: a flash, sparks thrown back the way the hit came, a ring of motes), and while the parry's invulnerability lasts the figure shimmers gold instead of the hurt pink (`RisoWizard.parry_glory`).
- A hit or death prints a 15% pink screen over the whole sheet for a moment (`RisoHud.flash`), on top of the wizard's own pink hit flash.

## Art

The worm's pink head has a Pac-Man wedge mouth cut out of its rounded front, centered on
the segment's solid circle, showing what is behind it. The front and bite use that same radius;
the mouth only removes flesh, so the head never extends past it. The wedge's root stays ahead
of the centre, clear of the health dots. Paper teeth line both jaws, those of one between those
of the other, so shut they mesh in a zigzag along the seam (`Worm._teeth`). It chomps
(`Worm._gape`): near the wizard the jaws ease open and snap shut, quicker and wider the nearer they
are (`CHOMP_SLOW` to `CHOMP_FAST` a second), and far off hold still a little open. Biting, they
spring wide and slam shut in 0.08 s (`BITE_SNAP`), throwing pink strokes out from the jaws that
fade over 0.3 s, and stay shut through the 0.65 s rest after; stunned, they hang nearly shut. The
front is clipped to the open air at rock faces. Striking (`Worm._strike_reach`, `_strike_gape`), it
pauses with its jaws shut, draws its head back into its neck as they open wide, lunges out along
its path and snaps them shut at full reach, then settles back. Its thorns, on alternate flanks
from one segment to the next, grow from fixed places along the body (four to a cell a side), so they ride along as it crawls: fanned apart round the
outside of a bend, shrinking smoothly away on its squeezed inside, and growing in or out as the
flank turns toward or away from the side they face, never popping (`Worm.thorn_spots`). Each shows
wherever the flesh it grows from is out in the open, so they go into a hole with it, one by one;
nothing shows through the rock. The half of each segment toward its thorns is speckled with
small plates of the thorns' pink lying back toward the tail (`Worm.scale_plates`), a rough hide
where it cannot be cut; the bare half is plain soft flesh. A burrow
hole is the ground pushed up round a dark pit: shoulders of the rock's own ink rising to a rim
either side of the worm's width, turfed like the floor's cap strip where the hole is in a floor,
with crumbs of freshly dug pale earth among clods of the ground's own (`Worm._draw_hole`), all
only over open air, so at the end of a space a cell wide it heaps against the walls. A hole
it digs into a wall appears as its nose reaches the face. `tests/capture_worm.gd` takes stills,
and strips of its chomps, a strike, a crawl round a bend and a dig. `tests/worm_test.gd`
checks the front against the rock and collider; `tests/worm_combat_test.gd` checks the bite
shape, recovery and real dash contact; `tests/capture_worm.gd` takes stills.

**The Fool** is the one figure the player is printed as (the wizard, the Arcane cosmonaut and the Mask shaman are gone, and so is the F7 choice between them). `RisoCostume` draws them on `RisoWizard`'s body canvas, using its feet, bow, lean and cloth springs: a broad poncho, a beret with a curved feather, floating gloves without arms, a carried lantern in one hand and, over the other shoulder, a knotted cloth bindle whose shape, folds, ink and arcane mark change with the damage slot's spell. The bindle is the Fool's wand: casting a damage spell (`Abilities.cast` sends the wizard's `cast` event, `RisoCostume.cast`), the pole swings forward up over the shoulder to point the bag the way the spell goes (a nova's straight up, a lunge's along its thrust, the rest the way the wizard aims) in `CAST_OUT` (0.07 s), coming round in front of the poncho, whipping a quarter past the spell's way (`CAST_OVER`), the bag hanging back from the turn, stretching as it flies (`CAST_STRETCH`) and leaving a smear, a fading fan of its ink over the arc it swept in the last few steps (`CAST_SMEAR`), then flung out with a puff of spell light (`RisoFx` "cast"); it eases back onto the spell's way over `CAST_HOLD` (0.1 s) and swings home in `CAST_BACK` (0.32 s), a little past rest and back (`CAST_UNDER`). The rest of the body answers: the gripping glove thrusts the pole the spell's way (`CAST_THRUST`), the figure stretches as the bindle swings out and squashes on the hold (`CAST_SQUASH`, about its feet), the body leans into the cast and the hat tip flicks (`CAST_LEAN`, `CAST_FLICK`), the poncho's hem whips back (`CAST_HEM`), the lantern swings back (`CAST_LANTERN`) and the head nods into it (`CAST_NOD`). `tests/fool_test.gd` covers it; `tests/capture_changes_gif.gd` (its `bindle` scene) records it close up. The **feather** is the movement slot (`feather_plate`): in the movement ability's glow ink while it is ready (dash pink, blink violet, astral teal, levitate mint, rift violet, warp coral, ferry green: `RisoPrint.GLOWS`, the glow plate taking the slot's colour), dark when spent or recharging, and in a running movement spell's last 1.5 s (`SPELL_WARN`) it blinks pink and dark, faster toward the end. Filling again (the dash back, a movement spell ready) it pops (`RECHARGE_TIME`, 0.45 s): it swells by up to a third and back, flashes paper-white for its first 0.14 s (`RECHARGE_FLASH`), flicks on the hat tip's spring, and throws off a flash, sparkles and a ring of motes in its glow ink (`RisoFx` "recharge"). While charged a soft, slowly breathing halo of its glow ink hangs round it (`FEATHER_HALO`), so a ready feather reads from a spent one at a glance. `tests/fool_test.gd` checks the pop; `tests/capture_wisp_lurch.gd` captures it (`feather_pop.png`). Nothing on the figure stands for the **utility** slot: there is no standing indicator, only feedback on use. It is seen when learned at a shrine, on the map and pause screens, and on the sitting card (see **Printed HUD**). Pressing Utility when the spell cannot work (cooling down, no draught left, nothing to heal) sputters: a dull puff of night and blue from the bag's knot and a dull click (the hurt sound played low and quiet, `RisoPrint.FIZZLE_*`; the `fizzle` visual event).

Their necklace shows current and maximum health, including vigor's six hearts. A warm lantern flame means spawn protection; a cold wick means the run is vulnerable, independently of health and dash. An unlit lantern leaves a wispy pink trail at its past world positions, one point every 0.06 s, fading over 1.4 s; it clears on teleport and uses no world RNG. A lantern snuffed at a font (`MapInfo.flame_snuffed`: out, but still protecting) is never pink: its glass goes dark night ink (`SNUFFED_GLASS`) holding a pale crescent moon (`SNUFFED_MOON`, the shade gates' mark) over a charred wick, and for `SNUFF_SMOKE` (2.6 s) after it goes out it trails the same smoke lifted pale instead (`SNUFF_SMOKE_LIFT`). `tests/capture_snuffer.gd` takes the lantern lit, snuffed and unprotected, outside and in a crypt. The bag shows recharge fill, running time and the last-seconds pink warning, and stays legible during astral translucency. Dash echoes, astral return marks, death ghosts and portal trips use the Fool's outline (`RisoCostume.silhouette`). `tests/fool_test.gd` checks the state cues, every damage spell's bag, the feather's ink and its blink, nothing printed for the utility spell, smoke, unchanged collision and RNG and effects. `tests/capture_characters.gd` takes the states side by side and a gameplay-scale view under `docs/mockups/wizard-replacements/game-captures/`, beside the concept sheets.

Cemetery fog can switch live between Original bands, Torn ribbons (the default), Billowing bank, Ragged ink, Spectral billows, Smoke plumes and Billows & ribbons, using the F7 **Fog shape** dropdown. These options change the cloud's outer silhouette and negative space: separated torn wisps, rounded lobes, jagged edges, tall spectral crests or curling plumes. The same translucent print treatment follows each contour, with no common oval envelope or interior eyes, stitches or particles. The drift, sleep boundary and spell suppression still come from `SleepFog`. The choice lasts for the current session and carries across level changes, like the portal style. `tests/fog_styles_test.gd` checks every menu option, distinct animated contours and the sleep effect; run with a renderer for comparison frames and the F7 selector in `art-captures/fog-shapes`.

Torn ribbons and the ribbons in the blend have a wider pink band along their upper edge. Both the pale body and pink edge fade to zero over roughly the outer quarter at each end, using graded ink coverage while retaining the torn contour and curling motion.

- **Motion: bend, never squash.** Nothing printed squashes or stretches; things bend, sway and trail like cloth on springs. The figure bows above the waist (`RisoWizard.bow`, bending more the higher up): forward over its feet on landing, back on take-off, flicked out like cloth coming out of a portal, while the poncho's hem, the feather and the lean keep their own springs. The hopper curls forward to crouch, arches through the air and is thrown forward on landing, its thorns whipping after on a looser spring (`RisoMarks.bent`). A wraith's shroud streams back from the way it drifts. Moths' wings beat on a hinge at the body. Stars rock on their points instead of flipping like coins, their gold printed on paper (the night and the rock's ink knocked out under each, as a cluster's are, `MoteArt`) so they read on a dark wall. A portal or warp trip whirls the silhouette into the core and unwinds it out (`RisoPortalWarp._silhouette`). Plants, lanterns and the bell already bent and swung. `tests/capture_motion.gd` takes a strip of a jump and a warp.
- **The rig** (`RisoWizard.gd`, on the Player): drawn at 1.3x around the feet so the figure is about a tile tall as in the prototype (level cells are 128 px, the collider ~62 px; collision is unchanged). It uses the spring rig from the HTML prototype, driven by the real velocity, floor, dash, climb and invulnerability state: planted feet, a cloth hem, a lagging head, eased turns, breathing and blinks.
- **Astral projection**: while phasing, the figure prints at 40% coverage on every plate, so the background shows through; the bag prints at its normal coverage throughout, with their usual countdown and warning blink. It stays translucent without hurt flashes and returns to full coverage when the 1.5–3 s projection ends. Hits cannot hurt or interrupt it. `tests/astral_moon_test.gd` checks coverage and damage immunity; `tests/capture_astral.gd` shows the normal and projected figure.
- **Robe ink** is the spell element: the bindle prints on its own plate in the ink of the damage slot's spell (`RisoPrint.ROBES`): hex burgundy, slash red, nova amber, push blue, parry flat gold, and federal blue with none. Learning a spell re-dyes it over a moment.
- **Glow ink** is the movement element: the glow plate takes the movement slot's colour (`RisoPrint.GLOWS`), so the Fool's feather and the dash's afterimages print in it (see **The Fool**).
- **Spell marks**: the bag carries the damage slot's spell (see **The Fool**). Ready, the mark is bare paper on full ink; recharging, the cloth is screened back and fills from the bottom with the charge; while a spell runs a ring drains round the mark with the time left, and in the last 1.5 s (`RisoCostume.SPELL_WARN`) it turns pink and blinks faster toward the end. The hex bolt prints in accent ink, as do the marks slash, nova and push leave as they are cast (`SpellMark`, 0.28 s, printed on top of whatever it crosses, the decor, rock and night under it lifted to paper first, as is a hex bolt's tail; moving with the wizard as they go, so they never walk out of their own swing, except a lunge's, which stays as the thrust's trail: a slash's crescent sweeping round its arc, thick at its leading edge and drawn out to a point at its trailing one (`SLASH_INNER`, `SLASH_TAPER`), a nova's ring swelling out with rays, a push's streaks blowing out across its cone, a lunge's thrust streaking out along its way; `tests/capture_damage_spells.gd`). A seeker prints as a hex bolt at `Seeker.SIZE` (0.7), its tail curling as it turns.
- **Dash** leaves afterimages: the Fool's outline every few steps along the dash, in the glow ink and fading over 0.3 s, newest strongest. Silhouettes read the same in every direction. The figure itself is not squashed. **Hits** flash the whole figure pink. **Astral projection** holds a glowing silhouette at the return point.
- **Terrain** (`RisoTerrain.gd`) is rebuilt from the TileMap's ground layer when `MapInfo` finishes a world. It is one contiguous mass with rounded outer corners, concave fillets and a thin cap strip per walkable run. Shading is two screened night bands inset 22 px and 58 px from every exposed edge, cleared around inside corners, so it follows the outline rather than the cell grid. Floating platforms print as bars that join neighbouring platforms and rock, each with its own cap strip, on a canvas of their own over the decor (`RisoTerrain.ledge_ink`, z −14, under the lanterns' light), so a plant or bookcase behind a ledge never hides it. It knocks the sky out beneath itself.
- **Background** (`RisoBackground.gd`) follows the camera: a night flood, parallax stars and a few abstract shapes per realm, drawn by that realm's backdrop (`RisoBackground._backdrops`, by realm as `RisoPrint.REALMS` holds its colours; a realm without one gets the night's orrery). Decor works the same way: an archetype names its plan (`Archetype.decor`, a `RisoDecor.PLANS` entry), and a band without one wears the garden's. Hyperspace prints in its own realm (violet rock, aqua stars), outside the F8 cycle: no skyline or still stars, just streaks of light rushing back past the view toward a glow ahead.
- **Keys and doors**: each key and door is dealt one of four key colours, printed as ink overprints (sun, ember, moss, plum). The rarer the colour (see `docs/DEEPER_PLAN.md` §3), the longer the key's shaft and the more teeth on its bit: sun one, ember two, moss three, plum four (`RisoMarks.key_shape`). A skeleton key is bone white, bare paper (`RisoPrint.ink_key`): a bone's two-knobbed end for a bow, and the shaft and bit of the other keys (three teeth, as a moss key's), in a pale halo with a paper twinkle (in night on paper, and a small grey bone on the map). A bone gate's ironwork is lifted to pale bone grey rather than blue (`RisoMarks._gate_iron`), its padlock bare white with a night bone on it, and the map marks it in grey (`tests/capture_bones.gd`). A vault is a door like any other, with its room open behind the bars so its loot shows (`tests/capture_vaults.gd`). A key waiting to be picked up floats in a soft halo of its own colour, like a star's. Every carried key trails the wizard in a chain (the HUD shows none). Doors are portcullises filling their corridor cell. Each has a header beam, four spiked iron bars, and one cross-rail hung with a padlock in the key colour it needs (`RisoMarks.padlock`: a pale body tinted with the colour, a dark shackle and keyhole; the cemetery's bells hang the same one), or, on a switch gate, the switch's emblem and the switch's sigil on a paper plate (`RisoMarks.sigil_plate`; the switch carries the same sigil on a plate on its base, and bells, vanes and parked lifts chained to a switch hang the same plate; see `docs/DEEPER_PLAN.md`, "Sigils"). Unlocking plays a short printed opening (`RisoDoorOpen.gd`): the grate winches up into the header with a shudder and a puff of dust, then fades.
- **Prompts** (`RisoPrompt.gd`): a printed paper disc pops up above the focused interactable with an interact symbol: a hand pointing down at it (`RisoPrompt.draw_hand`), one flat shape of night ink traced from a drawing of a pointing hand and turned to point down: the wrist above, the index finger pointing down and curling in a little toward its tip, the thumb laid across the three curled fingers and parted from them by paper cuts (no shading). Its points live in `RisoPrompt.HAND_PIECES` and `HAND_CUTS`; `docs/mockups/interact-hand/` keeps the same hand as an SVG and the script that draws it, to change both together. It taps about every 0.8 s (`tests/capture_prompt.gd`, `tests/capture_prompt_hands.gd`), a ring of accent ink rippling from the fingertip as it lands. On doors it shows the key colour needed instead. Only one interactable is focused at a time, the nearest the player is touching (`Interactable.focused`): only it shows a prompt and only it answers the interact key, so the shrine's neighbouring stations never both act. `tests/capture_prompt.gd` takes a strip of the tap.

What fills it is chosen on the F7 panel (`RisoPrint.portal_style`): bands of TV static (the default: thin rows of paper-and-night snow flickering about 18 times a second, in bands of a few rows, bright or dim, rolling down) or ripples on a pool of water (deep blue, rings spreading from a drip of light in the middle and fading at the rim, a sheen across the top). The wizard's rifts are torn in the air: a rim of flickering eye-yellow dashes, no threshold, a spark sigil; one waiting for its partner is a dim dashed outline that breathes. - **Thorns** (`ThornsArt.gd`): always out they are pink and printed once. They print on top of the decor round them (grass, hedges, plants): every plate under them is knocked to paper first, so their pink stays true where decor crosses them rather than sinking to brown. ## Interface

- **Surface decor** (`RisoDecor.gd`): small printed props that make the grotto feel lived in. They never affect gameplay.
  - **Floors:** grass tufts, moon-flowers, mushroom clusters, stones; in the garden, broad-leaved plants (two to four long stalks fanning from one root, each holding a big pointed leaf with a darker midrib, varied per cell, swaying), and hedges in runs of 2–5 cells along stretches of floor (a band of moss leafage with rounded tops, behind the fences).
  - **Ceilings:** hanging roots, stalactites, ink drips.
  - **Walls:** leafy vines.
  - **Placement:** every choice is a hash of the level seed and cell, so a level always wears the same decor. The world's RNG is untouched. Props stay inside the level and clear of structures (exits, shrine, doors, lanterns, thorns, portals, orbs, lifts, bells, switches, switch gates, keys, relics) and the cells either side of them, with a wider stretch kept clear by the tall ones. Decor is background: it prints at z -15 (fences at -16, the garden's hedges behind them at -17), under the lantern light (-12), every prop (2), the wizard's trail (9) and the wizard (10), so keeping it off structures means nothing of it shows over or through them.
  - **Drawing:** props are sketched and then grown ×2 from where they sprout. They are batched onto one ink canvas per 16×16 chunk and drawn once per level.
- **Sway:** plants (tufts, broad-leaved plants, moon-flowers, roots, vines) are drawn live on screen. They sway in a slow breeze and spring away from the wizard as they brush past, more so when moving fast. Lanterns rock on their hooks the same way. Static decor (stones, mushrooms) stays batched.

## Performance

Measured on a Ryzen 7 7800X3D and an RTX 4090 at 1280 × 720 with vsync off (`tests/bench_walk.gd`, the median frame at the garden, cemetery, crags, sky and institute): about 7.4 ms a frame standing and 7.8 ms walking (9.7 and 11.3 ms before the light was meshed and props reprinted every other frame, below; the institute walking 15 ms, now 9). Nearly all of a frame is GDScript on the main thread (the art building its shapes, then drawing them). On the GPU (`tests/bench_print.gd`, at 1920 × 1080) a frame is 2 to 2.6 ms at full graphics (2.1 to 3.6 ms before the print skipped empty plates) and 0.6 to 1 ms at light; an integrated GPU is something like 15 to 40 times slower, which is what light graphics are for. A level change stalls about 0.15 to 0.25 s, behind the transition's sheet, which holds 0.35 s anyway. `tests/bench_frame.gd` splits a frame (process, redraws, physics, render) at a few places and ranks the scripts by cost; `tests/bench_walk.gd` times the median frame standing and walking; `tests/bench_print.gd` times the print's passes on the GPU, one suspect off at a time, and compares the print against how it was before it skipped empty plates (`tests/proto/riso_*_ref`); `tests/bench_load.gd` times arriving at a level, step by step; `tests/bench_gen.gd` times laying levels out; `tests/bench_big_level.gd` times each part of the print; `tests/bench_chunks.gd` times level loads; `tests/bench_gpu.gd` says whether the CPU or the GPU holds a frame back and prices each part of the print on the GPU, with physics stepping as in play (several steps a frame to catch up); `tests/bench_profile.gd` ranks GDScript functions with the engine's script profiler (run it with `-d`).

On a laptop with integrated graphics (Iris Xe, i7-1165G7, maximized at 1600 × 829, light graphics; `tests/bench_gpu.gd`) a frame is about 26 ms at the start and on the largest level (29 and 33 ms before the noise table, the chunk sweep and the wisps' bands below). The GPU takes about 11 ms of it, the print's two passes 7 of that (10.5 before the noise table); at full graphics it takes 23 to 25 ms (26 to 29). The GPU and the CPU share the laptop's power: with the print running the CPU holds 1.5 to 2 GHz, and about 3 GHz with the GPU idle, where the same frame's CPU work takes 10 to 15 ms rather than 20; and a slow frame runs two or three physics steps to catch up. So time cut on the GPU is time cut on the CPU too.

- **One draw call per ink operation** (`InkOp.gd`): every shape an op holds goes into one triangle array. The terrain alone holds thousands, which had been thousands of draw calls on each ink plate.
- **Ink is only redrawn when it changes** (`InkCanvas._emit`, `RisoInkOp.Shape`): an op keeps what it printed while the art issues the same shapes, cover and scale again (most art redraws every frame, but much of what it draws holds still: light while the wizard stands, the still parts of a moving figure), comparing them against its own copy, as art may reuse its arrays. Ops printing the same shapes one after the other (ink, then the night it punches out) share one triangulation, and flat ink takes one colour rather than one per point. Drawing had been about half of a frame; it is now about a fifth.
- **Shapes that only scale and move** (`RisoShapes`: circles, ellipses, rounded rectangles, sparkles, almonds) are each worked out once at unit size, by point count, and placed with one transform, rather than point by point with a sine and cosine each.
- **Portals** keep their outline, rim and halo while they stand still, and their static between flickers. The static works its flecks' hash out inline and makes cells only for the flecks that print (`PortalArt._static_flecks`).
- **The print's noise is read from a table** (see **How it works**), not hashed for every pixel of every plate: on the laptop the print's passes went from about 10.5 ms to 7 at light graphics.
- **Chunks** (`LevelLoader.sleep_far_chunks`): the level is cut into 8 × 8 cell chunks; a few times a second each chunk wakes or sleeps by its distance from the view, or from the wizard (the camera lags a rift jump or respawn) (awake within 640 px, asleep past 896 px). Everything placed with the level in a sleeping chunk stops processing, leaves the physics world and is hidden. Bolts, ghosts and rifts always run. A sweep looks at as few things as it can (a deep level holds 7,000): what is awake (it may have walked into another chunk), what sleeps in a chunk that has just woken (what sleeps cannot move, so it is kept by the chunk it fell asleep in), what was added since the last sweep, and the few that wake from afar or spread over several chunks (worms, winds, gondolas); a sweep over every thing had cost several milliseconds every sixth physics step (3.6 ms a frame on the laptop on the largest level). `tests/chunk_sleep_test.gd` checks each thing wakes exactly while its chunk does as the view moves, things added later and things freed while asleep. An awakened worm also stays active for its pursuit across the level, even when its root's original burrow sleeps (`tests/worm_combat_test.gd`). Its full 12-cell activation range wakes it independently of those chunks, so a sleeping burrow cannot prevent emergence (`tests/worm_wake_test.gd`).
- **Props print as they come into view** (`RisoProp._process`), not all in the frame a level loads; still kinds then stop processing. Stars are printed once and animated by moving their canvases. Shields (`Shield`) print only near the view too (`RisoProp.near_view`).
- **Props reprint every other frame** (`RisoProp.REPRINT_EVERY`), half of them on even frames and half on odd ones: each follows its host every frame, so it moves smoothly, and only its shape changes thirty times a second. Kinds that draw something pinned to the world while their host moves print every frame (`every_frame`: a skeleton's planted feet, a gondola's cable, a lift's ropes, a balloon's string, a bird's mark, a hopper's trail, the stars, and anything reaching far).
- **Square corners are four points** (`RisoShapes.rrect`): a rounded rectangle with no rounding is just its four corners, and a pill keeps once the point where its two ends meet. Before, it gave twenty points, each corner five times over, which defeats the triangulator and sent every plank, slab, seam and mortar line the slow way round (Clipper untangling it first). Triangles and convex quads skip the triangulator altogether (`RisoInkOp._convex`). The institute's books, their gilt bands and the shelves are square-cornered (their rounding was under a pixel), and the decor's hash is written out rather than called through two functions: an institute level's decor went from 1.2 million points to 263 thousand (60 thousand shapes, most of them books), laying it out from 238 ms to 83 ms and its first print from 194 ms to 67 ms. Other bands' decor is 8 to 30 ms.
- **Swaying plants are bent again only as they move** (`RisoDecor._process`, `BREEZE_RATE`, `BEND_STEP`): the idle breeze steps 10 times a second (it sways them by 0.03, too little for the steps to show), and a plant keeps its last bent outline until its bend moves 0.002, so a frame with nothing brushed aside reprints none of them (about 0.1 ms a frame, from 0.5).
- **The backdrop slides rather than redraws** (`RisoBackground`): the stars and the far skyline are each laid out once and then slid with the camera (their canvases moved) until they have slid past the margin round the view (`STAR_SLACK` 60 px, `SKYLINE_SLACK` 160 px), and the realm's own shapes move on 30 times a second (`BACKDROP_RATE`; hyperspace's streaks every frame), so a still camera reprints none of it.
- **The wizard's ink** (`RisoCostume._solids`): shapes on one ink that never overlap, or are solid, print together in two ops rather than two each (the eyes, the bag's ties, each boot, the lantern's cap and base, the necklace's beads): 70 ops a frame, from 86. Shapes that only move are smoothed once and moved (`_moved`: the hair, collar, toe, each spell's bag), and the bow is worked out in one loop (`RisoWizard._sm`). Its art costs about 0.9 ms a frame standing and 1.2 ms walking (1.05 and 1.6 before).
- **Wisps** print with fewer points (their glows in 16, their bodies smoothed in 3 steps: 388 points, from 540), check a placed shape for folding over itself only on a sharp bend (`WISP_FOLD_TURN`), and further than `WISP_NEAR` (480 px) from the wizard print twenty times a second (`WISP_FAR_EVERY`, `RisoProp.reprint_every`).
- **The UI's plates are smaller than the scene's** (`RisoPrint.UI_SCALE_FULL` 0.75, `UI_SCALE_LIGHT` 0.5): the UI holds only a few small marks, but each of its nine plates costs the GPU its whole size; the print's wobble and screen hide the softer edges.
- **Rigs** (`InkCanvas.rig`, `shaders/ink_rig.gdshaderinc`): art that moves or bends draws its shapes once, in their rest pose, and places them every frame by bones on the GPU. Each shape is bound to one bone (`on_bone`) or to up to three along a chain (`on_chain`, a binder naming them for each rest point; bones and weights travel in each vertex's UV); a warp then bends the whole figure (`set_warp`: the wizard's bow and its lean against a wall, the same steps as `RisoWizard._smv`). Shapes that only move are neither rebuilt, triangulated nor sent to the renderer again; art that never changes an array it hands over sets `keeps_shapes`, and an op given the very same array keeps its shape without comparing it. `InkCanvas.rig_on_gpu` off places the same bones in script, as all art was before rigs, to compare against (`tests/capture_rig_gif.gd`, side by side, held still; `tests/bench_walk.gd -- script`). Rigged now: the wizard (`RisoCostume`: about 18 bones, the poncho, plume and folds still drawn anew; its draw 0.73 ms to 0.41 standing, 0.92 to 0.43 walking, its ops redrawn a frame 26 to 4; GPU and script prints match to 0.002/255) and the wisps (`WispArt`: a still chain and one per veil and the body, CHAIN bones each, the wave lifting a layer's bones; a print 0.36 ms to 0.18, close to the old per-point print, which `rig_on_gpu` off still draws). The scholars' rig (`ScholarArt._draw_rigged`) is written but off until it is checked against captures. Next by cost: watchers, hoppers, abominations, and the shrine, department arch and portals (`tests/proto` held the per-kind measurements). `Abilities.in_slot` is worked out once a slot (the art asked every frame).
- **Graded ink keeps its shape as it fades** (`InkCanvas._emit`): a graded op's cover at each point is scaled by the op's cover as it prints, so ink that only grows stronger or weaker is not cut into triangles again. A shape handed over already in triangles (`ink_mesh`, `lift_ink_mesh`) is never triangulated at all.
- **The light is cheap to print again** (`RisoLight`): its pools are flat (above), the still lights that print alike (the stars' sparks, the faint lanterns) print together, an op apiece, in the very arrays they printed last time while the same lights are in the group (`_batches`, `keeps_shapes`), and only the things that shine near the view are asked for their light, from an index of where they are by 512 px square (`_glows_near`, laid out again when the group changes or every second; a sky level has some 700 stars). The shade over what the wizard cannot see is worked out over the view grown `SHADE_SLACK` (192 px) and snapped to steps of it, so the camera's drift does not have it worked out again. Standing still the light costs 0.29 to 0.54 ms a frame (0.54 to 1.53 before).
- **Loading:** the layout (`LevelGen.new`) is built on the generator thread with the cells, and kept with them for revisits and the map. Rock is laid as plain tiles (the print draws its own rock; every rock tile has the same collision), and there is no background tile layer. Laying out takes 0.05 to 0.3 s a level, and about 0.65 s for the wide sky levels (it was 1 s); the neighbours are laid out ahead of time, so a transition seldom waits for it.
- **Wisps** place their bodies from a per-frame table of their trail (positions, directions and bends every 2 px) instead of walking the trail for each vertex: about 0.15 ms a wisp on screen. Each wisp looks for another ahead of it only among those near its own height (`Mover._wisp_ahead`, `WISP_BAND`), sorted once a physics step, rather than through every wisp in the level (1.9 ms a frame on the laptop on the largest level).

## Tests and captures

```
godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/riso_print_test.gd
godot --path . --script res://tests/capture_riso.gd
```

The headless test boots seed 28 and checks plates, stretch mode, cull mask, dressing of every spawned prefab, the dash smear, hat glow for dash and parry, the hurt state, the astral ghost, sheet advance, realm cycling and a clean off/on toggle. The capture script walks, jumps and dashes through the generated world in a 1280×720 window and writes frames to `../art-captures/riso-frames`.

## Limits

- Presentation only: no collision, physics or generation changes. The only gameplay-file edit is one notify call at the end of `MapInfo._arrived()`.
- Menus keep their Godot controls for layout and input; RisoMenu prints them.
- Prop art is sized to the current prefab scales (cells are 128 world px). Standing props measure their own ground each redraw, because checkpoints shift themselves after spawning; spikes stand on the surface they are rotated toward. A prefab added later needs an entry in `RisoPrint.DRESS` and a drawing in `RisoProp.gd`, or it stays invisible under the print.
