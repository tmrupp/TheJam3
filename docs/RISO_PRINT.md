# Riso print presentation

The main game is presented as a risograph "tarot print": flat ink shapes drawn in code, printed by a shader with misregistration, wobbling edges, dot gain, a grainy halftone and uneven laydown. It is always on: the prefabs keep their old sprites, but they sit under the print, unseen.

## Controls

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| F7 | Print controls (scrolls; grouped in sections that fold open or shut from their headers, a click or A: Travel (debug runs) and Print start open, Look, Keys and Abilities folded, and which are open is remembered while the game runs; `tests/capture_panel.gd` takes stills): print detail, sheet rate, zoom, plate offset, UI detail, specks (missed-ink flecks of bare paper; 20% by default), registration (new sheet / locked / drift), reprint on clock or motion, cut or blend between sheets, realm, plates (independent / trapped), portals (TV static / ripples), light by sight (rock stops it / off), cemetery fog, sky bottoms (Roots, Clouds, Clouds & roots; live cosmetic changes), a Keys section to equip or remove each shaped key and adjust skeleton keys (respecting keyring capacity), and an Abilities section that sets any ability's tier outright (a spell above none takes the slot) |
| F8 | Cycle realm (deep night, twilight, aurora) |

Defaults: deep night realm, independent plates, fine detail, new sheet registration at 8 sheets/s, reprint on motion (landings, take-offs, dashes, hits and portals print a fresh sheet), hard cuts between sheets, and the camera zoomed out to 0.72× of the scene zoom.

## How it works

- `scripts/riso/RisoPrint.gd` is added as the first child of `Main`. It creates eight plate `SubViewport`s (night, blue, pink, accent, eye yellow, hat glow, robe, traveler cloth) plus an overlay viewport. All of them share the game `World2D`, and each one culls to its own canvas visibility layer (bits 12–19; 20 is the overlay). A full-screen `ColorRect` on canvas layer 1 runs `shaders/riso_print.gdshader` over the plates. The HUD and menus draw above it. Traveler cloth uses fixed federal blue (`CLOTH_INK`), independent of the realm's terrain ink and the carried spell's ink.
- Art nodes lay ink *coverage* on a plate: alpha is coverage, so a solid is 1 and a "25" tint is 0.25. By default each ink also lifts the same coverage off the night plate. `knock()` clears plates to bare paper (multiply blend), which is how the eyes, lantern and hat bead read as light. `InkCanvas` keeps these operations ordered; plate order across nodes comes from `z_index`.
- A viewport only renders an item whose ancestors all share its layer, so every `InkCanvas` ORs the ink bits into its ancestors (`RisoPrint.share_layers`). Ancestors draw nothing themselves, and legacy sprites keep only layer 1, so they never reach the plates. The main viewport's cull mask hides the ink bits, and the opaque print covers the legacy art.
- The print is pinned to the world. `RisoPrint` passes the camera's pixel offset (`pin`), and every imperfection is evaluated at screen pixel + `pin`. That covers the halftone screen, grain, edge wobble, laydown, specks and paper texture. So as the camera moves, they stay on the paper instead of sliding over it. Only a new sheet reprints them. The noise uses an exact integer hash (PCG) with its domains turned off the pixel axes. The earlier `fract()` hash lost float precision at screen-sized coordinates, which lined specks up in columns. `tests/riso_pin_test.gd` (windowed) checks that a camera move shifts the printed rock by exactly the camera's pixel shift.
- While printing, the window uses `canvas_items` stretch, so the world renders at full resolution while UI keeps its 320×180 layout. Plates are capped at 1080 lines; the shader samples them by UV.
- Every plate registers on its own, as in the prototype (pull 10): its own offset, rotation and edge wobble, with the prototype's offsets (about 0.3–1.4 px at 720p) and up to ±0.6 px more per new sheet. Where inks meet, a paper sliver opens on one side and the inks overlap dark on the other, shifting with each sheet. **Plates: Trapped** on the F7 panel instead registers night to blue and shares one wobble across all plates, so those edges close up. `tests/capture_plates.gd` prints the same frame both ways side by side.
- The print-detail stops (screen size, wobble, grain, dot gain, laydown) are the prototype's.
- **UI print**: the HUD, interaction prompts, in-world plaques (prices, shrine pop-ups) and the printed map (on top of them all) draw in a canvas of their own (`RisoPrint.ui_canvas`, `InkCanvas.ui`), with its own eight ink plates plus a paper plate. A second pass of the same print (`shaders/riso_ui.gdshader`, sharing `riso_core.gdshaderinc`) lays it over the scene: the same inks and sheets, but finer (the **UI detail** slider on the F7 panel, default 70%, runs from printing like the scene to screen cell ×0.35, wobble ×0.1, grain ×0.4, dot gain ×0.3 and misregistration ×0.1; `UI_*` in `RisoPrint`) and pinned to the screen, so small marks and text stay legible. Knocks there lay paper, so plaques and discs print as paper over the scene.
- **Parry** (`RisoWizard._draw_guard`, `Parry`): raised with every damage spell as it is cast, the guard prints over the figure and is the same all round, as it guards every side. It is a thin bright band of gleam, in a softer wider one, sweeping across the figure once over the window, as light runs over polished metal, with no bubble or ring round it. A catch bursts in spell light where the hit landed (`RisoFx` `&"parry"`: a flash, sparks thrown back the way the hit came, a ring of motes), and while the parry's invulnerability lasts the figure shimmers gold instead of the hurt pink (`RisoWizard.parry_glory`).
- A hit or death prints a 15% pink screen over the whole sheet for a moment (`RisoHud.flash`), on top of the wizard's own pink hit flash.

## Art

The worm's pink head has a Pac-Man wedge mouth cut out of its rounded front, centered on
the segment's solid circle, showing what is behind it. The front and bite use that same radius;
the mouth only removes flesh, so the head never extends past it. The wedge's root stays ahead
of the centre, clear of the health dots. Paper teeth line both jaws, those of one between those
of the other, so shut they mesh in a zigzag along the seam (`Worm._teeth`). It chomps
(`Worm._gape`): near the wizard the jaws ease open and snap shut, quicker and wider the nearer they
are (`CHOMP_SLOW` to `CHOMP_FAST` a second), and far off hold still a little open. Biting, they
spring wide and slam shut in 0.08 s (`BITE_SNAP`), throwing pink strokes out from the jaws that
fade over 0.3 s, and stay shut through the 0.65 s rest after; stunned, they hang nearly shut. The
front is clipped to the open air at rock faces. Striking (`Worm._strike_reach`, `_strike_gape`), it
pauses with its jaws shut, draws its head back into its neck as they open wide, lunges out along
its path and snaps them shut at full reach, then settles back. Its thorns, on alternate flanks
from one segment to the next, grow from fixed places along the body (four to a cell a side), so they ride along as it crawls: fanned apart round the
outside of a bend, shrinking smoothly away on its squeezed inside, and growing in or out as the
flank turns toward or away from the side they face, never popping (`Worm.thorn_spots`). Each shows
wherever the flesh it grows from is out in the open, so they go into a hole with it, one by one;
nothing shows through the rock. The half of each segment toward its thorns is speckled with
small plates of the thorns' pink lying back toward the tail (`Worm.scale_plates`), a rough hide
where it cannot be cut; the bare half is plain soft flesh. A burrow
hole is the ground pushed up round a dark pit: shoulders of the rock's own ink rising to a rim
either side of the worm's width, turfed like the floor's cap strip where the hole is in a floor,
with crumbs of freshly dug pale earth among clods of the ground's own (`Worm._draw_hole`), all
only over open air, so at the end of a space a cell wide it heaps against the walls. A hole
it digs into a wall appears as its nose reaches the face. `tests/capture_worm.gd` takes stills,
and strips of its chomps, a strike, a crawl round a bend and a dig. `tests/worm_test.gd`
checks the front against the rock and collider; `tests/worm_combat_test.gd` checks the bite
shape, recovery and real dash contact; `tests/capture_worm.gd` takes stills.

**The Fool** is the one figure the player is printed as (the wizard, the Arcane cosmonaut and the Mask shaman are gone, and so is the F7 choice between them). `RisoCostume` draws them on `RisoWizard`'s body canvas, using its feet, bow, lean and cloth springs: a broad poncho, a beret with a curved feather, floating gloves without arms, a carried lantern in one hand and, over the other shoulder, a knotted cloth bindle whose shape, folds, ink and arcane mark change with the damage slot's spell. The **feather** is the movement slot (`feather_plate`): in the movement ability's glow ink while it is ready (dash pink, blink violet, astral teal, levitate mint, rift violet, warp coral, ferry green: `RisoPrint.GLOWS`, the glow plate taking the slot's colour), dark when spent or recharging, and in a running movement spell's last 1.5 s (`SPELL_WARN`) it blinks pink and dark, faster toward the end. Nothing on the figure stands for the **utility** slot: there is no standing indicator, only feedback on use. It is seen when learned at a shrine, on the map and pause screens, and on the sitting card (see **Printed HUD**). Pressing Utility when the spell cannot work (cooling down, no draught left, nothing to heal) sputters: a dull puff of night and blue from the bag's knot and a dull click (the hurt sound played low and quiet, `RisoPrint.FIZZLE_*`; the `fizzle` visual event).

Their necklace shows current and maximum health, including vigor's six hearts. A warm lantern flame means spawn protection; a cold wick means the run is vulnerable, independently of health and dash. An unlit lantern leaves a wispy pink trail at its past world positions, one point every 0.06 s, fading over 1.4 s; it clears on teleport and uses no world RNG. The bag shows recharge fill, running time and the last-seconds pink warning, and stays legible during astral translucency. Dash echoes, astral return marks, death ghosts and portal trips use the Fool's outline (`RisoCostume.silhouette`). `tests/fool_test.gd` checks the state cues, every damage spell's bag, the feather's ink and its blink, nothing printed for the utility spell, smoke, unchanged collision and RNG and effects. `tests/capture_characters.gd` takes the states side by side and a gameplay-scale view under `docs/mockups/wizard-replacements/game-captures/`, beside the concept sheets.

Cemetery fog can switch live between Original bands, Torn ribbons (the default), Billowing bank, Ragged ink, Spectral billows, Smoke plumes and Billows & ribbons, using the F7 **Fog shape** dropdown. These options change the cloud's outer silhouette and negative space: separated torn wisps, rounded lobes, jagged edges, tall spectral crests or curling plumes. The same translucent print treatment follows each contour, with no common oval envelope or interior eyes, stitches or particles. The drift, sleep boundary and spell suppression still come from `SleepFog`. The choice lasts for the current session and carries across level changes, like the portal style. `tests/fog_styles_test.gd` checks every menu option, distinct animated contours and the sleep effect; run with a renderer for comparison frames and the F7 selector in `art-captures/fog-shapes`.

Torn ribbons and the ribbons in the blend have a wider pink band along their upper edge. Both the pale body and pink edge fade to zero over roughly the outer quarter at each end, using graded ink coverage while retaining the torn contour and curling motion.

- **Motion: bend, never squash.** Nothing printed squashes or stretches; things bend, sway and trail like cloth on springs. The figure bows above the waist (`RisoWizard.bow`, bending more the higher up): forward over its feet on landing, back on take-off, flicked out like cloth coming out of a portal, while the poncho's hem, the feather and the lean keep their own springs. The hopper curls forward to crouch, arches through the air and is thrown forward on landing, its thorns whipping after on a looser spring (`RisoMarks.bent`). A wraith's shroud streams back from the way it drifts. Moths' wings beat on a hinge at the body. Stars rock on their points instead of flipping like coins. A portal or warp trip whirls the silhouette into the core and unwinds it out (`RisoPortalWarp._silhouette`). Plants, lanterns and the bell already bent and swung. `tests/capture_motion.gd` takes a strip of a jump and a warp.
- **The rig** (`RisoWizard.gd`, on the Player): drawn at 1.3x around the feet so the figure is about a tile tall as in the prototype (level cells are 128 px, the collider ~62 px; collision is unchanged). It uses the spring rig from the HTML prototype, driven by the real velocity, floor, dash, climb and invulnerability state: planted feet, a cloth hem, a lagging head, eased turns, breathing and blinks.
- **Astral projection**: while phasing, the figure prints at 40% coverage on every plate, so the background shows through; the bag prints at its normal coverage throughout, with their usual countdown and warning blink. It stays translucent without hurt flashes and returns to full coverage when the 1.5–3 s projection ends. Hits cannot hurt or interrupt it. `tests/astral_moon_test.gd` checks coverage and damage immunity; `tests/capture_astral.gd` shows the normal and projected figure.
- **Robe ink** is the spell element: the bindle prints on its own plate in the ink of the damage slot's spell (`RisoPrint.ROBES`): hex burgundy, slash red, nova amber, push blue, parry flat gold, and federal blue with none. Learning a spell re-dyes it over a moment.
- **Glow ink** is the movement element: the glow plate takes the movement slot's colour (`RisoPrint.GLOWS`), so the Fool's feather and the dash's afterimages print in it (see **The Fool**).
- **Spell marks**: the bag carries the damage slot's spell (see **The Fool**). Ready, the mark is bare paper on full ink; recharging, the cloth is screened back and fills from the bottom with the charge; while a spell runs a ring drains round the mark with the time left, and in the last 1.5 s (`RisoCostume.SPELL_WARN`) it turns pink and blinks faster toward the end. The hex bolt prints in accent ink, as do the marks slash, nova and push leave as they are cast (`SpellMark`, 0.28 s: a slash's crescent sweeping round its arc, a nova's ring swelling out with rays, a push's streaks blowing out across its cone, a lunge's thrust streaking out along its way; `tests/capture_damage_spells.gd`). A seeker prints as a hex bolt at `Seeker.SIZE` (0.7), its tail curling as it turns.
- **Dash** leaves afterimages: the Fool's outline every few steps along the dash, in the glow ink and fading over 0.3 s, newest strongest. Silhouettes read the same in every direction. The figure itself is not squashed. **Hits** flash the whole figure pink. **Astral projection** holds a glowing silhouette at the return point.
- **Terrain** (`RisoTerrain.gd`) is rebuilt from the TileMap's ground layer when `MapInfo` finishes a world. It is one contiguous mass with rounded outer corners, concave fillets and a thin cap strip per walkable run. Shading is two screened night bands inset 22 px and 58 px from every exposed edge, cleared around inside corners, so it follows the outline rather than the cell grid. Floating platforms print with the terrain as bars that join neighbouring platforms and rock and share the cap strip. It knocks the sky out beneath itself.
- **Background** (`RisoBackground.gd`) follows the camera: a night flood, parallax stars and a few abstract shapes per realm, drawn by that realm's backdrop (`RisoBackground._backdrops`, by realm as `RisoPrint.REALMS` holds its colours; a realm without one gets the night's orrery). Decor works the same way: an archetype names its plan (`Archetype.decor`, a `RisoDecor.PLANS` entry), and a band without one wears the garden's. Hyperspace prints in its own realm (violet rock, aqua stars), outside the F8 cycle: no skyline or still stars, just streaks of light rushing back past the view toward a glow ahead.
- **Keys and doors**: each key and door is dealt one of four key colours, printed as ink overprints (sun, ember, moss, plum). The rarer the colour (see `docs/DEEPER_PLAN.md` §3), the longer the key's shaft and the more teeth on its bit: sun one, ember two, moss three, plum four (`RisoMarks.key_shape`). A skeleton key is bone white, bare paper (`RisoPrint.ink_key`): a bone's two-knobbed end for a bow, and the shaft and bit of the other keys (three teeth, as a moss key's), in a pale halo with a paper twinkle (in night on paper, and a small grey bone on the map). A bone gate's ironwork is lifted to pale bone grey rather than blue (`RisoMarks._gate_iron`), its padlock bare white with a night bone on it, and the map marks it in grey (`tests/capture_bones.gd`). A vault is a door like any other, with its room open behind the bars so its loot shows (`tests/capture_vaults.gd`). A key waiting to be picked up floats in a soft halo of its own colour, like a star's. Every carried key trails the wizard in a chain (the HUD shows none). Doors are portcullises filling their corridor cell. Each has a header beam, four spiked iron bars, and one cross-rail hung with a padlock in the key colour it needs (`RisoMarks.padlock`: a pale body tinted with the colour, a dark shackle and keyhole; the cemetery's bells hang the same one), or, on a switch gate, the switch's emblem and the switch's sigil on a paper plate (`RisoMarks.sigil_plate`; the switch carries the same sigil on a plate on its base, and bells, vanes and parked lifts chained to a switch hang the same plate; see `docs/DEEPER_PLAN.md`, "Sigils"). Unlocking plays a short printed opening (`RisoDoorOpen.gd`): the grate winches up into the header with a shudder and a puff of dust, then fades.
- **Prompts** (`RisoPrompt.gd`): a printed paper disc pops up above the focused interactable with an interact symbol: a hand pointing down at it (a blue cuff, three curled fingers, the index finger with a paper nail) that taps about every 0.8 s, a ring of accent ink rippling from the fingertip as it lands. On doors it shows the key colour needed instead. Only one interactable is focused at a time, the nearest the player is touching (`Interactable.focused`): only it shows a prompt and only it answers the interact key, so the shrine's neighbouring stations never both act. `tests/capture_prompt.gd` takes a strip of the tap.
- **Props** are attached by a dresser by each prefab's art kind (`Placeables.TABLE`, `RisoPrint.DRESS` for the rest); `RisoProp.gd` is what they share (printing in view, plaques, pop-ups) and each kind's look is its own script in `scripts/riso/props/` (`RisoProp.KINDS`), with marks shared with the map and HUD in `RisoMarks.gd` and ability marks in `RisoGlyph.gd`: star motes, moons (an accent crescent in a halo that shrinks to a faint sliver while it wanes), the level's ink well (a big gold-rimmed pot under a beam of light and a pulsing halo, with a rolled map floating over it circled by motes, a drop of ink rising and falling, and a price plaque; dry once paid), crescent key, portals (an upright doorway about the wizard's height, shaped as a stadium: half circles top and bottom joined by straight sides. What fills it is chosen on the F7 panel (`RisoPrint.portal_style`): bands of TV static (the default: thin rows of paper-and-night snow flickering about 18 times a second, in bands of a few rows, bright or dim, rolling down) or ripples on a pool of water (deep blue, rings spreading from a drip of light in the middle and fading at the rim, a sheen across the top). A level's own are gates: a solid accent rim on a stone threshold with light pooled on the floor, and a sigil on a paper disc on top (one of the 12 in `RisoMarks.sigil`) shared by both ends of a pair and by no other link in the level while there are sigils to go round. The wizard's rifts are torn in the air: a rim of flickering eye-yellow dashes, no threshold, a spark sigil; one waiting for its partner is a dim dashed outline that breathes. With the wizard right at it (it takes standing close), the inside slows to about a third of its speed, as if it waits for them, and the rim brightens; a portal flares when someone comes through. A trip (`portal.gd`, `RisoPortalWarp.gd`) is three beats, the wizard held still: in, 0.3 s, the wizard's glow silhouette stretched tall and thin and pulled into the core along converging streaks, ending in a flash; a cut, the camera snapping to the far portal; out, the far core flashing, a sliver of light opening into the silhouette and dropping to the wizard's feet as they reappear (popped out tall, springing back), with a ring, streaks and motes bursting outward. `tests/capture_portals.gd` and `tests/capture_portal_warp.gd` take stills), portcullis doors, lantern checkpoint (an ember when unclaimed, fully lit when it is your respawn), level exits (a lit doorway under a chevron pointing where it leads; an unpaid deeper exit has a pink frame, stays dark, and prints its price on a paper plaque), the shrine (a plinth across two cells with three stations side by side: two niches where the offered ability's mark floats over its tier pips, and a bowl holding an ember bead for mending; stepping up to one pops up a larger plaque with its name, tier and price in thickened night ink; it dims once spent), floating ledge, straight thorns (rotated with the spikes), the death ghost (the wizard's astral silhouette in glow ink, with a dark hood and lit eyes, and the stars it holds circling it), cracked walls (printed as rock by the terrain, with a few fine night-ink cracks; the rock reprints when one breaks), the hex bolt (a crimson glow-ink comet with a tapering tail), Wisp nightmare (stunned eyes close), the watching eye shooter, the hopper (a squat pink toad of a nightmare with a ridge of thorns and paper eye slits that squashes to crouch, stretches as it leaps over its shadow and slumps as it lands), the laser (a dark housing set in the rock; warming up, a flickering pink sight line; firing, a broad pink beam with a bare-paper core and a splash where it strikes), and shard bullets. Interaction prompts and cooldown rings move to the overlay so they stay readable. Only on-screen props animate. ## Interface

- **Surface decor** (`RisoDecor.gd`): small printed props that make the grotto feel lived in. They never affect gameplay.
  - **Floors:** grass tufts, moon-flowers, mushroom clusters, stones; in the garden, broad-leaved plants (two to four long stalks fanning from one root, each holding a big pointed leaf with a darker midrib, varied per cell, swaying), and hedges in runs of 2–5 cells along stretches of floor (a band of moss leafage with rounded tops, behind the fences).
  - **Ceilings:** hanging roots, stalactites, ink drips.
  - **Walls:** leafy vines.
  - **Placement:** every choice is a hash of the level seed and cell, so a level always wears the same decor. The world's RNG is untouched. Props stay inside the level and clear of structures (exits, shrine, doors, lanterns, thorns, portals, orbs, lifts, bells, switches, switch gates, keys, relics) and the cells either side of them, with a wider stretch kept clear by the tall ones. Decor is background: it prints at z -15 (fences at -16, the garden's hedges behind them at -17), under the lantern light (-12), every prop (2), the wizard's trail (9) and the wizard (10), so keeping it off structures means nothing of it shows over or through them.
  - **Drawing:** props are sketched and then grown ×2 from where they sprout. They are batched onto one ink canvas per 16×16 chunk and drawn once per level.
- **Sway:** plants (tufts, broad-leaved plants, moon-flowers, roots, vines) are drawn live on screen. They sway in a slow breeze and spring away from the wizard as they brush past, more so when moving fast. Lanterns rock on their hooks the same way. Static decor (stones, mushrooms) stays batched.
- **Awareness pointers** (`RisoHud`): arrows at the edge of the view with a mark for each sensed thing off screen. Exit arches are pink for deeper; the ink well, shrine and keys get their own marks. Overlapping pointers are dropped.
- **Lantern light** (`RisoLight.gd`): every light a lantern throws lifts the night ink most at its flame and fades smoothly to nothing at its reach (`carried_falloff`: graded ink in small steps fanning out from the flame, `FADE_STEP`; a `RisoLight.Fade`). The lit respawn lantern's is wide and flickers softly (`RESPAWN_REACH`, `RESPAWN_LIFT`). It absorbs one death, then stays dark for the rest of the run with empty glass and a charred wick. Other unspent lanterns give a faint one (`FAINT_REACH`). The lantern the wizard carries lights the way too: while a lit lantern protects them, a warm light fading the same way follows its flame wherever they go (`CARRIED_REACH`, `CARRIED_LIFT`); unprotected, only a short, dim one faintly tinted pink (`UNLIT_REACH`). Swung into a wall, the flame's light is taken back to the rock's face so it never shines through. Everything else that shines gives the same diffuse light, fading smoothly from where it shines (`GLOW_REACH`/`GLOW_LIFT`, `BOLT_REACH`/`BOLT_LIFT`; they join the `RisoLight.GLOWS` group): open exits (an exit's door once its way is open), portals and rifts, an ink well not yet dry, and hex bolts in flight. The small things that shine no longer print flat halos: each throws a light of the same kind, cut by the rock and faintly tinted in its own ink (`RisoProp.light`, `shines`): a star a small spark (`RisoLight.SPARK`); a key (in its colour), a full moon, a freed bell or vane and a thrown switch a gleam (`GLEAM`); a star cluster, a relic, a shrine and an opened temple a beacon (`BEACON`). Uses `InkCanvas.lift_ink`, a partial knock. `tests/capture_carried_light.gd` takes stills.
- **Line of sight** (`RisoSight.gd`, `RisoLight`): rock stops light and sight. `RisoSight` works over the level's cells (the TileMap's ground, plus cracked walls until they break, shut doors and shut switch gates, kept up to date each frame): rays to every corner of the rock in a box, and just either side of each, outline what a point sees as one polygon, reaching `FACE` (26 px) into the faces of the rock it meets so a lit wall's face reads as lit, but never out the far side of the rock, so a corner's shadow starts at the corner. Every light is cut to what its flame sees (a still light's outline is kept until the rock changes), so walls cast hard-edged shadows. The wizard sees from their eyes to the edge of the view; what they cannot see (buried rock, caves behind a wall, and everything in them) lies under more night ink, the place's `shade` (`Archetype.shade`: 0.3, the catacombs 0.6), printed at z 8 over the props and under the wizard's trail, so it stays there to make out but dimmed. The catacombs are also deep dark (`Archetype.gloom`, 0.6): night ink over everything no light falls on, in sight or not, lifting smoothly with each light (where lights overlap, one prints the dark and the others leave it out), so most of what is seen is what a lantern lights and an unprotected wizard's short dim light is felt. The print fills only simple outlines, so the dark round a seen polygon is split into pieces with no holes (`RisoSight.outside`). **Light by sight** on the F7 panel (Look) turns the cut and the shade off to compare (the deep dark stays). It costs under 1 ms a frame in the garden and about 2.5 ms in the catacombs (up to 4 ms moving near a lantern). `tests/sight_test.gd` covers it; `tests/capture_sight.gd` takes stills (the garden with it on and off, the catacombs lit and unlit) and prints its cost.
- **Crypts** (`Crypts`): their insides are deep dark in every band (`Crypts.DARK`, 0.94), laid over each crypt's inside alone (`RisoLight.crypt_rooms`), the lights cut to it and lifting it as in the catacombs, so the terraces outside stay moonlit. Their walls print as dressed stone (`ashlar`, as the crags' masonry) and their insides as a back wall of stone; inside lie only urns, rubble, broken columns and cobwebs. The necromancer's eyes and candle flames print on a canvas of their own just over the dark (z 9), so in the dark they are all of it that shows. `tests/capture_crypt.gd` takes stills.
- **The cemetery's things** (`SnufferArt`, `SkeletonArt`, the necromancer's own art): a snuffing font is a blue stone basin with a bare-paper lip round black water and a bright snuffer's cone hung over it, a glint and a thread of cold air rising while the flame can be put out; a skeleton is a stick figure of bare bones lifted to paper (a bare round skull with two sockets, a spine, two ribs, a pelvis, limbs of two bones each, the arms hung from the ends of the top rib, the far leg and arm fainter), lying in its niche asleep and swinging up onto its feet as it rises; its limbs are posed by two-bone inverse kinematics, the feet taking turns, each planted until it is half a stride behind the hip and then stepping in an arc to half a stride ahead of where the hip will be as it lands, so the feet straddle the hip; the body lifts and the torso rocks with each step, the skull follows a moment late, bobbing and tilting, and the arms hang at its sides, each swinging opposite its own side's foot; a pink point burns in each socket once awake, printed over the darkness so it shows in a dark crypt (`tests/capture_skeletons.gd` takes a still of it asleep, rising and standing, `tests/capture_skeleton_gif.gd` the frames of its walk and how far its feet sit from under its hip); the necromancer is a hooded figure with a staff crowned with candles, pink flames on those still lit, a shadow in the dark and a pale robe in light. A wraith frozen in light cowers, drawn in and shivering, its eyes shut. A light gate is a portcullis whose cross-rail plate shows a yellow sun (a sun gate) or a pale crescent on night ink (a shade gate).
- **Plated guards** (`RisoWard.gd`): an enemy's shield (`Shield`, pink) and the wizard's ward (`Ward`, eye yellow) print the same way: a ring of curved plates, one per hit left, turning slowly, with wide gaps between them (`PLATE_SHARE`) so the plates left can be counted, the night and rock lifted underneath so they read bright over both, each with a paper glint, over a faint veil; struck, the ring flashes, and a broken plate flies apart in shards. An enemy's prints solid, centred on the middle of what its art draws (`RisoProp.guard_center`: a wisp's body trails behind its origin); the wizard's prints faint (`Ward.COVER`, thinner plates) so it never competes with the figure, flaring fully only when struck. A ward's charge growing back prints its plates faintly. `tests/capture_ward.gd` takes stills.
- **The bramble** (`Bramble`, the garden's way-up boss) prints itself on one canvas at the props' z (2), in the boss's rules: danger pink, weak points accent, health countable. Its knot fills the head of the shaft: a dark green mass (darker than rock, lighter than the open shaft, so it reads as solid) under thick stems of bark twisted across it, with pink thorns lifted bright off the bark and longer thorns along its underside, and a pip of accent for each bulb on a dark knot-hole (dark once burst). Roots of bark creep down the walls' faces to the bulbs and vines. A bulb is an accent swelling cupped in bark at the rock face, a paper gleam on it, its hits left as dark dots; it swells as it readies a seed and flashes pink when struck, and bursts into a dark husk with accent petals flying. A vine out is a writhing pink stem tapering from its root, thorns on both edges and a hooked tip; warning, three pink shoots tremble out of the rock; withered, a short dry curl of bark. Its seeds are spinning pink seeds with a dark eye (`ShardArt`). As the knot tears open its two halves pull apart toward the walls and shrink, and everything fades, ink lifted in step (never knocked to bare paper mid-fade). `tests/capture_bramble.gd` takes stills.
- **The spider** (`Spider`, the crags' boss, in its keep) prints itself, its webs and its canopy on one canvas at the props' z (2). Its body is dark (night with blue), big (`Spider.ART`), one look in every world: a black widow's abdomen with a paper gleam and a pink hourglass, pink fangs that spread as it rears and bites (half as it rears to spit). Its eight legs are set in poses, not worked out step by step (`Spider._legs`): each a femur and a shank at fixed angles about its hip, spread as it hangs and walks the canopy, drawn in (`curl`) on its thread, on its back, stunned and dying; walking, they swing in two sets of four in turn, a stride for every 150 px it goes; on a floor, seen from the side, its hips sit along its body and each leg arches up over its back to a foot on the floor ahead or behind (`FLOOR_FEET`), stepping as it goes. Its curl, its stance and its stride ease in (`POSE_EASE`, its pace smoothed), so it never jumps from one pose to the next. `tests/capture_spider_walk.gd` takes sheets of it walking the canopy and scurrying a floor. Its eyes are its health: two rows of four, knocked to bare paper, washed accent while it is open to strikes, inked dark once put out (the newest flashing pink). Webs are spun as the decor's cobwebs are and printed as they are, bare paper lifted off the keep's dark (no outline), with the decor's strands (as wide: `RisoDecor.WEB_W`, `WEB_RING_W`) at 1.2 times the decor's spacing on screen whatever their size (`Spider.plain_web`; `RisoDecor.WEB_RING_GAP` and `WEB_SPOKE_GAP`, worked out from the cobweb's own measures grown by `PROP_SCALE`, times `Spider.WEB_SPREAD`, 1.2), so a big web has more lines than a small one but never a mesh. Rings are strung straight from spoke to spoke about 16 px apart, the outer one 0.8 of the way out. Spokes stay about as far apart all the way out, as a real web's do: 6 to a whole turn run in to the hub, and wherever those about a ring have drawn 1.4 times their spacing apart (`SPLIT`), a new spoke starts from that ring between each two. Each web is a little uneven, hashed from where it is (no RNG): its spokes turned a little and each its own length, each ring a little off true where it meets a spoke, and a few pieces of ring left out. A splat and a hole's webs join what they touch, and only that (`Spider._moor`, `ADJACENT`): rock or another web no further from their rim than one ring's spacing (about 16 px). Against rock each spoke runs to the rock's face (found to a pixel) and stops, the rings past it left off, so the web lies flat against the rock as if spun on it. Two webs that touch share the line where they meet (the one their edges cross on, or would): each one's spokes run to it and stop, its rings stop short of it, and one thread runs along it (`_draw_seams`), so they read as one web. Webs further apart stay apart: nothing reaches across open air. A web across a hole is two splats' worth of round web side by side, joined to each other and the floor's ends; its spokes reach out and its rings after them as it is spun again; torn, a few ragged strands hang from the hole's edges. Festoons sag along the roof, and a quarter web 2.8 cells long sits in each top corner. A splat (a spat glob, stuck) is a small round web, fading as it comes away. Stunned, accent stars circle its head; dying it curls up, shrinks and fades. `tests/capture_spider.gd` takes stills of the fight, `tests/capture_spider_webs.gd` a row of its webs beside a decor cobweb, and `tests/capture_spider_brood.gd` a sheet of a hole's web, a glob, a splat, an egg sac, spiderlings and a keep crossbow.
- **Ambient life** (`RisoAmbient.gd`), only on screen:
  - paper-white fireflies fading in and out over flowers and grass
  - ink drops falling from ceiling drips and splashing where they land
- **Distant skyline** (`RisoBackground.gd`): ruined colonnades, towers and floating islands in a faint blue screen, sliding at a tenth of the camera's speed.
- **Enemy anchors:** wisps cast a night shadow on the floor, and watchers grow on a swaying stalk rooted in the floor.
- **A wisp carried** by what it stands on (a gondola's car, a lift; `Mover.carried`) has the path its body follows carried with it, so riding up or down never bends its body as walking would; only a real fall (`Mover.falling`) straightens it, and a path started afresh always lies level behind it. `tests/wisp_ride_test.gd` covers it; `tests/capture_gondola_wisp.gd` takes a sheet of one riding.

- **Visual language:**
  - **Colours:** pink is danger (thorns, wisps, watchers), yellow is reward (stars, keys, price plaques), glow ink is the player's own (the dash's afterimages, the ghost), and blue is the world. Decor uses only blue, night, moss (accent over blue) and bare paper, never pink or yellow.
  - **Shapes:** points hurt, rounds are collected, arches are ways through.
  - **Breakables:** thin night-ink lines in rock only ever mean a cracked wall; nothing else is drawn inside the rock.
- **Level transitions** (`RisoTransition.gd`): travelling between levels, or respawning in another level, sweeps a sheet of night ink with a ragged edge across the view like passing scenery: up when going deeper, down going back, sideways through the side doors. It holds for at least 0.35 s with the destination's marks and numbers on a paper plaque (see **Places without words**), then sweeps on and away. Coming into a new region (and at a run's start) its title card follows once the sheet is gone: the region's name in night serif on a paper banner across the top of the view between two short rules, rising in, held 2 s and fading (`RisoTransition.title`). `tests/capture_transition.gd` takes stills.
- **Camera kicks:** hex hits shake the camera a little, kills a little more, and breaking a cracked wall most. A bolt ending against rock gives a faint tick.
- **Printed map** (`RisoMap.gd`): a paper sheet over the view, opened with M. The level view prints seen rock in blue ink and explored ground as a light blue screen, from one-texel-per-cell textures on the blue plate. It marks exits, the shrine, lanterns, teleporters, doors, keys, the ghost and the wizard with small abstract marks printed over what is under them (never knocking it out), and its legend lists only what the page shows (see `docs/DEEPER_PLAN.md` §7). On the worlds page a cursor picks any visited level (W/A/S/D or the arrows; A past the leftmost tile turns back), and E, Space or Enter, or a click on its tile, opens that level's map as far as it was seen, rebuilt from its seed (`MapInfo.world_at`), with what is in it as its record leaves it: exits, shrine, lanterns, ink well, teleporters, keys (and keys dropped there) and doors in their colours (as the level dealt them, `RisoMap.dealt_colors`), gates, switches, relics, star clusters, bells, vanes and bridges. What they showed is in the game: health is the traveler's necklace, protection their lantern (lit, or a dim pulsing pink while unprotected), keys trail behind them, and the place shows when sitting and on the level transition. A place prints as a globe (a disc banded by two paper latitudes) before the world's number, a thick down arrow before the depth's, and, in a side world, an upright portal mark after them (its depth is that of the level it hangs off). Near the top of the view the card hangs lower rather than off the edge. The UI print is pinned to the screen, so text gliding across it shimmers: sitting pans the camera down, and the card is put on whole pixels where the traveler will be once the view settles (`RisoHud._settling`) and held there (`CARD_SLACK`).   - **Awareness pointers**, as below, and the **end card** when a run ends: the ghost over the run's world and the deepest depth reached.
  - **Icon ink:** HUD icons are night ink, never glow ink, because the glow plate takes the movement ability's colour, which can be pink.

## Performance

Measured on the largest level down (60 × 48, depth 8+) at 1280 × 720 with vsync off: about 7.5 ms a frame (11 ms before ink was kept between frames, below; 89 ms on the old 80 × 70 cap), and about 9 ms in the tall crag levels (17 ms before), the worst frames there about 12 ms. Nearly all of a frame is GDScript on the main thread (the art building its shapes, then drawing them); the GPU takes about 1 ms. A level change stalls about 0.15 to 0.25 s, behind the transition's sheet, which holds 0.35 s anyway. `tests/bench_frame.gd` splits a frame (process, redraws, physics, render) at a few places and ranks the scripts by cost; `tests/bench_load.gd` times arriving at a level, step by step; `tests/bench_gen.gd` times laying levels out; `tests/bench_big_level.gd` times each part of the print; `tests/bench_chunks.gd` times level loads.

- **One draw call per ink operation** (`InkOp.gd`): every shape an op holds goes into one triangle array. The terrain alone holds thousands, which had been thousands of draw calls on each ink plate.
- **Ink is only redrawn when it changes** (`InkCanvas._emit`, `RisoInkOp.Shape`): an op keeps what it printed while the art issues the same shapes, cover and scale again (most art redraws every frame, but much of what it draws holds still: light while the wizard stands, the still parts of a moving figure), comparing them against its own copy, as art may reuse its arrays. Ops printing the same shapes one after the other (ink, then the night it punches out) share one triangulation, and flat ink takes one colour rather than one per point. Drawing had been about half of a frame; it is now about a fifth.
- **Shapes that only scale and move** (`RisoShapes`: circles, ellipses, rounded rectangles, sparkles, almonds) are each worked out once at unit size, by point count, and placed with one transform, rather than point by point with a sine and cosine each.
- **Portals** keep their outline, rim and halo while they stand still, and their static between flickers.
- **Chunks** (`LevelLoader.sleep_far_chunks`): the level is cut into 8 × 8 cell chunks; a few times a second each chunk wakes or sleeps by its distance from the view, or from the wizard (the camera lags a rift jump or respawn) (awake within 640 px, asleep past 896 px). Everything placed with the level in a sleeping chunk stops processing, leaves the physics world and is hidden. Bolts, ghosts and rifts always run. An awakened worm also stays active for its pursuit across the level, even when its root's original burrow sleeps (`tests/worm_combat_test.gd`). Its full 12-cell activation range wakes it independently of those chunks, so a sleeping burrow cannot prevent emergence (`tests/worm_wake_test.gd`).
- **Props print as they come into view** (`RisoProp._process`), not all in the frame a level loads; still kinds then stop processing. Stars are printed once and animated by moving their canvases.
- **Loading:** the layout (`LevelGen.new`) is built on the generator thread with the cells, and kept with them for revisits and the map. Rock is laid as plain tiles (the print draws its own rock; every rock tile has the same collision), and there is no background tile layer. Laying out takes 0.05 to 0.3 s a level, and about 0.65 s for the wide sky levels (it was 1 s); the neighbours are laid out ahead of time, so a transition seldom waits for it.
- **Wisps** place their bodies from a per-frame table of their trail (positions, directions and bends every 2 px) instead of walking the trail for each vertex: about 0.15 ms a wisp on screen.

## Limits

- Presentation only: no collision, physics or generation changes. The only gameplay-file edit is one notify call at the end of `MapInfo._arrived()`.
- Menus keep their Godot controls for layout and input; RisoMenu prints them.
- Prop art is sized to the current prefab scales (cells are 128 world px). Standing props measure their own ground each redraw, because checkpoints shift themselves after spawning; spikes stand on the surface they are rotated toward. A prefab added later needs an entry in `RisoPrint.DRESS` and a drawing in `RisoProp.gd`, or it stays invisible under the print.
